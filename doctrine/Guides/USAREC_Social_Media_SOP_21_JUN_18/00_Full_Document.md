PURPOSE:
The purpose of this publication is to provide guidance and requirements to Soldiers, DA
Civilians, and contractors assigned to U.S. Army Recruiting Command regarding the use of
social media in an official capacity.
APPLICABILITY:
The standard operating procedures apply to all Soldiers, DA Civilians and contractors in U.S.
Army Recruiting Command using professional profiles and managing USAREC social media
accounts. Failure to follow procedure can result in loss of account or access to an account.

2. Introduction.
Social media is a part of our daily lives, and a powerful tool we use to share the Army’s story. It
helps us to create meaningful connections with our audiences and maintain trust and
confidence in America’s Army. The Army acknowledges the importance of social media and
encourages its commands, Soldiers, Families, and Army Civilians to use social media safely
and accurately to share their experiences and provide information. Advances in technology and
accessibility to connected mobile devices combined with society’s acceptance of social media
have changed how information is delivered and how people, organizations, and government
entities communicate.
For USAREC, social media is an indispensable tool that allows Recruiters to communicate in
the same space as their target audience. Recruiters must approach the use of social media
from the following three perspectives: inform, influence, and prospect.
a. Inform. Educating people about the Army and the opportunities available to help them
make an informed decision.
b. Influence. Using marketing packages, including branded content and messages that
appeal to the target demographic, to encourage people to choose Army service as a career
path.

c. Prospect. Prospecting is a recruiting function, operation, and critical task whereby a
Recruiter attempts to contact leads to generate interest in an Army enlistment or accession.  It is
also one of the eight recruiting functions and "an action a Recruiter takes to contact a lead."
Social media, if done correctly, has the ability to maximize recruiting efforts. However,
Recruiters and social media managers must use it in a professional, thoughtful, and deliberate
manner. Leaders must enforce the professional and measured use of social media.
3. Social Media Operational Security (OPSEC)
While social media helps organizations share information and keeps audiences connected, it
also has the potential to expose people to privacy issues. OPSEC and personal privacy
concerns are paramount when using social media, both personally and professionally. Sharing
seemingly trivial information online can be dangerous to loved ones, fellow Soldiers, and
applicants. America’s enemies and criminals scour blogs, forums, chat rooms, and personal
websites to piece together information that can harm the U.S. and its Soldiers.
a. Training Requirements. The Army requires Social media managers to complete two
OPSEC courses. Soldiers, DA Civilians, and contractors who manage or run Recruiting Station,
Company, Battalion or Brigade social media pages are considered social media managers and
must complete the required OPSEC training in addition to complying with the OPSEC training
requirements in accordance with Army Regulation 350-1.
(1) OPSEC for EOP Operators. To access go to:
https://federation.eams.army.mil/sso/authenticate/?u=https://iatraining.us.army.mil/_splashlogin/
login.fcc&m=GET&r=t
(2) DISA Social Networking Class. To access go to:
https://iatraining.disa.mil/eta/disa_sn_v21_fy17/launchPage.htm
b. Personally Identifiable Information (PII). In accordance with Army Regulation 25-22, the
Army will protect personally identifiable information in all media through appropriate security
safeguards against risk of loss, unauthorized access, destruction, etc. PII is information that can
be used to distinguish or trace an individual’s identity, either alone or when combined with other
personal or identifying information that is linked or linkable to a specific individual. Safeguarding
PII is a critical responsibility that recruiters must take seriously at all times. The loss of PII can
result in substantial harm to individuals, including identity theft or fraudulent use of information.
Examples of PII include but are not limited to the following types of information:
(1) Social Security Numbers
(2) Birthdates
(3) Birth Location
(4) Personal Telephone Numbers
(5) Personal Email addresses
(6) Home addresses
(7) Employment/Medical/Criminal Histories
(8) Education Records
(9) Spouse/Children’s Information
There are other types of PII not included in the items listed above and so it is important to use

(1) Name
(2) Rank
(3) MOS (with the exception of these MOSs:18X, 37F, Ranger option 40, 35L, 35M, 35P

(4) Unit
(4) Pictures taken during public events
(5) Pictures of applicants/Future Soldiers w/ release form
d. Release Authority.
(1) Local Level. Brigade and Battalion Advertising and Public Affairs specialists (A&PA)
maintain release authority at the local level. Recruiters and social media managers who have
questions as to whether information is releasable should submit content for review to their unit
A&PA and wait for approval before posting information on social media.
(2) HQ, USAREC public affairs office maintains release authority at the national level and
overall release authority of information within the command. A&PAs who are unsure should
submit content to USAREC public affairs for review and determination on whether information
can be released.
e. Fake accounts. A perfect example of issues that occur when using social media are the
appearance of false accounts. People are taking and using Soldiers’ photos and names and
using them to create social media profiles. With these fake profiles, these people run romantic
and financial scams on others using the network. Recruiters should use caution when adding
new friends on professional profiles. If Recruiters find fake accounts, they should document
them and follow the platform procedures for reporting for removal. Make sure to submit all
required information for the platform to ensure swift action. If there is no resolution, notify the
battalion or brigade A&PA for assistance. The A&PA will then forward USAREC cyber team at
usarmy.knox.usarec.list.hq-g6-ia-office-mgr@mail.mil and cc the USAREC public affairs office.
4. Social Media Online Conduct
The Army defines online conduct as the use of electronic communications in an official or
personal capacity in a manner that is consistent with the Army values and standards of
conduct. When logged into social media platforms, Soldiers represent the Army and must
abide by UCMJ at all times. Commenting, posting and linking to material that violates
regulations, polices, and/or the UCMJ is prohibited. Examples include, but are not limited to:
5

--- [Page 6] ---
libel, slander, harassment, bullying, hazing, stalking, discrimination, retaliation or any other
types of misconduct that undermines dignity and respect.
Recruiters and social media managers represent USAREC through official social media
accounts. Whether it is a Recruiter’s professional profile page or a Station, Company, Battalion
or Brigade page, the expectations for conduct are the same: treat everyone with dignity and
respect, and post in a professional manner as a representative of the U.S. Army. Always use
the model of Think, Type, Post when creating social media content.
a. Think. Think about what message is being communicated and who could potentially view
it. Content on social media is public and viewable by all. It has the potential to be seen by the
Sergeant Major of the Army and the Chief of Staff of the Army and various leaders through the
Army. Is the content going to reflect the Army values or bring dicredit upon the organization?
Remember, perception is reality.
b. Type. Type messages that are consistent with our U.S. Army Values and USAREC
messaging. Some information, while it may be beneficial for your recruiting mission at the time,
may not be consistent with what we are trying to convey to the general public about the Army,
which potentially damages the Army’s reputation overall.
c. Post. Post if the message demonstrates dignity and respect for self and others. All
interactions need to demonstrate respect and professionalism. Refrain from posting angry or
frustrated comments on professional Recruiter profiles and U.S. Army Recruiting pages. On
social media it doesn’t take much for something to go viral, especially if it is negative. Cyber
altercations with the public do not align with our values or project a positive light on the Army or
Recruiters. If you are not sure if something should be posted, get another opinion.
d. Appropriate Content. All postings must be Army or recruiting related to be considered
relevant and appropriate. They should clearly communicate the Army and USAREC’s messages
with content that is value-added to the organization’s overall strategy. The following is a
guideline of acceptable content to be shared and posted on official social media accounts. This
is not an inclusive list.
(1) Photos and video of Station/Company/Battalion/Brigade
events/functions/organizational days
(2) Photos highlighting Future Soldiers w/ signed waiver
(3) Photos and videos of Future Soldier Training Events
(4) Photos and videos of community partner interactions
(5) Photos and videos of Unit training
(6) General articles and information about the Army and Army programs
(7) Local Recruiter and Future Soldier media engagements
(8) Any USAREC/Army approved social media marketing/public affairs content or graphics
(9) Photos and videos highlighting community service activities
(10) Content shared from USAREC, U.S. Army, and Department of Defense, social media
platforms
(11) Leadership Tips
(12) Tips to prepare for Basic Combat Training
e. Inappropriate/Unauthorized Content. Postings must not cause embarrassment or harm
to the organization and send a message that conflicts with organizational messages. Content
that has no tie to the Army or no connection with furthering the mission is inappropriate and

unauthorized. The following is a guideline of unacceptable and unauthorized content that may
not be used on USAREC social media accounts. It is not all inclusive.
(1) Unprofessional Videos/Photos. Sharing or posting videos of horsing around, playing in
the office, and anything that demonstrates a lack of professionalism should not be posted on
official USAREC pages or professional Recruiter pages. It sends the wrong message to the
public about what kind of an organization the Army is and what it is all about. Find a
professional way to demonstrate that we can have fun in the Army just like everyone else.
(2) Military Lingo / Acronyms. Avoid using military lingo and acronyms. Civilians with no
affiliation will not be aware of lingo or acronyms used in the military, which could cause
confusion. To avoid confusion, ensure that you fully define/explain terminology.
(3) Protected Material. Posting or sharing music, logos, songs, or other information that is
protected by copyright, trademark, or any other restriction is illegal. By doing so you are
exposing yourself and the organization to legal action. This includes background copyrighted
background music for a video you create.
(4) Endorsements and Promotions. As members of the Army, we are not authorized to
endorse or promote external businesses or groups (this includes any personal endorsement or
mention you may want to post of a discount, movie, television show, product, brand, etc.) or
personal business or social media accounts.
(5) Political Posts or Endorsements. As Soldiers and civilians using social media in an
official capacity, you are representing the Army. Participation in political activities in an official
capacity is not allowed; therefore, we cannot post links to “share” or “retweet” comments or
tweets from a social media account of a political party or candidate running for partisan office.
Also, we cannot create posts that discuss the political atmosphere and share opinions about it.
(6) Religious Posts. While the Army supports the spiritual health of all Soldiers and
civilians, the Army does not affiliate itself with any particular religion. Do not post anything that
promotes or endorses any specific religion or religious organization.
(7) Profanity. Profanity is prohibited in the user terms of agreement. It is also
unprofessional in nature and, therefore, not authorized on official USAREC social media
accounts. Do not post anything with the use of profanity in the content or message.
(8) Discriminatory Content.  Any content that may be considered racists, sexist,
discriminatory in any way, sexual in nature, or could be construed as sexual harassment is
unauthorized. Posts found to have such content will be investigated based on Equal Opportunity
and the Recruiter responsible for the content could be subject to punishment under UCMJ.
(9) Slander Between Users. Slander goes against the Army values and violates the
guideline of treating everyone with dignity and respect. Do not get into arguments or
conversations that lead to name calling and personal attacks on social media. These social
media encounters reflect poorly on yourself and the organization.
f. Information Gathering. The use of social media to gather publicly available information on
subjects of recruiting efforts that is not directly relevant and necessary to the mission is
prohibited in accordance with AR 25-22. Recruiters and social media managers will not use
social media to determine how an individual exercises their first amendment right and will not

5. Messaging.
The goal of a social media post is to convey a message or idea in a digital forum to reach a
target audience. Posts are a combination of media and text that are selected to catch the
attention of social media users so that they engage with the content and come away from that
engagement with a new understanding or idea. Social media posts have the ability to help
Recruiters inform, educate, and change audience perceptions about the Army. As you create
posts, you must consider several things:
a. Audience.
(1) Determine Your Audience. There is a common misconception that when something is
posted on social media, it is posted for everyone. However, that is not the case because not
every message resonates with everyone, and each level of command reaches a different
audience. The primary audiences for each level within the Command are listed below.
(a) Stations: 17-24 year olds, their influencers, and local community partners
(b) Companies: 17-24 year olds, their influencers, and local community partners
(c) Battalions: Companies, Stations, influencers, and regional community partners,
possibly 17-24 year olds
(d) Brigades: Battalions, Companies, Stations, influencers and regional community
partners
(2) Know Your Audience. Find out what appeals to your audience. What do they want or
need to know? What are they interested in? If you are in tune with the community, then you will
be able to shape a message that not only reaches the audience but also is effective and
achieves the desired result.
b. Consistency. Messaging must remain consistent with the values and mission of the
organization. When a message is consistent, the odds are higher that more people will see it
and have a clearer picture of what you want them to know and understand. Include elements
from USAREC public affairs messages and marketing campaigns to ensure we are speaking
with one voice as an organization.
c. Timely. Messages should be timely with holidays, events and news. If you are active in the
community, and events and training are happening, then updates should be posted on social
media as quickly as possible. People want to see content happening right now, not last week.
Especially if there’s a particular information campaign that supports the command, it is important
to ensure the message is pushed as it was meant to be coordinated. Some content doesn’t
have a time limit and can be posted at any time as filler on your page.
For further information concerning messaging and creating a message see G3 Social Media
Messaging Training Packet.

7. General Account Requirements
While it is not required to have social media accounts, they can be beneficial to the mission. In
accordance with USAREC Regulation 27-4, all communication media with subject of recruiting
efforts will be official. The use of personal cell phones or personal unofficial social media is
strictly prohibited. Examples include, using personal social media accounts that are not used
exclusively for recruiting operations. Recruiters must separate personal social media accounts
from public/professional social media. There are no exceptions to these prohibitions.
There are several requirements necessary for establishing an official U.S. Army social media
account. Failure to follow these requirements can result in loss of account. Each platform may
be different, but the following requirements will be used when applicable.
a. Point of contact. Accounts must list a valid .mil address. Pages belong to an organization
(rather than an individual) will use an organizational email address, not an individual Recruiter’s
email address. Email addresses to be used are as follows:
(1) Station Pages. Station’s USAREC assigned email address.
(2) Company Pages. Company’s USAREC assigned email address.
(3) Battalion Pages. Battalion’s USAREC assigned email address.
(4) Brigade Pages. Brigade’s USAREC assigned email address.

page in accordance with DOD and U.S. Army policy. See Appendixes.
f. Open to the public. All official accounts must be open to the public.
g. Classified as a Government Organization. In order for an account to operate in an
official capacity, the account must be registered and labeled as a “government organization”
account. The use of a Facebook profile, community or group page for official purposes may
violate the social media platform’s terms of service agreement.

register their social media accounts with U.S. Army Recruiting Command at
usarec.army.mil/social_media

8. Federal Records Management and Social Media.
Social media allows individuals to collaborate, create, organize, edit, comment on, combine, and
share content. When this is done in an official capacity (i.e. on a professional profile/page) it
creates Federal records. The Federal Records Act (44 U.S.C. 3301) defines Federal records as
any material that is recorded, made or received in the course of Federal business, regardless of
its form or characteristics, and is worthy of preservation. Social media content created in an
official capacity meets this definition and must be managed according to applicable laws and
regulations.
Recruiters and social media managers using official profiles and USAREC social media
platforms are required to keep records of all social media interactions relating to USAREC’s
policies, business, or mission. AR 25-22 requires the storage of official communications with

subject of recruiting efforts, therefore we must maintain records for the minimum time prescribed
in accordance with AR 25-22 and AR 25-400-2. To alleviate some of the burden of this task,
USAREC social media managers and Recruiters using professional profiles/pages will keep all
posted content and direct messages on page itself and refrain from deleting content or direct
messages. Exceptions are as follows:
a. Terms of Service Violations. Posts and comments that violate page terms of service
agreements may be deleted. However, the posts and any engagement or interactions with such
posts prior to removal should be saved for record.
b. Spam. Frequently outside agencies will post information that is not applicable to the
conversation or tries to direct you to another site. This spam may be deleted at any time as it is
not record related material.
c. Direct Messages. Recruiters will not delete direct messages from leads or applicants from
the social media platform of origin unless they first record the entire message thread and save it
on file in another location.
Company commanders and station commanders are responsible for enforcing these
requirements and ensuring recruiters and social media managers are maintaining compliance.
DA Recruiters that complete their assignment with USAREC must maintain their profiles for the
minimum time prescribed by AR 25-22 and may delete their profile after that time.
9. Social Media Ads/Content Paid Promotion.
Social media managers who want to promote content on USAREC social media platforms using
paid social media marketing (e.g. Facebook boosts) are allowed to do so. The unit must pay
using unit Direct Funds via a Government Purchase Card (currently there is no mechanism to
establish a standing contract with Facebook). Social media managers should contact their
Battalion or Brigade A&PA to coordinate the purchase.
10. Social Media Platforms.
Social media platforms allow content and information to be shared and consumed across
networks and groups. They include social networking, video sharing, live-streaming,
microblogging, blogging, social news, photo sharing, content curation and wikis. There are an
array of social media platforms that fall under these categories, and more platforms are created
every day. Some platforms are quite helpful and beneficial to recruiting efforts, while others are
inappropriate and unacceptable for recruiting purposes.
a. Prohibited Platforms for Recruiting. Social media platforms with the following purposes
are prohibited for use in an official capacity:
(1) Any and all dating platforms (e.g. Tinder, Bumble, etc.)
(2) Platforms promoting/endorsing political agendas/candidates
(3) Platforms promoting/endorsing a specific religion
b. Approved Platforms for Recruiting. Recruiters and social media managers may use
popular or new social media platforms to stay in sync with the market as long as they do not fall
in the prohibited categories. The most popular and widespread social media platforms used in
the command are Facebook, Twitter, Instagram and YouTube, but this is not an exhaustive list.

11. Social Media Reporting Requirements.
If you are locked out of an account or a platform shuts down your account, you must report the
event within 24 hours to unit A&PA. The A&PA will report to HQ, USAREC Public Affairs.
12. Cease and Desist.
If recruiters or social media managers receive notification from the social media platform to
modify or terminate the continued use of the platform, immediately stop all communication on
the platform and report the issue to HQ, USAREC through the unit Staff Judge Advocate
assigned to the chain of command.
