## Appendix A - Household Segmentation

Household
Segment
Short Description
1
The nation's most exclusive addresses with the wealthiest lifestyle in America.
The highest concentration of residents earning over $200,000 a year or
possessing a postgraduate degree. Opulent standard of living. Household traits:
Spend $3,000+ foreign travel; Shop at Bloomingdale's;  Atlantic Monthly
magazine ; Golf Channel; Jaguar XK
2
The nation's second-wealthiest lifestyle is characterized by married couples
with children, college degrees, a significant percentage of Asian Americans and
six-figure incomes earned by business executives, managers and professionals.
Household traits: Shop at Talbot's; Take a skiing vacation; Architectural Digest
magazine; Scientific American magazine; Audi A8
3
America's up-and-coming business class: a wealthy suburban world of dual-
income couples who are highly educated, typically between the ages of 35 and
54, often without children. Ranks number-one for owning a small business and
having a home office. Household traits: Go scuba diving/snorkeling; Eat at
Bertucci's; Inc. magazine; Home study course by internet; Porsche 911
4
Tech-savvy singles and couples living in fashionable neighborhoods on the
urban fringe. Highly educated and ethnically mixed; typically filled with fitness
clubs, clothing boutiques, casual restaurants and all types of bars--from juice to
coffee to microbrew. Household traits: Shop at Banana Republic; Order from
J.Crew; Elle Decor magazine; Independent Film Channel; Range Rover SUV.
5
Affluent Baby Boomers who've fled the city for the charms of small-town living
in communities noted for their recently built homes on sprawling properties.
These families live in six-figure comfort and enjoy country club sports like golf,
tennis and swimming. Household traits: Order from online retailers; Go skiing;
Family Fun magazine; Pay-per-view movies; Lexus SUV
6
Large families in new-money subdivisions surrounded by recreational parks,
golf courses and upscale malls. With a median income of $100,000, residents
are big spenders who like to travel, go out to eat, shop at clothing boutiques and
take in a show. Household traits: Go downhill skiing; Shop at Ann Taylor;
Working Mother magazine; Nickelodeon TV; Infiniti SUV
7
Seem to have it all: high incomes, advanced degrees and sophisticated tastes
to match their credentials. Many are city dwellers who live in fashionable homes
on small, manicured lots. Household traits: Shop at Nordstrom; Eat at California
Pizza Kitchen; Read Sunday newspaper; Wall Street Week TV; Mercedes Benz
E class
8
White-collar professionals drawn to comfortable homes and apartments within
a manageable commute to downtown jobs, restaurants and entertainment. Filled
with significant numbers of Asian Americans and college graduates. Household
traits: Eat at Houlihan's; Shop at Express; Shape magazine; The Amazing Race;
Acura TSX
9
Older, upper-class, college-educated professionals who are often among the
leading citizens of their small-town communities. They enjoy the trappings of
success and spend freely, especially on computer technology. Household traits:
Belong to a country club; Own a motor home; Atlantic Monthly magazine; 1960s
nostalgia music; Lexus LS430

Segment
Short Description
10
Tend to be executives who decorate their $200,000 homes with multiple
computers, large-screen TV sets and an impressive collection of wines. More
than half hold college degrees; enjoy cultural activities (books, theatre, dance)
Household traits: Order from Readers' Digest; Travel domestically by rail; Inc.
magazine; Washington Week TV; Toyota Avalon
11
Upper-income couples in spacious homes. Typically college-educated Baby
Boomers, these Americans try to maintain a balanced lifestyle between high-
power jobs and laid-back leisure. Household traits: Travel for business; Take a
golf vacation; Skiing magazine; Outdoor Life Network; Toyota Land Cruiser SUV
12
Well-off, middle-aged couples who are the chic sophisticates living in the
nation's satellite cities. Have college educations, well-paying business and
professional careers and swank homes filled with the latest technology.
Household traits: Go to college sports events; Eat at Bennigan's; Macworld
magazine; Independent Film Channel; Volkswagen Passat
13
Home of those legendary Soccer Moms and Dads. Upscale families with dual
incomes, college degrees and new homes. Tend to be kid-obsessed, with heavy
purchases of computers, action figures, dolls, board games, bicycles and
camping equipment. Household traits: Take a skiing vacation; Go to the zoo;
Family Fun magazine; Nickelodeon TV; Toyota Sequoia SUV
14
Upper-middle income older Americans who pursue active--and activist--
lifestyles. Their grown-up children are recently out of the house. This is the top-
ranked segment for all-inclusive travel packages; the favorite destination is Italy.
Household traits: Take cruises, past 3 yrs; Contribute to PBS; Tennis magazine;
Washington Week TV; Cadillac Seville
15
Mature, empty-nesting couples in stable neighborhoods graced with backyard
pools and patios. White-collar managers and professionals now at the top of their
careers. Household traits: Shop at high-end department stores; Buy 1950s
nostalgia music; Sunset magazine; American Experience TV (AE); Mercury
Mariner SUV
16
Collection of young, mobile urbanites with the nation's most liberal lifestyles. A
progressive mix of young singles and couples, students and professionals,
Hispanics, Asians, African-Americans and whites. Quick to check out the latest
movie, nightclub, laptop and microbrew. Household traits: Eat at Bertucci's; Buy
Spanish/Latin music; New York magazine; Style channel; Mini Cooper
17
College-educated, upper-middle-class home-owners. Like many of their peers
who married late, they are still raising children in comfortable suburban
subdivisions, and they're pursuing kid-centered lifestyles. Household traits: Order
from Gevalia Kaffe; Buy collectables online; Seventeen magazine; The Disney
Channel TV; Isuzu Ascender
18
College-educated, white-collar professionals with administrative jobs, upper-
middle-class incomes and large families in recently built subdivisions. High rate
of Hispanic and Asian Americans. Household traits: Shop at The Disney Store;
Go to Chuck E. Cheese; Parenting magazines; Nickelodeon TV; Nissan Armada
SUV
19
Small, upper-middle-class families living in the nation's suburbs. Most have
gone to college and hold professional and white-collar jobs. With upscale
incomes and small families, they have comfortable lifestyles, filling their homes
with toys, TV sets and pets. Household traits: Go mountain biking; Eat at
Quizno's; Inc. magazine; Smallville TV; Saturn Vue SUV

Household
Segment
Short Description
20
Upscale incomes, numerous children and spacious homes. They have the
disposable income and educated sensibility to want the best for their children.
They buy all the latest technology, but also take advantage of their rustic locales
by camping, boating and fishing. Household traits: Own a power boat; Go cross-
country skiing; Family Fun magazine; Fuse Network; Dodge Ram
21
Middle-class, home-owning suburbanites who are aging in place rather than
moving to retirement communities. Mostly retired and live in quiet comfort.
Household traits: Shop at Lord & Taylor; Belong to a veterans' club; US Senior
Open (Golf) TV; Masterpiece Theatre; Buick LaCrosse
22
Young, middle-class singles and couples who are preoccupied with balancing
work and leisure pursuits. Having recently left college dorms, they now live in
apartment complexes surrounded by ball fields, health clubs and casual-dining
restaurants. Household traits: Play racquetball; Buy rap music; Vibe magazine;
King of the Hill in syndication; Mazda 3
23
Upscale exurban couples known for their active lifestyles. Most are married,
college-educated and own new homes; about a third have children. Pursue
outdoor activities. Household traits: Go snowboarding; Go horseback riding;
North American Hunter magazine; American Experience TV; Subaru Impreza
24
Young, midscale singles in a transitional lifestyle before they marry, have
families and establish more deskbound lifestyles. Includes many recent college
graduates who are into athletic activities, the latest technology and nightlife
entertainment. Household traits: Travel to South Pacific, past 3 yrs; Go in-line
skating; Maxim magazine; Blind Date TV; Mitsubishi Eclipse Spyder
25
Middle-aged, upscale, laid-back households that have started to empty-nest.
Have well-paying blue- or white collar jobs, or own small businesses. Have the
disposable income to enjoy traveling, owning timeshares and going out to eat.
Household traits: Go whitewater rafting; Belong to a civic club; Skiing magazine;
George Michael Sports Machine TV; GMC Sierra
26
Educated, midscale and multi-ethnic urban empty-nesters and college
graduates. A vibrant social scene surrounds their older homes and apartments,
and residents love the nightlife and enjoy leisure-intensive lifestyles. Household
traits: Shop at Macy's; Travel to Central/South America; Harper's Bazaar
magazine; ABC World News Now TV; Infiniti I35
27
Tend to be middle-class with solid managerial jobs and comfortable
retirements, settling in satellite communities for the lower cost of living and more
relaxed pace. Enjoy reading, playing musical instruments, indoor gardening and
refinishing furniture. Household traits: Play bingo; Do needlepoint; Saturday
Evening Post; Hollywood Squares TV; Mercury Sable
28
Upper-middle class, small-town couples beginning to enjoy their first empty-
nest years.  On their coffee tables are magazines with titles ranging from Country
Living and Country Home to Gourmet and Forbes. Big travelers. Household
traits: Domestic travel by motor home; Eat at Bob Evans; Golf tournaments on
TV; Triple Crown on TV; Buick LaCrosse
29
A living example of how ethnically diverse the nation has become. In these
multilingual, mostly urban neighborhoods--one in ten speaks a language other
than English--middle-aged immigrants and their children live in middle-class
comfort. Household traits: Go ice skating; Go sailing; Read Sunday newspaper;
Ebony magazine; Lexus IS300

Short Description
30
Midscale, middle-aged with decent jobs living in the heart of suburbia and
pursuing conservative versions of the American Dream. Among their favorite
activities are jogging on treadmills, playing trivia games and renting videos.
Household traits: Buy from Home Shopping Network; Eat at Fuddrucker's;
Entrepreneure magazine; Futurama TV; Ford Escort
31
Concentrated in the nation's port cities, and often the first stop for up-and-
coming immigrants from Asia, South America and Europe. Typically college-
educated and ethnically diverse: about a third are foreign-born, and even more
speak a language other than English. Household traits: Read American Photo;
Read The Source; Spin magazine; Jerry Springer TV; Volkswagen GTI
32
Young, upper-middle class families in small rustic townships. Dual-income
families with comfortable, child-centered lifestyles. Driveways filled with campers
and powerboats, and family rooms with PlayCenters and Game Boys. Household
traits: Go to Chuck E. Cheese; Buy videos online; 4-Wheel and Off-Road
magazines; Nickelodeon TV; Kia Sedona
33
Young rural families who have turned high school educations and blue-collar
jobs into busy, middle-class lifestyles. To entertain their sprawling families, they
buy virtually every piece of sporting equipment on the market. Household traits:
Own a camper; Go to auto races; North American Hunter magazine; Outdoor Life
Network; Dodge Ram
34
The stereotypical American household of a generation ago: young, middle-
class, married with children. But the current version is characterized by modest
homes and ethnic diversity--including a disproportionate number of Hispanics
and African-Americans. Household traits: Own a treadmill; Eat at Carl's Jr.; Baby
magazines; Toon Disney channel; Mitsubishi Montero Sport
35
Working-class residents who pursue active lifestyles amid sprawling
apartment complexes, bars, convenience stores and Laundromats.  Found in
fast-growing satellite cities with affordable housing, abundant entry-level jobs and
a thriving singles scene. Household traits: Go snowboarding; Watch Fuse
Network; The Source magazine; Futurama TV; Nissan Sentra
36
Young, sprawling families with well-paying blue-collar jobs living comfortable
lifestyles. Ethnically diverse neighborhoods feature compact, modestly priced
homes surrounded by commercial centers that cater to child-filled households.
Household traits: Shop at Lerner; Read baby magazines; Parenting magazines;
Children's TV channels; Isuzu Ascender
37
Small town, middle-class couples and families living the "old-fashioned way of
life".  Like to fish and hunt during the day, and stay home and watch TV at night.
Household traits: Eat at Lone Star Steakhouse; Go hunting with a gun;
Bassmaster magazine; Country Music TV; Chevy Silverado
38
Neighborhoods of lower-middle-class retired singles and couples living in
modestly priced homes. Many are high school-educated seniors who held blue-
collar jobs before their retirement. A disproportionate number served in the
military. Household traits: Belong to a veterans' club; Watch U.S. Senior Open
(golf) on TV; Jeopardy! TV; Wheel of Fortune TV; Ford Crown Victoria
39
Older, middle-class with high-school educations and fixed incomes. Maintain
an easy-going lifestyle and like to socialize by going bowling, seeing a play,
meeting at the local fraternal order or going out to eat. Household traits: Order
from Readers' Digest; ABC World News Now TV; Hollywood Squares TV;
Hallmark channel; Chevy Cobalt

Segment
Short Description
40
Predominantly older, African-Americans in the urban neighborhoods of mid-
sized metros. High school educated and empty nesting, these 55-year-old-plus
residents typically live in older city neighborhoods, enjoying secure and
comfortable retirements. Household traits: Shop at Macy's; Eat at Denny's; Read
Sunday newspaper; People's Court TV; Suzuki Grand Vitara SUV
41
Lower-middle-class who have retired or are getting close to it. Have modest
educations and incomes. Maintain a low-key lifestyle filled with newspapers and
television by day and family-style restaurants at night. Household traits: Belong
to a veterans' club; Eat at casual/buffet restaurant; CBS Sunday Night Movie TV;
Price is Right TV; Mercury Grand Marquis
42
Middle-aged, high school educated, lower-middle class families that tend to
have solid, blue-collar jobs in manufacturing, milling and construction. Shop at
chains like Wal-Mart, Radio Shack and Payless Shoes. Household traits: Eat at
Houlihan's; Buy from Home Shopping Network; Car Craft Magazine; Speed
channel; Kia Optima
43
Small families and empty-nesting couples with working-class jobs living in
small middle-class towns. Pursue a rustic lifestyle where hunting and fishing
remain prime leisure activities along with cooking, sewing, camping and boating.
Household traits: Own a motor home; Eat at Golden Corral; Outdoor Life
Network; CBS Early Show TV; Mazda Pickup
44
Young, single adults in transition. Many residents are twenty something’s just
starting out on their career paths--or starting over after recent divorces or
