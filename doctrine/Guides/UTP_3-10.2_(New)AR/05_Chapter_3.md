## Systems


## INTRODUCTION

3-1. USAR recruiters will benefit from knowing which systems and reports provide focused data to support
their mission, especially concerning TPU information and USAR vacancies listings. Strong familiarity with
USAR vacancies associated with TPUs by MOS is a great tool for recruiters to match recruits with jobs
available in the geographical area—a big selling point. Refer to UTC 5–02 for a thorough review of
intelligence systems and reports, including specific applications to USAR recruiting. The systems profiled in
this chapter are specific to finding USAR vacancies.
Note: Products produced by the former USAREC G–2 are transitioning to the USARD Mission
Analysis, Fusion, and Intelligence Directorate in 2025 and 2026.

## AUTOMATED VACANCY ENTRY

3-2. The USAR’s Automated Vacancy Entry (AVE) system automates the process of identifying vacant
USAR positions and sends validated and well-formed vacancy load files to REQUEST. It is generally the
most up-to-date vacancies resource, and it feeds the weekly vacancies list produced by the USARC G–1
REQUEST Team. Refer to chapter 4 for extensive information on AVE and how to access it.
3-3. BEST PRACTICE: “My honest and humble opinion: AVE is key to success. It’s the most updated
resource to find specifics for Reserve units as well as direct contact information.”

## G2 INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY

3-13. The purpose of the G2 Intelligence Summary (INTSUM) is to provide leaders with situational
understanding of the enlisted recruiting market through analysis of environmental effects and key
performance indicators at the command, brigade, and battalion echelons. By reviewing this data, USAR
recruiters can zero in on appropriate locations and markets for lead generation and prospecting.
3-14. The INTSUM is published twice per fiscal year (FY), at the beginning of first quarter and third quarter,
and is published via the G2 SharePoint at https://armyeitaas.sharepoint-mil.us/sites/TR-USAREC-
HQ/SitePages/Directorates/G2/HomePage.aspx, under Market Intelligence Summaries (see figure 3–9).
Figure 3–9. Market Intelligence Summaries Link to the INTSUMs
3-15. INTSUM command and brigade reports include the USAR Market Geography section (see figure 3–
10). This section includes a table showing the number of NPS vacancies, the USAR mission, and USAR
production for the FY by brigade, along with the command total. The section also includes a map displaying
the geographic distribution of NPS vacancies and comments summarizing key insights regarding the share
of vacancies, mission, and production among the brigades. Since the USAR market and mission are

## HUMAN RESOURCES COMMAND VACANCY SEARCH TOOL

3-22. Download the PDF posted at https://armyrotc.army.mil/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How_to_Search
_for_TPU_1LT_Vacancy.pdf and follow the steps to access this report. The vacancy records search in this
resource is viable and easy to use even though the tool is hosted on older version of the HRC website. The
simple search returns a United States map with a notation of the number of vacancies per state, or a list of all
vacancies in the USAR can be generated at once.

## RESERVE UNIT ZONE

3-23. Reserve Unit Zone connects talent to the USAR as a one-stop interface for managing USAR vacancies,
aligning qualified applicants, and driving mission success. It gives recruiters a venue to record detailed
information about assigned USAR units within the Recruiting Station Identification Designator (RSID)
boundaries.
3-24. Reserve Unit Zone data update frequency varies, but the data returned is valuable to gain significant
knowledge of USAR vacancy MOS availability in the AO (see figure 3–14). Be aware that the data for units
is updated by the local recruiters, so it could be hit or miss with up-to-date names, numbers, and emails.

## SUMMARY

3-34. Recruiters tasked with an AR recruiting mission know the importance of understanding what kind and
composition of AR units that reside in their AO, who are the points of contact, and what those units are doing.
Additionally, understanding what AR vacancies exist within those units greatly assists in both planning and
executing the AR mission. Understanding how to access reports using recruiting systems and what those
reports provide is vital to mission success.
