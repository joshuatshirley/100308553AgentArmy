Chapter 6

## Future Soldier Management


## INTRODUCTION

6-1. A recruiter’s responsibility doesn’t end when the applicant takes the oath and accesses into the USAR.
That Future Soldier still must ship to training. Preparing Future Soldiers for IADT is a joint effort between
recruiters and TPUs. Both must take equal ownership of training and motivating Future Soldiers.

## IN-PROCESSING

6-2. Recruiters should escort newly accessed USAR Soldiers to their new TPU within three to ten days of
accession. This amount of time allows their information to upload to the Defense Manpower Data Center and
the TPU’s systems. During this time, the Future Soldiers are encouraged to learn about the basics of starting
their service time.
6-3. Recruiting unit requirements:
o
Introduce the Future Soldier to TPU personnel who are responsible for assisting them in making the
transition from civilian to military life.
o
Notify TPUs of all accessions promptly.
o
Encourage the TPU to submit DA Form 1379 or DA Form 1380 so the Future Soldier is paid for
time served.
o
The recruiter will telephonically contact the TPU of assignment within 3 working days after
accession to coordinate a date and time for the Future Soldier’s sponsorship appointment.
o
The recruiter will escort the Future Soldier to the TPU no later than 10 working days after accession
or transfer (date reservation pulled) for their sponsorship by the TPU personnel section.
o
USAR Form 62–2–R will serve as the primary source document for annotating a TPU handoff.
o
The recruiter will initiate a USAREC Form 601–210.23 to prepare a Future Soldier for IADT.
o
The recruiting battalion will notify the TPU and initiate discharge orders should a Future Soldier
fail to report to IADT.
6-4. TPU requirements:
o
Assume all administrative and personnel actions effective as of the date of accession.
o
Establish in-processing dates and times for the purpose of predictability.
o
Establish a list of required documents the recruiter must provide during Future Soldier in-
processing.
o
Counsel the Future Soldier on responsibilities while awaiting IADT.
o
Assist in motivating, training, and preparing the Future Soldier for IADT. If possible, based on time
and personnel, the TPU should develop a training schedule specifically for pre-IADT Future
Soldiers to teach pre–basic training tasks.
o
Notify the recruiter immediately if a condition exists that will prevent the Future Soldier from
shipping to IADT and provide supporting documentation.
6-5. Common access card: The Future Soldier should get a CAC prior to in-processing.
6-6. Pay:
o
Bringing a Standard Form 1199A will allow the TPU to input the new Soldier into the pay system.
USAR Soldiers’ pay comes from a different funding stream than active-duty Soldiers’ pay, so the
Soldier still needs that form for IADT.
o
There are two different options for the newly accessed Soldier: Training Pay Category (TPC) P
(with pay) and TPC P (without pay). TPC P (with pay) is for those who will attend battle assembly
prior to attending IADT. TPC P (without pay) is for those who will not attend battle assembly prior
to IADT. With either option, the Soldier will incur monthly debt for selected options such as
Servicemembers’ Group Life Insurance or the initial buy-in for the Montgomery GI Bill Selected
Reserve. Refer to AR 601–210 for more information.

Future Soldier Management
o
If the TPU authorizes it, retain newly accessed Soldiers at the recruiting station until they ship.
Recruiters can submit DA 1380s for RST and pay while teaching the Soldiers critical skills they will
need at basic training.

## SUMMARY

6-7. Future Soldiers need to be transitioned into their new careers with professionalism and integrity to
complete recruiting success. This is the only way USAREC will meet its mission to provide the Army’s end
strength.
