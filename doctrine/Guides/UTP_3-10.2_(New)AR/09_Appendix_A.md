## Processing Into the United States Army Reserve

After successfully prospecting and engendering a commitment for enlistment from a PS
applicant or agreement to transfer from a member of the ARNG, several specific processes
must occur.

## PRIOR SERVICE PROCESSING

A-1. Process a PS applicant into the USAR by determining eligibility, gathering required documents,
passing packet validation, and transmitting source documents to the Military Entrance Processing Station
(MEPS).
A-2. Step 1: Determine eligibility in accordance with (IAW) AR 601–210
o
Determine eligibility using the APPLEMDT process:

A = age

P = physical

P = prior service

L = law violations

E = education

M = marital status

D = dependents

T = testing
o
Verify PS IAW AR 601–210.
o
Review the applicant’s DD214:

Block 6, Reserve Obligation Term Date (This determines if the applicant is still in the IRR with
a military service obligation. If the applicant is still in the Army IRR, USAREC is not
authorized to process them. They must be referred to a career counselor (79V) per UR 601–
210.)

Block 9, Command to which transferred

Block 12, Record of Service

Block 18, Remarks

Block 26, Separation Code

Block 27, Reentry Code

Block 28, Narrative Reason for Separation
o
For service members from all service branches, including United States Coast Guard, access the
Reenlistment Eligibility Data Display (REDD) report at the Military Recruiter Information Suite
(MRIS) site at https://mris.dmdc.osd.mil/mris/public/ if required IAW UR 601–210. The REDD
report provides branch and dates of service, type of discharge, and reason for separation.

o

o
E-5 and higher who access within 48 months from separation will maintain pay grade.
o
E-5 through E-9 who access after 48 months from separation with no MSO will access one grade
lower.

Former commissioned officers and warrant officers can access with a grade determination waiver.

Glossary
SECTION II – TERMS
Access
A Recruiter’s ability, right, or permission to approach, enter, speak with, use, or have admittance to
target market population, key influencers, schools, and other locations.
Accession
Regular Army: A person who has entered onto active duty. Army Reserve: A person who has enlisted
into an Army Reserve program or transferred from the Individual Ready Reserve to a troop program
unit.
APPLEMDT
An acrostic used to aid the recruiter in remembering all the areas that address a prospect’s
qualifications. The acrostic stands for: A=age, P=physical, P=prior service, L=law violations,
E=education, M=marital status, D=dependents and T=testing.
Applicant
A prospect who agrees to process for enlistment or commissioning. Also: Any person who has
commenced processing for enlistment or appointment in any of the Military Services by initiating a
DD Form 1966, “Record of Military Processing - Armed Forces of the United States,” or comparable
form. (DoDI 1304.33)
Army Interview
A formal meeting between a recruiter and a prospect for the purpose of telling the Army story and
counseling them on the benefits of an Army enlistment or commission.
Bonus
An enlistment incentive linked to specific military occupational specialties, programs, or units; offered
to applicants who meet specific criteria. The amount of the bonus varies with the needs of the Army
and the term of enlistment. Enlistment bonuses attract qualified applicants into critical skills, programs,
or units.
Career counselor
An RA career counselor (formerly retention NCO) and RC career counselor (formerly transition
NCO). The term “career counselor” describes the commander’s principal asset responsible for advising
Soldiers on their careers and the various options offered by an RA or RC Army career. RA career
counselors routinely counsel Soldiers on procedures and eligibility for RA reenlistment. However, the
RA career counselors are also responsible for counseling on opportunities available with the USAR
and ARNG, and they work in tandem with the RC career counselor in processing Soldiers who
separate from the RA and elect enlistment or affiliation with the RCs. RC career counselors
consummate RC contracts and perform necessary coordination with RC commands as necessary to
facilitate a Soldier’s affiliation with the RCs, in addition to the counseling associated with their normal
duties. DA Pam 611–21 describes various duty positions within the career counseling MOS.
Centers of influence
Individuals or groups who can help develop a better image of the Army, influence individuals to enlist
in an Army program, or refer names of leads to Army recruiters. They can be civic or business leaders,
educators, members of professional groups, groups of high school or college students identified as
class leaders or influencers, news media representatives, nurses, convention officials, or other
influential individuals or groups.
Community partner
A person or persons capable of directly or indirectly influencing people to seek more information
about Army enlistment or commissioning opportunities and boost the Army’s market access and
credibility through referrals, testimonials, simple undervalued word-of-mouth, and third-party
endorsements.

Glossary
Delayed Entry Program
A Regular Army enlistment program which allows an applicant to delay their entry onto AD for a
period of time. This program is subject to controls, as determined by the Commanding General,
USAREC.
Delayed Training Program
An Army Reserve enlistment program which allows a recruit to enlist and delay their entry into initial
active duty for training (IADT) for up to 365 days. Persons who enlist in this program may or may not
participate with their unit of assignment while awaiting IADT.
Enlistment
The enlistment occurs after the oath has been taken. Voluntary contract (DD Form 4) for military
service establishing military status as an enlisted member of the Active Army, USAR, or ARNG. This
includes enlistment of both NPS and PS personnel. The latter group includes prior Army and RC
personnel and any persons with PS in any other Armed Force of the United States. The enlistment
agreement is legally binding after the oath has been taken, notwithstanding the applicant’s failure to
sign.
Enlistment Option
The opportunity afforded a prospective enlistee to select the type of duty or occupational field of
training and/or the unit or geographical area to be assigned upon enlistment.
Future Soldier
A person who has enlisted in the DEP, DTP, or delayed status, but who has not shipped to the training
base.
Lead
A name or names with an address, telephone number, e-mail address, or social media site, and for
whom does not or has not had an appointment for an Army interview. Also: Any person who has
expressed, to recruiting personnel, an interest in enlisting or receiving an appointment in a Military
Service and who appears to possess, or who may in the future possess, the potential and qualifications
for enlistment or appointment in said Military Service. (DoDI 1304.33)
Lead generation
Activities to obtain the names, addresses, and telephone numbers or email address of individuals who
may have an interest in the Army.
Market
The population in a geographic region who physically and mentally qualify for service.
Market analysis
A quantitative and qualitative assessment of the market that is the basis for establishing geographic
boundaries for recruiting centers and the size and positioning of the required recruiting force.
Operational environment
A composite of the conditions, circumstances, and influences that affect how recruiters understand and
evaluate the local recruiting environment.
Processing
A recruiting function that begins when a prospect agrees to process for enlistment or accession.
Processing includes the preparation of forms, mental testing (enlistment processing only), a medical
exam, background investigations, and administering the oath of enlistment.
Prospect
A person who has agreed to meet with an Army recruiter, or a person who has been interviewed but
who has not committed to process into the Army.
Prospecting
A recruiting function, operation, and critical task whereby a recruiter attempts to contact leads for the
purpose of generating interest in an Army enlistment or accession. Also: one of the eight recruiting
functions. Also: “an action a recruiter takes to contact a lead.”

References
Referral
A lead furnished to a recruiter by any individual who may ultimately be processed for an Army or
Army Reserve contract or accession.
Segment
A section of the recruiting market as defined by demographics, consumer behavior, geographic data,
and other factors. Used in market analysis, marketing, and targeting.
Ship
To transport an enlistee to a reception battalion. Often used by recruiters with reference to transporting
prospects and applicants to Military Entrance Processing Stations for testing.
Vacancy
In the Army Reserve, a vacancy is an unfilled position within a TPU’s manpower requirements.
Recruiters must match qualified applicants with vacancies for the applicant to access into the USAR.
