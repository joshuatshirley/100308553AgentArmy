Chapter 5

## Army Reserve Enlistment Programs, Incentives, and


## Benefits


## INTRODUCTION

5-1. Understanding and then applying knowledge of USAR enlistment programs, incentives, and benefits
during the conduct of recruiting operations or the Army Interview is critical to achieving the USAR mission.
Whether it is Regular Army or USAR enlistment, having a firm grasp and understanding of Army programs
and product knowledge will lead to success during all phases of the process.

## ADVANTAGES TO THE RECRUITER

5-2. Increased recruiting. Detailed knowledge allows recruiters to provide accurate and comprehensive
information to prospects to attract applicants who may choose USAR service based on specifics. This ensures
that prospects understand what they are signing up for, what they will receive, and what expectations will be
placed upon them.
5-3. Increased retention. When current Soldiers are aware of all that is available to them, they are more
likely to feel valued and remain in the service. This is crucial for maintaining a strong, experienced, and
motivated force and greatly assists PS recruiting for the USAR.
5-4. Informed decision-making. Those considering joining the Army make informed decisions about their
careers based on the benefits and incentives available to them. This can help a USAR prospect become an
applicant who confidently signs a contract.
5-5. Increased trust and credibility. Recruiters with a comprehensive understanding of USAR programs
build trust with potential applicants, their families, and the public. Demonstrating deep knowledge and
answering questions with confidence and accuracy fosters credibility.
5-6. Optional Career Trajectory. Understanding the intricacies of the USAR assists recruiters in courting
into part-time service a prospect or applicant who is unable to commit to full-time opportunities.
5-7. Improved personal and professional development. The Army offers a multitude of training,
educational benefits, and professional development opportunities. USAR Soldiers and potential recruits who
are well-informed take full advantage of these to further personal and professional growth.
5-8. Expanded promotion of the Army’s image. Understanding the full scope of programs, incentives, and
benefits helps promote a positive and accurate image of the Army. Highlighting this promotion counters
misconceptions and negative stereotypes.
5-9. Improved customization of career path. Every Soldier or potential recruit has unique aspirations and
circumstances, and knowledge of these pathways allows prospects to tailor the Army experience in a way
that aligns with their personal and professional goals, which includes choosing the USAR component.
5-10. Learning how to access and leverage as much information as possible will increase a recruiter’s
efficiency and success rate. See UTC 5–3.02 for details and examples of using programs, incentives, and
benefits in the Army Interview.

## DATA CURRENCY AND APPLICABILITY

5-11. Because of the variable nature and availability of Army programs, incentives, and benefits, knowing
where to find the most up-to-date, accurate information is critical. Recruiters must verify and validate
enlistment eligibility, availability, and qualifications for specific enlistment options during Army enlistment
processing.

## PROGRAMS

5-18. Army enlistment programs are pathways to service guaranteed in a contract. These programs and
options are designed to merge valid Army requirements with personal desires. To best serve these two
purposes, recruiters must ensure that applicants are informed of the precise nature of their commitment and
the extent to which specific options will satisfy the applicant’s personal desires. The following programs,
some of which offer scholarships, are available to USAR applicants:
5-19. Standard Training Program. This program is for NPS applicants who will complete Initial Active
Duty for Training (IADT), which includes both Basic Combat Training (BCT) and AIT back-to-back. Most
USAR enlistments are in this program.
o
For USAR Soldiers, the eight-year MSO normally will split into two parts: part one is time as a
current drilling member of a TPU, and part two is time in the IRR.
o
USAR recruits can enlist on three-, four-, five-, or six-year contracts, offering a huge amount of
flexibility. Be sure to emphasize that incentives are not authorized for contracts shorter than the six-
year option.
o
Refer to https://www.usar.army.mil/IRR/ for more information about the IRR.
5-20. Alternate Training Program. In this Reserve split training program, IET is divided between two time
periods, usually two summers, one year apart. This enables students to continue high school, college, or
vocational studies. BCT is conducted in the first summer, usually between the school’s spring and fall
semesters. Then AIT is conducted the following summer. Only select MOSs qualify for this program. Refer
to USARD Messages for guidance.
o
Only MOSs with AIT of fewer than 13 weeks are eligible. USAREC posts an official list at the
beginning of each FY in a USARD Message and on the G–3 Enlisted Eligibility Processing Division
(EEPD) Recruiting Operations Center (ROC) SharePoint page at https://armyeitaas.sharepoint-
mil.us/sites/TR-USAREC-HQ/SitePages/Directorates/G3/RecruitingOperationsCenter.aspx.
o
Applicants enlisting under this program must ship to BCT within 270. They are authorized 36
individual paid periods (battle assembly days) prior to BCT (9 months) and must attend AIT within
one year of BCT graduation.
o
Soldiers obtain any bonuses and benefits immediately upon award of MOS completion of AIT,
within two years of accessing.

5-24. Simultaneous Membership Program. There are two different types of applicants for SMP: Participant
and potential participant.
o
A participant is currently under contract with ROTC with a DD4. These applicants do not attend
BCT.
o
A potential participant is not currently in ROTC but has the potential to be. These applicants do
attend BCT. High school seniors are eligible for this program.
o
If accepted and accessed through this program, Soldiers will be non-deployable until they have
finished their degrees and receive their commissions to second lieutenant.
5-25. Direct Commission Program. USAREC manages, executes, and sustains this program for both active
duty and USAR service. Seasoned professionals may qualify to directly commission as officers with an initial
rank up to Colonel. Commissioning paths are available for specific job fields that require highly specialized
training such as the Army Chaplain Corps, the Judge Advocate General Corps, AMEDD, and Cyber.
o
Different prior experiences are required to receive a grade determination.
o
Refer to https://talent.army.mil/direct-commissioning/ for guidance and a list of eligible job fields.
5-26. Guaranteed Reserve Forces Duty Minuteman Scholarship Campaign: The Minuteman
Scholarship covers up to four years/eight semesters of full tuition and mandatory fees (uncapped) or $10,000

o

Refer to https://www.usar.army.mil/MinutemanScholarship/ for more information.

5-27. Individual Mobilization Augmentee.  The overall objective of the IMA program is to facilitate the
rapid expansion of the active Army wartime structure of the Department of War (DoW) to meet military
manpower requirements in the event of military contingency, pre-mobilization, mobilization, sustainment,
and/or demobilization operations. HRC manages USAR’s IMA program. Proponent agencies provide HRC
with area of concentration/MOS requirements for USAR vacancies, and HRC appropriately
realigns/reassigns Soldiers to ADOS, Contingency Operations ADOS, and other mobilization tours. IMA
Soldiers have great flexibility in dates to serve, access to unique jobs, and the option to travel often. This is
a prime tool to capture skilled Soldiers leaving the active Army or ARNG and recruiting them into the USAR.
o
The IMA program provides both opportunity and flexibility for civilians to work in their professions
while also serving in the USAR. IMA positions are only funded for up to 48 four-hour periods of
individual duty for training and 12 annual training days, which equals 36 days a year.
o
IMA Soldiers qualify for the same benefits as TPU Soldiers.
o
Also reference the USAR IMA site at https://www.usar.army.mil/IMA/ or the HRC IMA Homepage

Chapter 6

## Future Soldier Management


## INTRODUCTION

6-1. A recruiter’s responsibility doesn’t end when the applicant takes the oath and accesses into the USAR.
That Future Soldier still must ship to training. Preparing Future Soldiers for IADT is a joint effort between
recruiters and TPUs. Both must take equal ownership of training and motivating Future Soldiers.

## IN-PROCESSING

6-2. Recruiters should escort newly accessed USAR Soldiers to their new TPU within three to ten days of
accession. This amount of time allows their information to upload to the Defense Manpower Data Center and
the TPU’s systems. During this time, the Future Soldiers are encouraged to learn about the basics of starting
their service time.
6-3. Recruiting unit requirements:
o
Introduce the Future Soldier to TPU personnel who are responsible for assisting them in making the
transition from civilian to military life.
o
Notify TPUs of all accessions promptly.
o
Encourage the TPU to submit DA Form 1379 or DA Form 1380 so the Future Soldier is paid for
time served.
o
The recruiter will telephonically contact the TPU of assignment within 3 working days after
accession to coordinate a date and time for the Future Soldier’s sponsorship appointment.
o
The recruiter will escort the Future Soldier to the TPU no later than 10 working days after accession
or transfer (date reservation pulled) for their sponsorship by the TPU personnel section.
o
USAR Form 62–2–R will serve as the primary source document for annotating a TPU handoff.
o
The recruiter will initiate a USAREC Form 601–210.23 to prepare a Future Soldier for IADT.
o
The recruiting battalion will notify the TPU and initiate discharge orders should a Future Soldier
fail to report to IADT.
6-4. TPU requirements:
o
Assume all administrative and personnel actions effective as of the date of accession.
o
Establish in-processing dates and times for the purpose of predictability.
o
Establish a list of required documents the recruiter must provide during Future Soldier in-
processing.
o
Counsel the Future Soldier on responsibilities while awaiting IADT.
o
Assist in motivating, training, and preparing the Future Soldier for IADT. If possible, based on time
and personnel, the TPU should develop a training schedule specifically for pre-IADT Future
Soldiers to teach pre–basic training tasks.
o
Notify the recruiter immediately if a condition exists that will prevent the Future Soldier from
shipping to IADT and provide supporting documentation.
6-5. Common access card: The Future Soldier should get a CAC prior to in-processing.
6-6. Pay:
o
Bringing a Standard Form 1199A will allow the TPU to input the new Soldier into the pay system.
USAR Soldiers’ pay comes from a different funding stream than active-duty Soldiers’ pay, so the
Soldier still needs that form for IADT.
o
There are two different options for the newly accessed Soldier: Training Pay Category (TPC) P
(with pay) and TPC P (without pay). TPC P (with pay) is for those who will attend battle assembly
prior to attending IADT. TPC P (without pay) is for those who will not attend battle assembly prior
to IADT. With either option, the Soldier will incur monthly debt for selected options such as
Servicemembers’ Group Life Insurance or the initial buy-in for the Montgomery GI Bill Selected
Reserve. Refer to AR 601–210 for more information.
