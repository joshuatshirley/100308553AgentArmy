Appendix K
Army Family Action Plan Program
K – 1.  Concept and purpose
a.  The AFAP Program was established in 1983 as a result of the Chief of Staff, Army 1983 White Paper, “The Army
Family.”
b.  The HQDA AFAP GOSC Charter establishes the HQDA AFAP GOSC as an interagency committee under the guide-
lines in AR 15 – 1, and DA Memorandum, 13 September 11, SUBJECT: Army Family Action Plan Memorandum of Agree-
ment.
c.  The AFAP Program provides a mechanism for all individuals who compose the Army’s force - Soldiers (RA and
RC), retirees, DA civilians, and their Family members to identify quality of life concerns to leadership at regular intervals.
Through this process, issues are selected, assigned to a lead agency for resolution, and an action plan is established to
achieve desired change. The issues are monitored through a formal resolution process that dictates leadership review,
direction, and approval.
K – 2.  Army Family Action Plan Program execution
a.  AFAP issue development.
(1)  IMCOM garrisons will host at least one garrison AFAP issue-generating forum (for example, conference, town hall,
focus group, social media, virtual, and so forth) at any time during the year to solicit quality of life issues.
(2)  Subordinate units of AFAP GOSC member commands (for example, U.S. Army Recruiting Command, Texas
ARNG, Fifth Special Forces Group, Tripler Army Medical Center, and so forth) must submit their AFAP issues through
their AFAP GOSC member command.
(3)  Issues from the AFAP GOSC member commands that cannot be resolved at the command level must be elevated
to the Office of the Assistant Chief of Staff for Installation Management (OACSIM) for potential HQDA resolution. The
submitting command must obtain a general officer or civilian senior executive approval to submit the issue to OACSIM.
(4)  Issues must be received by OACSIM by 15 March annually to be included in the annual AFAP Command Issue
Prioritization Groups.
b.  Army staff vetting process.
(1)  OACSIM must coordinate elevated issues with Army staff functional proponents (for example, Deputy Chief of
Staff, G – 1; Office of the Surgeon General; and so forth).
(2)  If an issue does not meet criteria as provided in paragraph K – 3, OACSIM will provide an explanatory disposition
paper to the submitting AFAP GOSC member command.
(3)  OACSIM will combine AFAP issues with similar outcomes (for example, extension of Federal spouse hiring pref-
erence timeline).
(4)  The DAS will task appropriate Army staff proponents to prepare information papers on issues selected for resolu-
tion.
(5)  If there are more than 25 issues submitted, OACSIM will categorize them into separate subject area workgroups
(for example, Family support, Soldier support, employment, and so forth).
c.  AFAP Command Issue Prioritization Groups.
(1)  AFAP Command Issue Prioritization Groups are comprised of AFAP GOSC member commands.
(2)  AFAP Command Issue Prioritization Groups include a demographic breakout of a single Soldier with no depend-
ents, a DA Civilian, a single Soldier parent, a retiree, a dual military Soldier, a survivor, a junior enlisted Soldier, a junior
enlisted Soldier Family member, a noncommissioned officer (NCO), an NCO Family member, a company grade officer,
a company grade officer Family member, a field grade officer, and a field grade officer Family member from the AFAP
GOSC member command. A person can fulfill more than one demographic (for example, a dual military Soldier and an
NCO).
(3)  OACSIM will forward AFAP issues and information papers to AFAP GOSC member commands by workgroups.
(4)  The AFAP Command Issue Prioritization Group members will independently examine and rank issues by
workgroup based on their perceived importance to the Army.
(5)  The AFAP Command Issue Prioritization Group’s consolidated workgroup prioritization list must be approved by
a general officer or civilian senior executive to submit to OACSIM.
(6)  OACSIM will consolidate all AFAP GOSC member command workgroup prioritization lists.
(7)  The top two AFAP issues from each workgroup will be sent to the Vice Chief of Staff, Army (VCSA) for approval
and entry into the HQDA AFAP for Army staff action.
(8)  Upon VCSA approval, the issues are tasked to the appropriate Army staff agency to be worked toward resolution.

(9)  Issues that do not enter the HQDA AFAP are eligible for resubmission during the next submission cycle.
K – 3.  Criteria for Army Family Action Plan issues
a.  There is no limit to the number of issues that can be forwarded to the OACSIM for consideration to enter the AFAP
process.
b.  An AFAP issue must meet the following criteria to be elevated:
(1)  Issue must be a minimum of three sentences but no longer than ten sentences.
(2)  Issue must be within DAS tasking authority to resolve. Sister Service specific issues, State’s rights, or issues that
fall under the purview of agencies such as the Veterans Affairs or Department of Homeland Security are not within DAS
tasking authority.
(3)  Issue must be limited to one topic.
(4)  Issue must identify a specific demographic group impacted.
(5)  Issue must have only one recommendation.
(6)  Issue must address a measurable end product.
(7)  Issue requires a single functional proponent for resolution.
(8)  Issue cannot duplicate an existing program, service, policy, or law.
(9)  Issue cannot duplicate an active HQDA AFAP issue or an issue that has been closed in the past 3 years.
c.  Issues that do not meet criteria will not be elevated and will be returned to the submitting organization with the
reason(s) why the issue did not meet criteria.
K – 4.  Tracking and managing issues
a.  All issues are managed and tracked at all levels of the AFAP Program (garrison and/or unit; midlevel; HQDA) by
the corresponding AFAP Program manager or liaison and results are communicated to their constituents, following con-
ferences and steering committee meetings.
b.  An action officer within the functional proponent is assigned to develop and implement an action plan to resolve the
issue. Support agencies may be assigned to assist the lead in completing the issue’s action plan. Action officers will provide
updates on the status of issue resolution, as required, at all levels.
c.  A transfer of the responsibility for an issue may be initiated by the functional proponent. If a transfer is not mutually
agreeable, the Steering Committee chair will determine the functional proponent. At HQDA, the DAS may determine the
functional proponent.
K – 5.  Resolving issues
a.  AFAP issues are assigned a status—
(1)  Active.  Issues with an action plan and in the working phase of resolution.
(2)  Combined.  Issues that are incorporated into another active issue because they have the same objective.
(3)  Elevated.  Issues that are elevated to a higher headquarters for review and/or resolution.
(4)  Completed.  Issue is resolved; recommendation or “best solution” attained.
(5)  Unattainable.  Issue cannot be resolved at this time because of absence of support or resources.
b.  Issues remain active until the Steering Committee or HQDA AFAP GOSC determines the issue is elevated, com-
pleted, combined, or unattainable. After a 3–year hold, completed or unattainable issues may be addressed again.
