5–12.  Recoupment from Soldiers who voluntarily reclassify
Except as provided in paragraph 5–11b(7), a Soldier who voluntarily reclassifies and fails to complete the
obligated service will refund an amount equal to the unfulfilled portion of the agreement.
a.  Soldiers who are bonus recipients may voluntarily reclassify provided—
(1)  They volunteer in writing and agree to the recoupment of the unearned portion of the bonus.
(2)  The MOS requested is a more critical skill than the PMOS currently held as determined by HRC.
b.  The effective date of recoupment will be based on the date of reclassification.
c.  Approval authority of all reclassifications involving bonus recipients is the CG, HRC, Retention and
Reclassification Branch.
5–13.  Annual selective retention bonus reporting requirement to Director of Military Personnel
Management, Army Retention Branch
a.  The SRB program review must include—
(1)  Total cost (projected).
(2)  Total cost (actual).
(3)  Soldiers retained (projected).
(4)  Soldiers retained (actual).
(5)  Recoupments (USD).
(6)  Recoupments (total number).
(7)  Demographics. Demographics will include, at a minimum, biological sex and race/ethnicity.
(8)  Critical MOSs retained. Critical MOS is defined as MOSs listed as priority skill sets in the quarterly
enlisted retention reports to the Office of the Secretary of Defense.
b.  A consolidated report will be submitted electronically to the Senior Army Retention Operations NCO
mailbox at usarmy.pentagon.hqda-dcs-g-1.mbx.army-retention@army.mil.
Chapter 6
Regular Army Transfer of Post-9/11 GI Bill Education Benefits
6–1.  Program responsibilities
a.  The DCS, G – 1 (Retention) will—
(1)  Approve or disapprove exception to policy requests to reenlist or extend to meet SRR for transfer of
education benefits (TEB).
(2)  Verify separation approval status in the DES process upon submission of request for extension.
(3)  Provide current DA forms to senior career counselors to conduct mandatory TEB DES counseling.
b.  CG, HRC (Education Incentives Branch (EIB)) will—
(1)  Approve or disapprove Soldiers requesting TEB.
(2)  Determine Soldiers’ eligibility to maintain TEB benefits.
(3)  Adjust Soldiers’ obligation end dates for those previously approved for TEB, requiring an exception
(see DA Pam 601 – 280).
c.  Commanders of ACOMs, ASCCs, and DRUs will incorporate TEB Program into inspections at all
levels to ensure successful accomplishment of subordinates’ responsibilities.
d.  Command career counselors will—
(1)  Process exception to policy requests to reenlist or extend to meet the SRR for TEB.
(2)  Coordinate with installation offices to ensure Soldiers are referred to their proper approving official.

(3)  Supervise senior career counselors and career counselors to ensure all responsibilities are com-
pleted in a timely and proficient manner.
(4)  Conduct semiannual training for all assigned career counselors.
(5)  Ensure Soldiers of units within the installation’s geographical oversight are supported appropriately.
(6)  Ensure that senior career counselors (operations-level career counselors at a minimum) have e-

Division office for mandatory DES TEB counseling.
e.  Senior career counselors will—
(1)  Conduct individual training for subordinate career counselors.
(2)  Manage and request Benefits for Education Administrative Services Tool (BEAST) (review only)
accounts for subordinate counselors (in accordance with DA Pam 601  – 280).
(3)  Obtain weekly DES rosters as posted by HRC, Retention and Reclassification Branch to ensure
Soldiers are coded with Restriction Code RET9H.
(4)  Conduct mandatory TEB counseling for Soldiers in their units upon entry into DES.
(5)  Career counselors assigned to Soldier Recovery Units will be required to conduct mandatory DES
TEB counseling for all enlisted Soldiers upon in-processing to unit. Mandatory TEB DES counseling will
be done on a standard DA Form 4856 (Developmental Counseling Form). These mandatory DES TEB
counseling forms will be maintained on file for 5 years.
f.  The servicing career counselor (MOS 79S) is the subject matter expert for the transfer of Post-9/11
GI Bill benefits for the enlisted Soldiers of their respective unit. Career counselors will have read-only ac-
cess on the BEAST web page. Career counselors will—
(1)  Obtain TEB rosters (submitted, pending, and rejected) weekly and notify Soldiers of the SRR re-
quirement.
(2)  Properly counsel Soldiers on meeting the required service obligation through an extension or reen-
listment, if required. Career counselors will notify applicable Soldiers that they have to take action to meet
the required service obligation even if their TEB request was denied due to not meeting the SRR.
Note. Soldiers will be denied in TEB immediately if they do not meet the SRR on the date of submission.
(3)  Counsel every Soldier using a DA Form 4856 on the date of reenlistment or extension of the re-
quirement to submit or resubmit their request for TEB (available at https://milconnect.dmdc.osd.mil) im-
mediately upon acquiring the 4-year SRR. This counseling form will be kept with the Soldier’s residual file
for the reenlistment or extension.
(4)  Notify the Soldier if they are eligible to apply for an extension.
(5)  Contact HRC, EIB if there is an error with a Soldier’s obligation end date.
Note. All other questions or concerns to include any action with career counselor error should be directed
through the career counselor’s retention hierarchy to Army G  – 1 (Retention).
(6)  Ensure leaders and Soldiers are aware of program rules and requirements.
(7)  Take part in the units’ in-processing and clearing checklists.
(8)  Provide Soldiers basic TEB information.
(9)  Ensure each Soldier understands that their obligation end date is verified in TEB.
(10)  Ensure each Soldier understands they must notify the certifying or approving official if separated
before the obligation end date to determine if they are eligible to maintain benefits.
(11)  Monitor submitted, pending, and rejected TEB requests daily.
(12)  Contact each Soldier who desires to transfer, but does not have sufficient remaining service (4
years).
(13)  Notify Soldiers who are eligible to apply for an exception to explain the requirements of the excep-
tion.
(14)  Comply with all instructions and responsibilities described herein or be subject to disciplinary ac-
tion.
g.  Soldiers will—
(1)  Provide a signed memorandum of justification for exception to policy requests to reenlist or extend
to meet the SRR for TEB (see DA Pam 601  – 280).
(2)  Notify HRC, EIB when they are unable to complete TEB service obligation.
(3)  Verify their status (submitted, pending, approved, or rejected) on the TEB web page in milConnect.
