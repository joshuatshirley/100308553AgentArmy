7–10.  Administrative withdrawal of access
As set forth in paragraph 7–4c, access must be withdrawn in the DISS when the events described therein
occur. When regular access to the level of classified information is no longer required in the normal
course of an individual’s duties, the individual will be debriefed in accordance with provisions of AR 380–5
and access withdrawn in DISS.
7–11.  Suspending access
a.  Commanders, or those listed in DoDM 5200.02 may suspend access for cause when information
relative to any of the adjudicative guidelines exists and raises a serious question as to the individuals’
ability or intent to protect national security information (see para 9–13).
b.  Commanders and security managers must annotate access suspensions in DISS. Informal suspen-
sion or just removing access is not authorized.
c.  Commanders must notify persons in writing, within 5 business days, when their access has been
suspended and include a brief statement of the reason for the suspension of access consistent with the
interests of national security. Individuals will acknowledge, in writing, receipt of the suspension notifica-
tion, which must state that the receipt is not an acknowledgment of culpability or concurrence with the
suspension. Supervisors will also be notified of the access suspension or suspension of sensitive duties.
d.  When information relative to any of the adjudicative guidelines no longer raises serious concerns
regarding the individual’s ability or intent to protect classified information, suspended access may be re-
stored in DISS. The restoral of access by the commander does not prohibit the CAS from making an un-
favorable national security determination.
Chapter 8
Personnel Security Actions
Section I
Requirements
8–1.  General
a.  The authority to make national security eligibility determinations that will result in an unfavorable
personnel security action is limited to those authorities designated in DoDM 5200.02.
b.  Unfavorable national security eligibility determinations do not include administrative national security
eligibility downgrades or withdrawals based on changed duties or similar circumstances unrelated to a
national security eligibility adjudication or withdrawals of interim eligibility based on reportable activities.
c.  This chapter is intended only for the internal operation of the DA and is not intended and may not be
relied upon to create or enlarge the jurisdiction or review authority of any court or administrative tribunal,
including the Merit Systems Protection Board.
d.  It is strictly prohibited to take, fail to take, or threaten to take any action affecting an individual’s eligi-
bility for access to classified information as a reprisal for a protected disclosure of fraud, waste, or abuse
pursuant to Presidential Policy Directive 19 (see DoDM 5200.02).
8–2.  Administrative removal of eligibility
a.  See DoDM 5200.02 for loss of jurisdiction.
b.  Debrief the individual in accordance with AR 380–5 and remove access in DISS upon receipt of a
loss of jurisdiction, no determination made, administrative withdraw, or other administrative removal of
eligibility.
c.  The security manager will verify the reason for the administrative removal of eligibility is not for de-
rogatory reasons prior to the Commander or designee granting an interim.
8–3.  Suspension of national security eligibility
See DoDM 5200.02 for suspension of national security eligibility.
a.  Except for cases involving NISP contractor personnel that are administered according to the sus-
pension standards in DoDD 5220.6 and DoD 5220.22–R, the CAS and the DoD Intelligence Community
central adjudication facilities are solely responsible for suspending national security eligibility.

b.  The CAS authorized representative notifies persons in writing, through the local security manager,
when their eligibility has been suspended and includes a brief statement of the reason for the suspension

Section II
Due Process
8–4.  Unfavorable personnel security actions
a.  When the CAS receives reportable activities from any source, including an incident report, a CE
alert, or a background investigation, the CAS authorized representative will determine if the information is
within the scope of SEAD 4. If a denial or revocation of national security eligibility is considered appropri-
ate, the CAS authorized representative will forward a LOI and enclosed SOR through the security man-
ager to the individual.
b.  No unfavorable personnel security action will be taken under the authority of this regulation or
DoDM 5200.02 without providing the individual a written LOI and SOR regarding why the unfavorable per-
sonnel security action is being taken. Exceptions may occur in accordance with EO 12968. DCS, G–2
(DAMI–CD) will process those exceptions through OTJAG and Army Office of the General Counsel for
decision by the Under Secretary of the Army or Secretary of the Army.
c.  LOIs and SORs are processed in accordance with DoDM 5200.02.
d.  At the time the LOI and SOR is issued, the commander will determine whether it is in the best inter-
est of national security to take interim action to suspend the individual’s access to classified information or
suspension of assignment to sensitive duties or other duties requiring a trustworthiness determination.
e.  An incident report will not be submitted on the same information that is included in the LOI and
SOR. The commander’s access determination will be documented and signed and a copy maintained by
the security manager until the final adjudication. If access is suspended—
(1)  Annotate the access suspension in DISS. Informal suspension (removal of access) is not author-
ized.
(2)  Debrief the individual from access to classified information in accordance with AR 380–5.
(3)  Individuals will sign a receipt, acknowledging receipt of the access suspension notification.
f.  The commander will designate a point of contact to serve as a liaison between the adjudication facil-
ity and the individual. The commander’s responsibilities, through the point of contact, include—
(1)  Delivering the LOI and SOR to the individual.
(2)  Obtaining an acknowledgment receipt with the individual’s intent to respond within the time speci-
fied and submitting the receipt to the CAS in DISS within 10 calendar days.
(3)  Explaining the consequences of the proposed action and the need to provide a complete response
that addresses each security concern within the required timeframe.
(4)  Ensuring the individual is aware of the due date for the information.
(5)  Explaining how to request extensions.
(6)  Explaining how to obtain copies of investigative records.
(7)  Explaining the procedures for responding to the LOI and SOR.
(8)  Explaining the individual’s entitlement to obtain legal counsel or other assistance at their own ex-
pense within the relevant time periods.
(9)  Reviewing the response package for completeness prior to submission.
(10)  Reviewing supporting documentation from the individual to verify the individual has addressed
each security concern and providing additional guidance as necessary to ensure the package is com-
plete.
(11)  Submitting supporting documentation from the commander and individual in DISS.
(12)  Closing out the request for action within the timelines identified by the CAS or requesting an ex-
tension if necessary.
g.  The security manager will promptly return an LOI, SOR, letter of denial (LOD), or letter of revocation
(LOR) to the CAS when the individual is no longer assigned to the command. If the individual has been
discharged from military service with no Reserve obligation, is incarcerated, or is dropped from the rolls

as a deserter, the security manager will return the LOI, SOR, LOD, or LOR and upload the discharge or-
ders or supporting documentation to the CAS via the DISS.
h.  Individuals may request a copy of the background investigation and adjudication files under the pro-
visions of the Privacy Act (5 USC 552a) from the ISP FOIA office.
i.  The commander or supporting security manager will ensure that the individual acknowledges receipt
of the LOI and SOR by signing and dating the response form enclosed in the LOI and SOR. If the individ-

sor and returned to the CAS.
j.  The commander will ensure that the individual is counseled regarding the seriousness of the CAS
contemplated action and offer resources to assist the individual. The security manager will offer advice
and assistance needed in forming a response.
k.  The individual’s written response should address each issue raised in the LOI and SOR. Failing to
address each issue may result in the CAS rendering an unfavorable determination. Any pertinent written
documentation must be labeled in a manner to ensure it is properly associated with the issue raised in the
LOI or SOR. Commanders must include a command recommendation to CAS on whether to retain the
individual’s national security eligibility pending the conclusion of an investigation or when rendering a final
determination, and provide the individual with a copy of that recommendation. The individual will forward
the response through the security manager to the CAS.
l.  The security manager will notify the CAS when an individual no longer wishes to respond to an LOI
or SOR. The security manager will upload a signed document from the individual, documenting the deci-
sion not to respond, prior to the 30-day suspense or the suspense of any granted extension. If the individ-
ual refuses to sign the document, the refusal will be documented and signed by the commander or super-
visor and returned to the CAS.
m.  The final CAS decision will be forwarded through the servicing security manager to the individual.
The security manager will also notify the commander, the servicing personnel office, the special security
officer if the individual had SCI access, and the servicing program security officer if the individual had
SAP access.
8–5.  Final unfavorable national security eligibility determinations
a.  See DoDM 5200.02 for final unfavorable national security determinations.
b.  Upon receipt of an LOD or LOR, the security manager will debrief the individual from access to clas-
sified information in accordance with AR 380–5.
8–6.  Appealing final unfavorable determination
a.  See DoDM 5200.02 for appealing final unfavorable determinations.
b.  Extension requests must be in writing, endorsed by the immediate commander, include a justifica-
tion, and submitted through the security manager to the PSAB president for approval. If the individual
does not submit an appeal, the denial or revocation made by the CAS is final. No further appeal will be
authorized and due process will be considered complete.
8–7.  Defense Office of Hearings and Appeals personal appearance
See DoDM 5200.02 for personal appearances with the Defense Office of Hearings and Appeals.
8–8.  Written appeals
a.  See DoDM 5200.02 for written appeals to the PSAB.
b.  Written appeals should address the disqualifying issues identified in the LOD or LOR and should
present any existing mitigation as defined in SEAD 4, to include pertinent supporting documentation.
8–9.  Army Personnel Security Appeals Board
a.  See DoDM 5200.02 for the PSAB structure and functioning.
b.  The PSAB will be comprised of a President and two members at the minimum military grade of O–6
or civilian grade of General Government (GG)-15 or equivalent. In cases where the appellant is at or
above the grade of military O–6 or GG–15 or equivalent, at least one member of the board will be equiva-
lent or senior in grade to the appellant.
c.  The board will have access to legal counsel and medical expertise.
d.  Members of the PSAB must have Top Secret national security eligibility.
