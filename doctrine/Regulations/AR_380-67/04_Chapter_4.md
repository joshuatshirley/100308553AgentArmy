Chapter 4
Reciprocal Acceptance of Prior Investigations and Personnel Security Determinations
4–1.  General
a.  Investigations will not be duplicated when a previously conducted and current investigation meets
the prescribed scope and standard to support a national security or access determination. Previously con-
ducted investigations and previously rendered national security determinations will be accepted in accord-
ance with DoDM 5200.02.
b.  Interim national security eligibility will be reciprocally accepted throughout the DA (see DoDM
5200.02 for reciprocity exceptions).
c.  When a valid DoD national security eligibility exists, security managers will not request a new back-
ground investigation or request prior investigative files for review. Exceptions to reciprocity are in accord-
ance with DoDM 5200.02.
d.  If the individual provides information regarding a previous investigation or a valid adjudicative deter-
mination from a non-DoD agency, this information must be verified by the security manager. The security
manager will advise the CAS of the existing investigation or adjudicative determination, provide any avail-
able documentation, to include proof of current eligibility or documentation verifying an individual’s previ-
ous investigation, and request the CAS grant eligibility in the DoD.
e.  Reciprocity for positions that require access to SCI information will be made in accordance with
DoDM 5105.21, Volume 3.
4–2.  Transferring to the Army from another DoD Component or federal agency
a.  Commanders of gaining activities of individuals transferring to the Army will request individuals iden-
tify any changes since their last SF 86 submission.
b.  Following review of the changes, new reportable activities (paragraphs 9–3 to 9–5) of derogatory
nature will be forwarded to the CAS via a DISS incident report.
c.  New information that is reportable but not derogatory in nature will be forwarded to the Director,
DCSA via a DISS supplemental CSR.
Chapter 5
Requesting Background Investigations
Section I
Limitations and Restrictions for Submitting Background Investigations
5–1.  General
The ASO is the Army’s centralized office to request initial investigations and reinvestigations from the ISP.
Requests for background investigations will be limited to those required to accomplish the DA mission.
Requests for background investigations will be submitted only by the authorized requesters designated in
paragraph 5–3. These authorized requesters will be held responsible for determining if persons under
their jurisdiction require a background investigation. Proper planning is required to ensure that investiga-
tive requests are submitted in sufficient time to allow completion of the investigation and adjudication.

5–2.  Authorized personnel security investigative agencies
The Army’s authorized ISP is the DCSA. Commanders are not authorized to conduct background investi-
gations on individuals. This authority is explicit to the ISP. Exceptions to the delegated investigative au-
thority must be approved by the DNI.
5–3.  Authorized requesters
a.  Background investigations will only be submitted to the ISP from the following:
(1)  ASO.
(2)  CAS.
(3)  DCS, G–2 (DAMI–CD).
(4)  U.S. Army Recruiting Command.
b.  Security managers and HR representatives are authorized to submit background investigation re-
quests to the ASO. Requesters to the ASO are responsible for determining if persons under their jurisdic-
tion require a background investigation.
c.  DA contractors and vendors are authorized to submit requests for background investigations to the
ASO under the oversight of the supporting Government security office. Contractors are not authorized to
submit background investigations to the ISP, except for those contractors supporting the ASO mission or
under the direct supervision of Army military or civilian personnel.
d.  The DCS, G–2 (DAMI–CD) reserves the right to approve the submission of requests for background
investigations by other than those listed in paragraph 5–3a.
5–4.  Limits on submitting background investigations
a.  Personnel who are employed by or serving in a military, civilian, contractor, or consultant capacity
may be submitted for national security background investigation only when such eligibility is required for a
lawful and authorized Government purpose in connection with official duties. The number of persons re-
quiring background investigations and national security eligibility will be limited to those that are essential
to current operations and clearly authorized by Army policy.
b.  Unauthorized, unnecessary, or duplicative background investigations are prohibited. An investiga-
tion is not authorized unless there is an official requirement.
c.  Commanders of the sponsoring Army activity will submit contractors and consultants who require
national security eligibility but no access to classified information for the required investigation in accord-
ance with paragraphs 3–13 and 3–14. DCSA will not process a background investigation request for an
employee of or a consultant to a contractor when there is not a legitimate requirement for access to clas-
sified information in supporting a U.S. Government or foreign government requirement in accordance with
32 CFR 117 and DoDM 5200.22, Volume 3.
d.  Spouses of general officers and flag officers will not be processed for national security eligibility for
access to classified information unless there is need for them to access classified information or perform
sensitive duties as part of an official unit support or readiness function.
e.  With the exception of military personnel, minors who are under the age of 18 will not be investigated
nor granted national security eligibility.
f.  Investigative requests will not be submitted for eligibility at a level higher than what has been desig-
nated for the position or required by the MOS or duty assignment.
g.  One-time access (see para 7–8) will be used in lieu of a background investigation if access to classi-
fied information or performance of sensitive duties is required for less than 180 days.
Section II
Requesting Background Investigations
5–5.  Criteria for requesting background investigations
a.  Authorized requesters will use table B–1 (see app B) to determine the type of investigation required
for the specific position or duty designation.
b.  Only submit a request to reopen a background investigation to obtain a missing scope item when
directed by the CAS.
c.  Only request RSIs when a subject interview is required upon return from deployment.

d.  Promptly notify the ASO when an investigation is no longer needed. Do not cancel an investigation if
an individual is transferring to the Individual Ready Reserves or another Army or DoD activity.
e.  Facility security officers will submit investigative requests for contractor personnel under the Na-

f.  Only request Advance NAC Status Reports on initial background investigation requests. Do not re-

g.  Submit spouse SACs on individuals with SCI access who intend to marry or have married a foreign

i.  Security managers, to include those at the U.S. Army Recruiting Command, will address correction
notifications from the ISP on all personnel within 10 business days after receipt of the correspondence.
j.  If the ISP rejects a background investigation request, resubmit the investigation request within 7
business days.
5–6.  Request procedures
To ensure efficient and effective completion of required investigations, all requests for background investi-
gations will be prepared and forwarded in accordance with appendix C and paragraph 5–2.
5–7.  Priority requests
a.  To ensure that background investigations are conducted in an efficient manner, requests for priority
for individual or categories of investigations will be kept to a minimum. Authorized requesters (see para
5–3) will not assign priority to any DA background investigation or categories of investigations, except
those in support of deployment, Soldiers in Recovery, training requiring final Top Secret or Top Se-
cret/SCI, Presidential Support Program, or contract linguists without approval from the DCS, G–2 (DAMI–
CD).
b.  To ensure that background investigation requests are submitted to the ISP in an orderly and efficient
manner, requests for expedited service (internal to the ASO) will be kept to a minimum. The ASO may
discontinue expedited requests in accordance to changes in mission.
5–8.  Personal data provided by the subject of the background investigation
a.  The ISP requires certain relevant data concerning the subject of the investigation to conduct the
background investigation. The subject of each PSI will provide the personal information required by the
ISP.
b.  Additional information required to make a final or interim personnel security determination will be
obtained as appropriate from the ISP or CAS or through the conduct of local records checks of local per-
sonnel, base, military police, medical, and security records, as applicable. Security officers are not author-
ized to conduct investigations off the installation or through the internet (for example, courthouse records,
credit checks, or law enforcement checks). Local record checks will be retained on file until final eligibility
is determined (see para 5–13).
c.  When the ISP returns fingerprint results indicating that the quality of the fingerprints is not accepta-
ble, an additional set of fingerprints will be obtained from the subject. To avoid additional charges, the fin-
gerprints will be coded as a reprint in accordance with ISP guidance.
d.  It is incumbent upon the subject of each background investigation to provide the personal infor-
mation required by this regulation. At a minimum, the individual will complete the appropriate investigative
forms and provide fingerprints of a quality acceptable to the FBI, as necessary. During the course of a
background investigation, individuals will update any changes in employers, addresses, and other contact
information through the security office.
e.  Failure to provide requested information within the time limit prescribed or refusal to provide or per-
mit access to the relevant information required by this regulation may result in termination of the individ-
ual’s background investigation request, national security eligibility, access to classified information, or as-
signment to sensitive duties using the policies of paragraph 8–4 or 9–13.

5–9.  Change of duty station during a background investigation
If a personnel file, security file, or DISS indicates that an investigation was initiated by the losing com-
mand or activity, gaining security managers should notify the ISP to ensure the investigation is not can-
celled, update the subject’s location with the ISP, and update the Security Management Office in DISS

5–10.  Polygraph
The policies, responsibilities, and procedures for the use of polygraph for security purposes in the Army
are governed by AR 381–20, AR 195–6, and DoDM 5200.02.
5–11.  Post-adjudicative inquiries
Post-adjudicative inquiries may be conducted by CAS personnel or an approved ISP in accordance with
DoDM 5200.02.
Section III
Accountability of Personnel Security Reports and Records
5–12.  General
Personnel security data, reports, records, and investigative results must be handled with the highest de-
gree of discretion. Access to and dissemination of such information is afforded only for the purposes in
the applicable SORN and to persons whose official duties require such information. Personnel security
records may be used only to determine continued national security eligibility requiring such investigation
and for quality assurance, compliance oversight, law enforcement investigations, insider threat actions,
authorized CI inquiries and investigations, and other official uses stated in the applicable SORN or as im-
plemented by AR 25–22. Other uses by security offices are subject to the specific written authorization of
the USD(I&S).
5–13.  Retention of documents used to request a background investigation
a.  Security managers will retain national security position designation and background investigation
justification documentation for 5 years.
b.  Security managers will retain local records checks on file until the final clearance is granted.
c.  Documentation will be maintained in a paper file or electronically. Electronic files will be saved in a
secure location, in accordance with personally identifiable information storage requirements, that allows
for future retrieval (for example, shared drive limited to the security office vice personal email).
5–14.  Safeguarding responsibilities
a.  DA personnel responsible for administering the DA PSP and all DA personnel authorized access to
personnel security reports and records will ensure that the use of such information is limited to that au-
thorized by this regulation and that such reports and records are safeguarded as prescribed herein.
b.  Commanders will establish internal controls to ensure adequate safeguarding and limit access to
and use of personnel security reports and records as required by paragraphs 5–14 through 5–16.
c.  The creation, maintenance, and disposition of personnel security records in the Army should be in
accordance with paragraph 5–16 and paragraph 5–17, the applicable SORN, and AR 25–400–2.
