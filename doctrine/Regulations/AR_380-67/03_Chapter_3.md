Chapter 3
Specific Investigative Requirements by Population
Section I
Restrictions on Designating National Security Positions or Requiring National Security Eligibility
3–1.  General
The number of persons requiring access to classified information or required to hold a sensitive position
will be kept to a minimum, consistent with the requirements of operations. Special attention will be given
to eliminating unnecessary access to classified information and requests for background investigations.

3–2.  Position Designation Tool
The PDT is the DoD-accepted web-based tool to determine position designations and to determine if civil-
ian or military personnel or contractors and consultants require national security eligibility.
3–3.  Limits on requiring national security eligibility
a.  National security eligibility must be kept to the absolute minimum necessary to meet mission re-
quirements. National security eligibility will not be issued—
(1)  To non-U.S. citizens.
(2)  To persons in non-sensitive positions.
(3)  For ease of movement of persons within a restricted, controlled, or industrial area whose duties do
not require access to classified information.
(4)  To persons who may only have inadvertent access to sensitive information or areas, such as emer-
gency service personnel, firemen, doctors, nurses, police, ambulance drivers, or similar personnel.
(5)  To persons who can be escorted by personnel with national security eligibility and access (for ex-
ample, maintenance personnel and emergency responders).
(6)  To drivers, chauffeurs, food service personnel, vendors, and similar commercial sales or service
personnel whose duties do not require access to classified information.
(7)  To maintenance or cleaning personnel who may only have inadvertent access to classified infor-
mation unless such access cannot be reasonably prevented.
(8)  To persons who perform maintenance on office equipment, such as computers, telephones, print-
ers, and similar equipment, who can be denied classified access by physical security measures.
b.  Summer hires, non-appropriated fund employees, ARNG State employees in direct support of a
DoD mission, or those who are employed by or serving in a consultant capacity to the DA may be consid-
ered for national security eligibility only when it is required in connection with official duties. Temporary,
intermittent, summer hire, or seasonal employees will not be assigned to special sensitive or critical sensi-
tive positions unless approved as a waiver by the DCS, G–2 (DAMI–CD).
c.  With the exception of military personnel, minors who are under the age of 18 will not be investigated
for nor granted national security eligibility.
Section II
National Security Positions
3–4.  Designation of national security positions
a.  Certain positions entail duties of such a sensitive nature, including access to classified information,
that the misconduct of an incumbent in such a position could result in an unacceptable adverse effect on
the national security. These positions are referred to in this regulation as sensitive or national security po-
sitions. It is vital to national security that great care be exercised in the selection of individuals to fill such
positions. Similarly, only positions truly meeting one or more of the criteria established using the PDT, as
set forth in paragraph 3–5, will be designated as national security positions. A national security position
will not be downgraded or reclassified as non-sensitive solely to aid in recruiting personnel or to retain
personnel who no longer possess national security eligibility as required for a position.
b.  Previously, the designation of positions was reserved for civilian positions. The DA has further ex-
tended position designations to all populations to include military and contractors. The PDT must be used
to determine the proper sensitivity level prior to designating any position as national security or prior to
upgrading any position from noncritical sensitive (Secret) to critical or special sensitive (Top Secret or Top
Secret/SCI).
3–5.  Criteria for designation of national security positions
a.  The PDT contains the criteria to designate a national security position. The PDT will be used to de-
termine the position designation of sensitive and national security positions.
b.  Commanders will designate, in writing, an entity to use the PDT for military positions to verify the
requirement for a T5 background investigation if a T5 is not already required by the MOS.
c.  In accordance with DCS, G–1 procedures, hiring managers will use the PDT to determine the inves-
tigative requirements for civilian positions.
d.  The entity using the PDT must coordinate with the local security manager.

3–6.  Authority and requirements to designate national security positions
a.  The authority to designate national security positions is limited to HQDA principal officials and com-
manders of ACOMs, ASCCs, and DRUs.
(1)  HQDA principal officials may delegate this authority, in writing, to applicable subordinate officers
and DA Civilians no lower than the rank of O–6 or grade of GS–15.
(2)  Commanders of ACOMs, ASCCs, and DRUs may delegate this authority, in writing, to applicable
subordinate commanders no lower than the rank of O–6 or grade of GS–15.
(3)  Designation authority of critical and special sensitive positions for the Reserve Components may be
delegated in writing to an O–6 or GS–15 in their headquarters’ G–2 office.
b.  Commanders will document national security position designations with written justification and re-
tain locally for 5 years.
3–7.  Limitation of sensitive positions
It is the responsibility of those authorities authorized to designate national security positions to ensure
that only those positions that meet the criteria established in the PDT are designated as sensitive and the
designation of national security positions is held to a minimum, consistent with mission requirements.
Designating authorities will maintain an accounting of the number of sensitive positions by category (that
is, special-sensitive, critical-sensitive, or noncritical-sensitive).
Section III
Civilian Personnel
3–8.  General
Before a DA Civilian can be assigned to a sensitive or national security position or be given access to
classified information, a background investigation must be favorably adjudicated (see para 7–3 for interim
or temporary eligibility). The investigation requirements are set forth in this section according to the posi-
tion designation.
3–9.  Noncritical-sensitive positions
a.  Civilian personnel holding noncritical-sensitive positions require a favorably adjudicated T3 (see
para 7–3 for temporary or interim eligibility). A NACLC favorably adjudicated during a previous military or
contractor employment may be used to support civilian appointment, provided a T3 background investiga-
tion has been submitted to the ISP and the individual has not had a 24-month break in service since com-
pletion of the last investigation.
b.  Temporary, intermittent, summer hire, or seasonal employees normally do not require access to
classified information. If the position requires access to classified information, the proper investigation will
be initiated to meet the required access level (see para 5–4f for age limitations).
c.  Dual status technicians of the ARNG and military technicians of the Army Reserves are both civil
service employees and military National Guard or Reserve members. Dual status technicians are in a
minimum of a noncritical-sensitive position and must have a minimum of a favorably adjudicated T3. The
Military NACLC is insufficient for dual status or military technicians.
3–10.  Special sensitive and critical sensitive positions
Civilian personnel holding special sensitive and critical sensitive positions require a favorably adjudicated
T5 background investigation (see para 7–3 for temporary or interim eligibility).
Section IV
Military Personnel
3–11.  General
a.  All military positions are national security positions regardless of whether the Servicemember re-
quires access to classified information.
b.  The T3 or its equivalent is the minimum investigation required for entry into the U.S. Army, U.S.
Army Reserve (USAR), and ARNG (see para 7–3 for temporary or interim eligibility).

c.  The appointment, enlistment, and induction of each member of the Military Departments or their Re-
serve Components will be based on a favorably adjudicated background investigation (see para 7–3 for
temporary or interim eligibility).
d.  The T3 or its equivalent will be conducted upon re-entry into the U.S. Army, USAR, and ARNG
when there has been a break in service longer than 24 months. This includes retired Soldiers ordered to
active duty as Retiree Recalls, retired Regular Army Soldiers appointed in the Ready Reserve, and Gray

3–12.  Reserve Components
a.  Reserve Component (ARNG and USAR) military personnel are subject to the requirements of para-
graph 3–11.
b.  For ARNG civilian or contractor personnel, to include State employees, who require a background
investigation to support a national security determination, the ARNG security manager will validate the
investigative requirements for all State employees prior to submitting a request for a background investi-
gation. ARNG State employees who principally perform homeland security missions not in direct support
of the DA and require access to non-DA classified information or non-DA information technology systems,
will consult the State’s respective Homeland Security Advisor’s Office for background investigation pro-
cessing.
c.  The commander of each Reserve Component (ARNG and USAR) must authorize the designation of
a critical and special sensitive position requiring a T5 investigation. Designation may be delegated in writ-
ing to the minimum rank of O–6 or grade of GS–15 in their headquarters G–2 office.
d.  Dual status technicians and military technicians are subject to paragraphs 3–9 and 3–11.
Section V
Contractors and Consultants or Grantees
3–13.  Contractors
a.  Background investigation requirements for contractor personnel requiring access to classified infor-
mation are addressed in 32 CFR 117 and AR 380–49.
b.  Contractors requiring a T3 or T5 but who do not require access to classified information will be pro-
cessed by the sponsoring Army activity.
c.  The sponsoring Army activity commander or security manager will manage the DISS record and be
responsible for oversight of the contractor’s national security eligibility, to include reporting reportable ac-
tivities.
d.  See paragraph 3–12 for ARNG contractors.
3–14.  Consultants and grantees of a Department of Defense Component
A consultant or grantee in a national security position who is directly engaged by the Army (as opposed to
an employee of a contractor) is processed in accordance with DoDM 5200.02.
3–15.  Temporary employees
Refer to DoDM 5200.02 for temporary employees requiring access to classified information or perfor-
mance of sensitive duties.
Section VI

entities who are not supporting a DoD mission.

Section VII
Special Programs
3–19.  General
It is the policy of the DA to establish, to the extent possible, uniform and consistent background investiga-
tion requirements. Accordingly, investigations exceeding established requirements are authorized only
when mandated by statute, national regulations, international agreements, or applicable EOs or when ex-
pressed authority has been provided by the USD(I&S). There are certain special programs originating at
the national or international level that require background investigations and procedures of a special na-
ture. These programs and the special investigative requirements imposed by them are described in this
section.
3–20.  Presidential support activities
a.  DoDI 5210.87 prescribes the policies and procedures for the nomination, screening, selection, and
continued evaluation of DA military personnel, civilian personnel, and contractor employees assigned to
or used in Presidential support activities. The type of investigation for individuals assigned to Presidential
support activities varies according to whether the person investigated qualifies for category one, category
two, or category three.

3–25.  Others
Requests for approval to conduct an investigation on other personnel not provided for in this chapter or
that deviate from the position designations outlined in the PDT will be submitted, detailing the justification,

3–26.  Combat operations, Department of the Army–directed mobilization
a.  National security eligibility requirements for contractor personnel in a theater supporting contingency
