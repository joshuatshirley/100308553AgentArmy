Chapter 7
National Security Eligibility and Granting Access
Section I
National Security Eligibility
7–1.  General
a.  National security eligibility indicates that an individual has been granted eligibility to hold a sensitive
position or to access classified information, should their official duties require it and there is a need to
know in accordance with DoDM 5200.02.
b.  National security eligibility determinations are a function distinct from granting access to classified
national security information. The adjudication facility determines the level of eligibility based on the level

c.  With the exception of military personnel, minors who are under the age of 18 will not be investigated
nor granted national security eligibility.
7–2.  National security eligibility
a.  Authorities designated in DoDM 5200.02 record the issuance, denial, or revocation of national secu-
rity eligibility in the DISS.
b.  National security eligibility remains valid until an individual has no further official relationship with
Federal agencies for more than 24 months, the eligibility is administratively withdrawn, or official action
has been taken to deny or revoke the national security eligibility.
c.  National security eligibility of DA military and civilian personnel is granted, denied, or revoked only
by the CAS, except in rare circumstances by the Secretary of the Army or the Director, DCSA. When na-
tional security eligibility has been issued by another Federal agency, the security manager will follow reci-
procity procedures in chapter 4 and DoDM 5200.02.
d.  If a final national security eligibility determination or due process action is not received within 45
days of the investigation close date, the security manager will request an update from the CAS.
e.  If derogatory reportable activities arise while the case is pending adjudication at the CAS, the com-
mander will submit an incident report so the CAS may evaluate the information.
f.  To facilitate communication with the CAS, security managers will ensure the timely processing of
new personnel and immediately establish a security management office owning relationship in the DISS.
7–3.  Interim (or temporary) national security eligibility
Unless otherwise prohibited by policy, an individual may be granted temporary national security eligibility
where official functions must be performed prior to completion of the national security investigation and
adjudication process. Within the DoD, temporary eligibility is also referred to as “interim eligibility” or “in-
terim.”
a.  Commanders in the minimum rank of O–5 are authorized to grant an interim eligibility. Granting in-
terims may be delegated in writing to command or agency designees in the minimum rank of O–4 or GS–
13.
(1)  Document interim eligibility in the DISS, whenever possible.
(2)  Notify the servicing HR office of the decision.
b.  Interim eligibility may be reciprocally accepted throughout the DoD and DA.
c.  An interim Secret or Confidential is valid for access to the level of eligibility granted. Access to re-
stricted data, communications security information, and NATO information is not authorized.
d.  An interim Top Secret is valid for access to Top Secret information. Access to restricted data, com-
munications security, and NATO information at the Secret and Confidential level is authorized if the indi-
vidual has final Secret national security eligibility.
e.  Interim SCI eligibility is granted by the CAS.
f.  Interim eligibility for contractor personnel under the NISP is governed by the Director, DCSA.
g.  Interim eligibility determinations and access are prohibited for National Security Agency/Central Se-
curity Service assignment, detail, or employment in accordance with DoDI 5210.45.
h.  Interim eligibility will be valid for up to 1 year.
(1)  A 6-month extension may be made by the commander if the background investigation has not been
completed due to deployment or the adjudication is pending at the CAS and the individual still requires
national security eligibility.
(2)  The security manager will notify the CAS of the extension in DISS.
(3)  Interim national security eligibility will be removed when derogatory reportable activities become
known or if a valid incident report is entered in DISS.
(4)  The CAS representative will update DISS to reflect the withdrawal of interim eligibility after 1 year
or after the expiration of an approved 6-month extension.
i.  The authority to request credit reports is limited to the ISP, the CAS, and the PSAB. Financial con-
cerns will be addressed through a review of the SF 86.
j.  In those instances where management has extended a tentative offer of employment to an individual
who is deemed ineligible for interim national security eligibility, the background investigation may continue
if management chooses to hold the final offer pending a final national security eligibility determination by

(a)  Acceptable proof of citizenship (see para 7–3k).
(b)  Favorable review of a completed SF 86.
(c)  Favorable review of local record checks (local personnel, base, military police, medical, and secu-

(d)  An appropriate national security investigation opened by the ISP.
(e)  Favorable review of FBI Criminal History Report (fingerprint results).
(2)  When a prior NACLC, ANACI, T3, or higher scope investigation exists that is less than 5 years old

(a)  There has not been a break in service greater than 24 months.
(b)  The required background investigation has been opened by the ISP.
(c)  A request is submitted to the CAS requesting a Secret national security eligibility be granted.
m.  Interim Top Secret national security eligibility.
(1)  When a valid need to access Top Secret information is established, an interim Top Secret national
security eligibility may be issued.
(2)  Favorable completion of all requirements cited for interim Confidential or Secret eligibility.
(3)  Favorable review of an Advance NAC or favorable NAC results completed, which must be inclusive
of a FBI fingerprint and name check.
(4)  When a prior valid T5, SSBI, SSBI PR, PPR, or equivalent investigation exists that is no more than
5 years old and it has not been adjudicated, an interim national security eligibility is not required if—
(a)  There has not been a break in service greater than 24 months.
(b)  The required background investigation has been opened by the ISP.
(c)  A request is submitted to the CAS requesting a Top Secret national security eligibility be granted.
n.  An interim is not authorized when an individual has a current denial or revocation. Prior to granting
an interim, security managers will coordinate with the CAS to verify if a loss of jurisdiction or no determi-
nation made was not a result of derogatory information.
7–4.  Administrative downgrading
a.  National security eligibility will not be administratively downgraded or withdrawn for any of the follow-
ing reasons:
(1)  A person has been assigned to duties that require access to a lower level of classified information.
(2)  A change in permanent duty station.
(3)  To avoid revocation in the face of reportable activities.
b.  Individuals with outdated investigations will not have their accesses administratively downgraded if
the required investigation has been submitted to the ASO for processing.

Section II
Granting Access to Classified Information
7–6.  General
a.  In accordance with DoDM 5200.02 and AR 380–5, access determinations are made solely on the
basis of the eligible individual’s need for access to classified information to perform official duties. Access
determinations (other than for SAPs) are not an adjudicative function relating to an individual’s suitability
for such access. Rather, they are decisions made by the commander indicating access is officially re-
quired.
b.  The CAS determines the level of eligibility based on the level of background investigation. The em-
ploying activity determines access level based on eligibility, need-to-know, and the requirements of the
position held. Before granting access to classified information, the individuals must sign appropriate non-
disclosure agreements if the DISS does not reflect the date of the previously signed nondisclosure agree-
ment.
c.  In accordance with AR 380–5, access is granted when an individual has the required national secu-
rity eligibility and need-to-know and signs SF 312 (Classified Information Nondisclosure Agreement).
When the provisions of AR 380–5 are met, access may be granted up to the level of the national security
eligibility, not to exceed the position designation.
(1)  Security managers will upload the SF 312 in DISS, add the date, and forward the original to the
supporting S1/HR office.
(2)  Security managers may grant access in DISS if there is a date of a previously signed SF 312 but
no uploaded document.
(3)  A new SF 312 is not authorized for the purposes of uploading a form to DISS.
d.  The access level of cleared individuals will be entered into the DISS by the supporting security man-
ager.
e.  The security manager will assume a servicing role in the DISS for all supported or assigned contrac-
tor personnel to receive notifications on eligibility changes.
f.  The Commander, U.S. Army Human Resources Command will establish an owning relationship of all
Individual Ready Reserves or individual mobilization augmentee personnel.
g.  No person is entitled to knowledge of, possession of, or access to classified information or perform
sensitive duties solely by virtue of office, position, grade, rank, or national security eligibility.
7–7.  Access by retired flag and general officers and senior executive service-equivalents
a.  See DoDM 5200.02 for access to classified information by retired flag and general officers and sen-
ior executive service-equivalents.
b.  Provide a courtesy copy of all waivers to the DCS, G–2 (DAMI–CD).
c.  SCI access may be granted under the provisions of DoDM 5105.21, Volume 3.
7–8.  One-time or short duration access
See DoDM 5200.02 for granting and managing one-time access.
7–9.  Special cases
When necessary, in the interests of national security, the DoD Component heads or their senior agency
official may authorize access to classified information by persons outside the Federal Government, as
prescribed in AR 380–5 and DoDM 5200.02.
