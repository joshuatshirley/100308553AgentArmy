Appendix E
Internal Control Evaluation
E–1.  Function
The function covered by this evaluation is the Army PSP.
E–2.  Purpose
The purpose of this evaluation is to assist commanders and organizations in evaluating key internal con-
trols listed. It is not intended to address all internal control elements.
E–3.  Instructions
Answers must be based upon the actual testing of key internal controls (for example, document analysis,
direct observation, sampling, and simulation). Answers that indicate deficiencies must be explained and
the corrective action indicated in the supporting documentation. These internal controls must be evalu-
ated at least once every 2 years. Certification that this evaluation has been conducted must be accom-
plished on DA Form 11–2 (Internal Control Evaluation Certification).
E–4.  Test questions
a.  Are security managers appointed in writing?
b.  Are duties delegated to security managers in writing?
c.  Is personally identifiable information protected in accordance with AR 25–22?
d.  Has a DISS account been established for security managers, are email addresses current, and are
owning or servicing relationships in the DISS reviewed or updated annually?
e.  Is the DCS, G–2 monitoring, evaluating, and reporting on the administration of the Army PSP?
f.  Did the commander establish written local security policies and procedures?
g.  Has the commander established a self-inspection security program for headquarters and subordi-
nate organizations?
h.  Are subordinate oversight visits conducted?
i.  Are funds allocated to implement the PSP?
j.  Does the security manager advise, update, and communicate with the commander to ensure matters
related to national security actions are presented for a final decision?
k.  Does the security manager adhere to deadlines and provide consultation to personnel who receive
an LOI or SOR on the seriousness of the action and provide support to such persons to ensure due pro-
cess is afforded?
l.  Are personnel submitted for reinvestigations in a timely manner?
m.  Do security managers receive formal training?
n.  Have supervisors ensured that subordinate personnel are trained in, understand, and follow require-
ments of this regulation, local command policy, and procedures concerning the PSP?
o.  Has the commander established an initial and annual security training for personnel having contin-
ued access to classified information in accordance with AR 380–5?
p.  Has the commander ensured background investigations are only initiated through the ASO on indi-
viduals that require a background investigation in accordance with their MOS or duty assignment, to in-
clude deployment and schools?
q.  Has the commander ensured prior to indoctrination that initial security briefings were conducted, ed-
ucating personnel on their security responsibilities?
r.  Has the commander ensured supervisors are familiar with special responsibilities regarding indica-
tors that may signal matters of personnel security concern and reinforce the requirements for self, super-
visor, and command reporting of security incidents via the DISS?
s.  Has the commander ensured personnel with national security eligibility report reportable activities to
the security office?
t.  Has the commander immediately documented in writing any derogatory reportable activities and the
access determination on DA Form 7873 and subsequently submitted an incident report via the DISS to
the CAS within 10 business days or within 3 business days for reportable activities for felony offenses,
sexual assaults, terrorism-related activity, and violent gang activity?
u.  Does the commander deny or suspend access to classified information?

v.  Do appointed personnel in the rank of O–6 or grade of GS–15 use the PDT to designate sensitive or
national security positions?
w.  Are all personnel entering or exiting the organization in- and out-processed in DISS within 14 calen-
dar days?
x.  Are gain and loss rosters requested and relationships updated in DISS within 14 days?
y.  Are individuals granted conditions or waivers monitored?
z.  Do security managers manage the PSP for contractor personnel not requiring access to classified
information?
aa.  Do security managers complete in and out-processing checklists and transfer reportable activities
from the losing unit to the gaining unit?
bb.  Do commanders ensure all national security personnel in the organization have a current and fa-
vorably adjudicated background investigation?
cc.  Do security managers have new personnel transferring from other DoD activities and provide all
changes since the last SF 86? Are these changes subsequently submitted to the CAS?
dd.  Does the commander or designee have close coordination between supervisors and security, HR,
medical, insider threat, law enforcement, CI, and legal communities to ensure that all pertinent infor-
mation available within a command is considered in the personnel security process?
ee.  Are LAA’s limited to those with a validated need?
ff.  Is an office designated in writing to use the PDT to determine if a military position requires a Top Se-
cret security clearance?
gg.  Does the commander document national security position designations with written justification and
retain locally for 5 years?
hh.  Does the security manager respond to requests from the CAS by the due date?
ii.  Are reportable activities and security records forwarded to the gaining command when personnel
transfer?
jj.  Are personnel debriefed in accordance AR 380–5 when access is no longer required?
kk.  Do security managers understand what AR 381–12 information is reportable to CI or have direct
contact with CI to determine if information should be reported to CI?
E–5.  Supersession
This evaluation replaces the evaluation previously published in AR 380–67, dated 24 January 2014.
