Chapter 9
Continuous Evaluation and Reporting Requirements
Section I
Evaluating Continued National Security Eligibility
9–1.  General
a.  A national security eligibility determination assesses whether an individual can be trusted to protect
national security. It is impossible to establish with certainty, based on an eligibility determination, that indi-
viduals will continue to behave in ways to retain such trust. Accordingly, a favorable national security eligi-
bility determination is but one facet of an effective PSP. There is the clear need to ensure that, after the
personnel security determination is reached, the individual’s trustworthiness is a matter of continuing as-
sessment. The responsibility for such assessment must be shared by the commander, the individual’s su-
pervisor, the security manager, coworkers, and to a large degree, the individual.
b.  Potential terrorist or CI concerns will be reported pursuant to the policy prescribed in AR 381–12.
c.  Commanders and security managers are not authorized to mitigate reportable activities nor are they
authorized to restrict reporting of information based on its source.

Section II
Continuous Evaluation Responsibilities
9–3.  Reportable activities by others
To ensure the protection of classified information or other information specifically prohibited by law from
disclosure, all covered individuals (individuals in national security positions and as defined in SEAD 3),
regardless of access to classified information, will report their knowledge of reportable activities regarding
other covered individuals to their designated security office (see app D). Reportable activities include—
a.  An unwillingness to comply with rules and regulations or to cooperate with security requirements.
b.  Unexplained affluence or excessive indebtedness.
c.  Alcohol abuse.
d.  Illegal use or misuse of drugs or drug activity.
e.  Apparent or suspected mental health issues where there is reason to believe it may impact the cov-
ered individual’s ability to protect classified information or other information specifically prohibited by law
from disclosure.
f.  Criminal conduct.
g.  Any activity that raises doubts regarding whether another covered individual’s continued national
security eligibility is clearly consistent with the interests of national security.
h.  Misuse of U.S. Government property or information systems.
i.  Concerning behavior or conduct that may be considered to constitute a disqualifying condition under
SEAD 4. Reports of concerning behavior or conduct must be based on credible information, not solely on
rumor, innuendo, or unsubstantiated hearsay (that is, statement of persons relating what someone else
may have heard or observed that have not been independently corroborated by other facts).

9–6.  Commander responsibilities
a.  Commanders will establish and maintain a program designed to evaluate on a continuing basis the
status of personnel under their jurisdiction with respect to national security eligibility.
(1)  This program should ensure close coordination between supervisors and security, HR, medical,
insider threat, law enforcement, CI, Inspector General, and legal communities to ensure that all pertinent
information available within a command is considered in the personnel security process. Commanders will
ensure reportable activities developed through HR, law enforcement, or judicial punishment are for-
warded to the supporting security manager.
(2)  Commanders will not use this program to avoid or restrict reporting reportable activities to the CAS.
Reporting of reportable activities is not discretionary.
b.  Commanders will establish a continuous personnel security training and awareness program to in-
form the workforce of requirements for obtaining and maintaining national security eligibility and their re-
sponsibilities for protection of classified information.
c.  Commanders will ensure personnel assigned to sensitive duties receive an initial security briefing
and annual refresher briefings on the national security implications of their duties and their individual re-
sponsibilities in accordance with AR 380–5. These briefings will emphasize the individual’s responsibility
to meet the standards and criteria for national security eligibility as stated in SEAD 4 and paragraphs 9–3
through 9–5. Training records are maintained for 5 years. Forward training records to the gaining unit.
d.  Commanders will ensure personnel in national security or sensitive positions are provided with infor-
mation about available programs (for example, employee assistance program) designed to help employ-
ees address questions or concerns regarding issues that may affect their ability to remain eligible for ac-
cess to classified information or assignment to sensitive positions.
(1)  Such initiatives will be designed in a way to avoid stigmas associated with seeking support. Com-
manders are encouraged to ensure all Soldiers, civilians, and contractor personnel assigned are aware
that seeking financial, marital, mental, behavioral, and other types of counseling is a positive step in sup-
porting continued national security eligibility.
(2)  These initiatives will identify potential concerns at an early stage so that any assistance rendered
by the employing activity will have a reasonable chance of avoiding long-term, job-related security prob-
lems.
e.  Commanders will ensure supervisory personnel are informed of their personnel security responsibili-
ties. Programs will include—
(1)  Training and continuous education on reportable activities.
(2)  Procedures for immediate reporting of reportable activities and potential subsequent due process
procedures.
