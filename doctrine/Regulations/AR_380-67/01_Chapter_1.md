Chapter 1
Introduction
Section I
General
1–1.  Purpose
This regulation establishes the Department of the Army (DA) Personnel Security Program (PSP) and im-
plements DoDM 5200.02. It sets forth the standards and criteria to determine investigation requirements;
submit background investigations; make interim and obtain final national security eligibility; adjudicate na-
tional security background investigations; grant access to classified information; report reportable activi-
ties; and provide due process when national security eligibility is suspended, revoked, or denied (adminis-
trative due process for contractor personnel is governed by DoDD 5220.6).
1–2.  References, forms, and explanation of abbreviations
See appendix A. The abbreviations, brevity codes, and acronyms (ABCAs) used in this electronic publica-
tion are defined when you hover over them. All ABCAs are listed in the ABCA database located at
https://armypubs.army.mil/.
1–3.  Associated publications
This section contains no entries.
1–4.  Responsibilities
Responsibilities are listed in section II of this chapter.
1–5.  Records management (recordkeeping) requirements
The records management requirement for all record numbers, associated forms, and reports required by
this publication are addressed in the Records Retention Schedule–Army (RRS–A). Detailed information
for all related record numbers, forms, and reports are located in Army Records Information Management
System (ARIMS)/RRS–A at https://www.arims.army.mil. If any record numbers, forms, and reports are not
current, addressed, and/or published correctly in ARIMS/RRS–A, see DA Pam 25–403 for guidance.
1–6.  Limitations
a.  Nothing in this regulation will be deemed to limit or affect the responsibility and powers of the Direc-
tor of National Intelligence (DNI), Principal Deputy DNI, or the Secretary of Defense, pursuant to Section
7532, Title 5, United States Code (5 USC 7532); Executive Order (EO) 12968; and Intelligence Commu-
nity Directive Number 704 revisions, updates, and supporting documents and relative Intelligence Com-
munity Policy Guidance.
b.  This regulation does not apply to—
(1)  Foreign contractors, international organizations, or designated representatives of foreign govern-
ments.
(2)  The Industrial Security Program (see AR 380–49 and Part 117, Title 32, Code of Federal Regula-
tions (32 CFR 117)).
(3)  Unfavorable personnel security action procedures pertaining to contractor personnel requiring col-
lateral access to classified information (see AR 380–49, 32 CFR 117, and DoDD 5220.6).
(4)  Suitability, Homeland Security Presidential Directive 12, or additional vetting.
(5)  Foreign national vetting.
1–7.  Waivers
The Deputy Chief of Staff (DCS), G–2 will review and act upon requests for waivers or exceptions to this
regulation as appropriate and as consistent with controlling law and policy. Commanders of Army com-
mands (ACOMs); Army service component commands (ASCCs); and direct reporting units (DRUs); and
Headquarters, Department of the Army (HQDA) principal officials and leads in which Army is the execu-
tive agent will submit such requests with supporting justification, including a formal review by the activity’s

Section II
Responsibilities
1–8.  Headquarters, Department of the Army principal officials
HQDA principal officials have the same responsibilities as commanders in paragraphs 1–17 and 1–19
and throughout the publication.
1–9.  Assistant Secretary of the Army (Manpower and Reserve Affairs)
In addition to the responsibilities in paragraphs 1–17 and 1–19, the ASA (M&RA) will—
a.  Provide guidance to and coordinate with the DCS, G–2 on the development of personnel security
policies and procedures.
b.  Supervise and coordinate the development of personnel security policy and the execution of Army
protection initiatives.
1–10.  General Counsel
The General Counsel will exercise the responsibility as directed by the Secretary of the Army to provide
legal counsel regarding the PSP.
1–11.  Chief Information Officer
The CIO will—
a.  Develop policy and procedures for authorizing access to Army information technology systems and
establish personnel security standards for information technology positions.
b.  Develop cybersecurity policy and procedures relating to personnel security in coordination with the
DCS, G–2 Director, Counterintelligence, Human Intelligence, Disclosure and Security (DAMI–CD).
1–12.  Deputy Chief of Staff, G–1
The DCS, G–1 will—
a.  In coordination with the DCS, G–2, advise and assist in the development and issuance of policy per-
taining to designating civilian position sensitivity in accordance with the Position Designation Tool (PDT).
b.  Ensure the submission of background investigations for new hire DA Civilians (see paras 3–8
through 3–10).
1–13.  Deputy Chief of Staff, G–2
The DCS, G–2 is the principal military advisor to the Secretary of the Army and Chief of Staff of the Army
on intelligence and counterintelligence (CI). The DCS, G–2 is the Army’s senior agency official for person-
nel security. The DCS, G–2 will—
a.  Provide overall program management and oversight of the DA PSP in accordance with DoDM
5200.02.
b.  Serve as the head of an Intelligence Community element in accordance with DoDM 5105.21, Vol-
ume 1.
c.  Provide PSP program management through issuance of policy and operating guidance.
d.  Validate PSP requirements, including allocating for background investigations, pursuant to the pro-
gram objective memorandum.
e.  Assist commanders to address PSP policy and operational concerns.
f.  Designate the president of the Army Personnel Security Appeal Board (PSAB) (see app E).
g.  Provide guidance, direction, and oversight necessary to ensure the PSP is administered effectively.
h.  Establish and maintain an annual self-inspection program to evaluate and assess the effectiveness
and efficiency of Department of Defense (DoD) PSP implementation.
i.  Submit incident reports, as necessary, when individuals fail to report reportable activities.
j.  Direct the suspension of access to classified information and sensitive duties, as necessary.

k.  Maintain a Privacy Act System of Record Notice (SORN) that describes the DA’s collection, use,
and maintenance of records obtained through the administration, management, accountability, and over-
sight of security functions.
l.  The Commander, U.S. Army Intelligence and Security Command (INSCOM) will—
(1)  Establish and maintain the Army Security Office (ASO).
(2)  Review or process requests for background investigations to the authorized investigative service
provider (ISP) for all Army activities, in support of national security eligibility determinations; suitability for
Federal employment and physical and logical access; or other vetting requirements.
(3)  Ensure completeness of background investigation questionnaires prior to releasing the request to
the ISP.
(4)  Validate if a prior investigation exists that meets current investigative requirements.
(5)  Receive, monitor, and analyze derogatory reportable activities from multiple sources, including ISP,
law enforcement, CI, CE, reports of investigations, and insider threats.
(6)  Forward derogatory reportable activities to the Army Insider Threat Hub in accordance with Army
Insider Threat Program reporting requirements and criteria.
(7)  Provide reporting and response requirements training to commanders and security managers.
(8)  Execute the responsibilities in paragraph 9–8.
1–14.  Chief, National Guard Bureau
The Director, Army National Guard, if directed by the Chief, NGB, will ensure owning relationships are es-
tablished in DISS until Soldiers reach their first duty station.
1–15.  The Judge Advocate General
a.  TJAG will ensure that servicing SJA/JAs and other legal advisors provide commanders and Army
personnel with advice and counsel on law and policy governing the personnel security program.
b.  TJAG provides the legal advisor to the PSAB.
1–16.  Provost Marshal General
The PMG will—
a.  Ensure information contained in military police records is released under the provisions of AR 25–22
to authorized personnel, to include the ASO, for valid background check and CE purposes.
b.  Ensure arrests and apprehensions are reported, via a law enforcement report, to the appropriate
commander and the local security manager.
1–17.  Commanders of Army commands, Army service component commands, and direct
reporting units
Commanders of ACOMs, ASCCs, and DRUs will—
a.  Establish a PSP to execute procedures and processes in accordance with this regulation.
b.  Ensure the proper allocation of funds (excluding background investigations) to implement the PSP.
c.  Establish and update standard operating procedures for personnel security.
d.  Appoint in writing a command personnel security manager who will be responsible for overall per-
sonnel security management and the development of command, agency, and staff procedures for person-
nel security.
(1)  Command personnel security managers will assist in program implementation, maintenance, and
local oversight to ensure personnel adhere to program requirements.
(2)  The command personnel security manager will have direct access to the commander and will be
organizationally aligned to oversee prompt compliance with PSP requirements.
(3)  Appointment letters will be provided to the DCS, G–2 (DAMI–CD) within 7 days of appointment.
(4)  Ensure security managers are trained in accordance with chapter 10 and consistent with assigned
duties and this regulation.
(5)  The personnel security manager, on behalf of the commanding general (CG), will serve as the De-
fense Information System for Security (DISS) (or current system of record hereafter DISS) hierarchy and
account manager for subordinate activities.
e.  Budget for and conduct oversight visits of subordinate activities at least once every 24 months.
f.  Implement in- and out- processing procedures, to include signing of checklists, for all military, civil-
ian, and contractor personnel in the command.

1–19.  Commanders and Civilian Directors
Commanders (O–5 and above) and civilian directors of organizations/activities will —
a.  Ensure reporting of reportable activities is consistent with the criteria in paragraphs 9–3 through 9–5
and Security Executive Agent Directive (SEAD) 4 to the Defense Counterintelligence and Security
Agency (DCSA) Consolidated Adjudication Services (CAS) (see paras 9–2 and 9–13).
b.  Submit national security background investigation requests for contractor personnel not requiring
access to classified information, according to the terms of the contract or performance work statement.
c.  Grant interim national security eligibility (up to Top Secret) and deny or suspend access to classified
information following procedures in paragraph 7–3. This responsibility may be delegated in writing to
command or agency designees in the minimum rank of O–4 or general schedule (GS)-13 or equivalent.
d.  Monitor individuals granted national security eligibility based on conditions and waivers and report
the results to the CAS semi-annually or as required by the CAS, until the conditions are removed.
e.  Ensure individuals who lack favorable national security eligibility (that is, denial, revocation, no de-
termination made, loss of jurisdiction, or administrative withdrawal) do not remain in national security posi-
tions, do not have access to classified information, and do not perform sensitive duties.
f.  Submit incident reports and make access determinations when an individual fails to report reportable
activities as required.
g.  Through the appointed security manager, G–2, or S–2—
(1)  Initiate requests for background investigations through the ASO.
(2)  Request national security eligibility.
(3)  Act as the command’s liaison officer to the CAS for all matters pertaining to the granting, denial, or
revocation of a national security eligibility for individuals assigned to the command.
(4)  Provide procedural assistance to individuals completing responses to letters of intent (LOIs), state-
ments of reasons (SORs), appeals, and other requests for information received from the CAS or the
PSAB. Review packages for completeness prior to submission.
(5)  Assist hiring managers in their determination of the correct position designation, in coordination
with the supporting human resources (HR) office.
(6)  Maintain records containing all documents relative to requests for background investigations, ac-
cess determinations, justifications for national security position upgrades, granting interim national secu-
rity eligibility, incident reports, and so forth in accordance with paragraph 5–13. Transfer records of de-
parting personnel to the gaining organization or unit.
(7)  Conduct briefings and debriefings in accordance with chapter 10.
(8)  Complete in- and out-processing checklists for Soldiers for all personnel (military, civilian, and con-
tractors).
(9)  Report reportable activities to the CAS in accordance with chapter 9.
(10)  Respond to all CAS and PSAB requests within the given timeline.
(11)  Check DISS tasks daily and complete and close all CAS requests for action by the suspense date.
(12)  Create and maintain records in DISS for contractor personnel not requiring access to classified
information.
