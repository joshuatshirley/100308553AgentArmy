Section III
Reports and Inspections
1–20.  Reports
Information contained in reports is essential for basic PSP management and for responding to requests
from the Secretary of Defense and the United States Congress.
a.  Primary sources of PSP management data are contained in the DISS and DCSA databases. The
data includes the number of personnel holding national security eligibility within the DA; the number of na-
tional security eligibility determinations issued, denied, or revoked each fiscal year; the number of back-
ground investigations conducted each year; and other program management data, as applicable.
b.  The PSAB president or designee will provide a quarterly report to the DCS, G–2 Director, DAMI–CD
detailing—
(1)  The number of appeals heard, categorized by associated adjudicative concern.
(2)  The number of appeals sustained.
(3)  The number of appeals overturned.
(4)  The demographics of appellants, categorized by military (that is, enlisted and officer), civilian, and
contractor status.
1–21.  Inspections
Commanders will ensure that the PSP is a component of the Command Assessment Program and is in-
cluded in command inspection programs.
Chapter 2
National Security Investigations
Section I
Types and Scope of Personnel Security Investigations
2–1.  General
a.  Personnel assigned to national security positions will be subject to an investigation to determine
whether they are and will remain reliable, trustworthy, of good conduct and character, and loyal to the
United States, and whether granting or continuing national security eligibility is clearly consistent with the
national interest.
b.  National security position and duty requirements or civilian position sensitivity will not be down-
graded to facilitate recruitment or retention of personnel or to accommodate adverse security determina-
tions.
c.  Investigative requests will not be submitted for higher than what has been designated for the posi-
tion or required for the duty to be performed.
d.  Limited access authorization (LAA) procedures (see para 3–18) apply to non-U.S. citizens who re-
quire access to classified information.
2–2.  Federal Investigative Standards
The Federal Investigative Standards provide consistent standards for investigations to facilitate reciprocity
across the Government and ensure cost-effective and efficient protection of national interests. The stand-
ards apply to investigations used to determine eligibility for access to classified information, to hold a na-
tional security position, for physical and logical access, and for suitability for Government employment.
The Federal Investigative Standards established a new tier and naming system for background investiga-
tions (see app B). The scope of a background investigation may be neither raised nor lowered without the
approval of the DNI.

2–3.  Tier 3
Investigations conducted to this standard are for positions designated as non-critical sensitive or requiring
eligibility for L access (Department of Energy-specific access) or access to Confidential or Secret infor-
mation. This is the lowest level of investigation acceptable for access to classified information, using SF
86 (Questionnaire for National Security Positions) or its successor form. Tier 3 (T3) replaces the Access
National Agency Check and Inquiries (ANACI) and National Agency Check with Law and Credit Check
(NACLC). The reinvestigation is T3 reinvestigation.
2–4.  Tier 5
Investigations conducted to this standard are for positions designated as critical sensitive, special sensi-
tive, or requiring eligibility for Q access (Department of Energy-specific access) or access to Top Secret
or Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI), using the SF 86 or its successor form. Tier 5 (T5) re-
places the Single Scope Background Investigation (SSBI). The reinvestigations, Single Scope Back-
ground Investigation Periodic Reinvestigation (SSBI PR) and Phased Periodic Reinvestigation (PPR), are
replaced with the T5 reinvestigation.
2–5.  Other background investigation types
Tier 1, 2, and 4 background investigations for suitability or credentialing do not fall under the authority of
this regulation.
2–6.  Fingerprint Special Agreement Check
The Fingerprint Special Agreement Check (SAC) is an integral part of all initial investigations and is con-
ducted in conjunction with the submission of all initial background investigations and some reinvestiga-
tions. Fingerprint SACs will be electronically captured and submitted.
2–7.  Reimbursable Suitability/Security Investigation
a.  A Reimbursable Suitability/Security Investigation (RSI) is a background investigation requested by
the CAS to prove or disprove allegations that have risen concerning an individual in a national security
position.
b.  RSIs are scoped as necessary to address the specific matters requiring resolution in the case con-
cerned and generally consist of record checks or interviews with persons potentially knowledgeable of the
issues. An RSI may include an interview with the subject of the investigation, when necessary, to resolve
conflicting information or to provide an opportunity to refute or mitigate adverse information.
2–8.  Reopen
a.  Previously closed investigations are reopened to complete a discontinued investigation. Discontin-
ued cases may only be reopened within 4 months (120 days) of discontinuance.
b.  Reopens are also requested if a background investigation closed without a subject interview due to
deployment. Request the reopen within 12 months of the closure; otherwise, an RSI is required. Do not
request the reopen until the subject returns and is available for the subject interview.
2–9.  Spouse Special Agreement Checks
See DoDM 5105.21, Volume 3, for the submission of Spouse SACs on individuals with SCI access who
marry or intend to marry a foreign national.
2–10.  National Agency Check
a.  The National Agency Check (NAC) is an integral element of all background investigations. The NAC
is a records check of databases that includes the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) fingerprint check,
FBI name check, a list of previous investigations recorded in DCSA Security/Suitability Investigations In-
dex, a Defense Central Index of Investigations (DCII) check, and a credit check. Checks such as Selec-
tive Service, military personnel records, and State Department Passport Office are scheduled when cer-
tain conditions are met.
b.  The Advance NAC consists of an itemized list (generally FBI fingerprint check, FBI name, DCSA Se-
curity/Suitability Investigations Index, and DCII checks) of the NAC results and search status.

Section II
Investigative Requirements for National Security Positions
2–12.  General
a.  If an individual has a current investigation that meets or exceeds the required investigation type for
access to classified information and a final national security eligibility has not been granted, a request for
adjudication will be made to the CAS via the DISS.
b.  National security eligibility granted under less stringent or older investigative requirements retains its
validity; however, if a higher level of national security eligibility is required, a new investigation is required
(see apps B and C).
c.  Non-U.S. citizens may be granted favorable eligibility to hold a sensitive position. Non-U.S. citizens
are not granted national security eligibility. See paragraph 3–18 for access to classified information by
non-U.S. citizens.
2–13.  Investigation requirements for final national security eligibility
a.  Background investigations for Top Secret or Sensitive Compartmented Information and critical and
special sensitive positions.
(1)  Final national security eligibility requires a T5 completed within the last 5 years. If the investigation
is greater than 5 years old from date of completion and continued eligibility is required, the individual must
submit a reinvestigation (see para 2–14).
(2)  The former SSBI PR and PPR meet the requirement for final Top Secret national security eligibility.
b.  Background investigations for Confidential and Secret, non-critical sensitive positions.
(1)  A T3 or higher level background investigation must have been completed within the last 5 years
without a break in service of more than 24 months. If the investigation is greater than 5 years old from
date of completion and continued eligibility is required, the individual must submit a reinvestigation (see
para 2–14).
(2)  A T3 is required for civilians even if the individual held valid national security eligibility based on a
National Agency Check with Law and Credit (NACLC) during prior military or contractor employment.
(3)  The former ANACI meets the requirement for civilians requiring Secret national security eligibility.
(4)  The former NACLC meets the requirement for military and contractor personnel who require Secret
national security eligibility. The former NACLC reinvestigation conducted on a civilian meets the require-
ment for national security eligibility for civilians.
c.  Individuals with a break in service over 24 months.  Individuals with a break in service over 24
months require a new (initial) background investigation.
Section III
Reinvestigations
2–14.  General
a.  In accordance with the Federal Investigative Standards, reinvestigations may be performed at any
time after national security eligibility has been granted. Additionally, Army employees in national security
positions and contractor personnel performing national security duties without access to classified infor-
mation will be subject to reinvestigation on a recurring basis, as described in this section. Submission of
out-of-cycle reinvestigations must be justified and approved by the DCS, G–2 (DAMI–CD) prior to submis-
sion to the ASO.
b.  Each military, civilian, and contractor occupying a special sensitive or critical sensitive position or
requiring continued national security eligibility at an equivalent level will undergo a T5 reinvestigation
every 5 years from the close date of the previous investigation.
c.  The T3 reinvestigation is the reinvestigation required for military, civilian, and contractor personnel
requiring continued eligibility for access to classified information up to Secret or occupation of a noncriti-
cal sensitive position. Reinvestigations are initiated every 5 years from the closed date of the previous in-
vestigation.
