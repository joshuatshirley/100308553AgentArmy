Chapter 3
Enlisted Assignment System
Section I
Managing Enlisted Assignments
3–1.  Overview
The primary goal of the enlisted personnel assignment system is to satisfy the personnel requirements of
the Army. The secondary goals are to—
a.  Professionally develop Soldiers.
b.  Maximize dwell time.
c.  Meet Soldiers' personal desires.
3–2.  Assignment selection criteria
Except for CONUS requirements that will be filled from OCONUS returnees who are IA, the primary con-
siderations in reassigning a Soldier will be the Soldier's current qualifications and ability to fill a valid re-
quirement. A secondary consideration will be achieving co-location of dual military couples. Other fac-
tors, such as availability, volunteer status, and TOS, spouse employment, spouse, and child educational
activities, volunteer activities, and so forth, may be considered when in line with mission requirements
and Servicemember career development.
a.  See AR 614–30 for policy on assignment to Germany of Soldiers who are German aliens and as-
signment to Türkiye of Soldiers who are Türkish aliens or Dual U.S./Türkish citizens.
b.  See AR 614–30 for policy on assignment of Soldiers who are former members of the Peace Corps.
c.  Soldiers released from civil or military prisons being restored to duty will not be assigned to the for-
eign country or area where the offense was committed.
d.  Former officers or warrant officers reverting to enlisted status will be reassigned to another installa-
tion. They will be reported IA. Exceptions may be granted when—
(1)  The Soldier requests to remain at the original installation.
(2)  The commander concurs.
(3)  There is a valid vacancy.
(4)  No higher priority requirement dictates a reassignment.
e.  Promotion of Soldiers to master sergeant (MSG) and below will not be the sole reason for a PCS.
However, Soldiers selected for promotion will be considered for assignments in the grade selected for
promotion.
f.  A sole surviving son or daughter will be exempt from assignment to a hostile fire area (HFA) un-less
the Soldier waives the designation (see para 5–7).
g.  Enlisted Soldiers will be assigned to positions in accordance with applicable DA and DoD policy.
h.  Prior to departure in compliance with AI, Soldiers must take action to meet any remaining service
requirement (see AR 601–280).
i.  Soldiers will normally receive a reporting date no less than 150 days beyond the date AI are issued
by HRC.
j.  Equal assignment opportunities are as follows:
(1)  Assignments will be made for all Soldiers without regard to their color, race, religious preference,
ethnic background, national origin, age, marital status, sexual orientation, or sex, consistent with require-
ments for physical capabilities and with DA, DoD policy, and applicable law. (This applies equally to both
PCS and temporary duty (TDY) assignment actions). Exceptions to this policy include:
(a)  Marital status for military couples.
(b)  Marital status when the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Manpower and Reserve Affairs (ASD
(M&RA)), with the concurrence of the General Counsel of the Department of Defense, determines on a
case-by-case basis and for reasons of national security that marital status is an essential assignment
qualification for particular military billets or positions.
(c)  Sex in cases approved by the Secretary of Defense. This authority will not be delegated.
(d)  Cases where status of forces agreement (SOFA) protections are not extended to same sex
spouses.
(e)  The religious preference of chaplains.

(2)  Personnel decisions, including those related to the assignments of Soldiers, will not be influenced
by the employment, educational activities, volunteer activities, or health of a Soldier’s family member, with
the following exceptions:
(a)  When necessary to ameliorate the personal hardship of a Soldier or family member because a fam-

1315.19 and DoDI 1342.12.
(b)  When otherwise required by law in instances in which a prohibited conflict of interest may exist be-

(3)  Individual cases approved on a case-by-case basis.
k.  Restrictions on assignments OCONUS prior to completion of basic training (BT) are contained in AR
614–30. Limitations on the number of moves for first-term Soldiers serving initial enlistments of—
(1)  Three years or less will be given no more than one assignment before their expiration term of ser-
vice (ETS) following basic and initial skills training, unless required to serve in a dependent-restricted tour
area overseas, in which case such Soldiers will be given no more than two assignments in different loca-
tions.
(2)  More than 3, but no more than 4 years, will be given no more than one CONUS assignment be-fore
their ETS following initial BT and skill training. If overseas assignment is required, the Soldier will be given
no more than two assignments before ETS.
(3)  More than four, but no more than 5 years, will be given no more than two assignments in different
locations before their ETS following initial BT and skill training, regardless of tour length.
(4)  More than 5 years will be given no more than two assignments in different locations, unless re-
quired to serve an overseas assignment. In that case, no more than three assignments before their ETS
following initial BT and skill training, regardless of tour length.
l.  The following reassignments of first-term Soldiers are not subject to the limitations listed above:
(1)  Reassignment to a different duty station to or from training based on the needs of the Army.
(2)  Reassignments under the MACP.
(3)  Reassignments under the EFMP or for compassionate reasons.
(4)  Reassignment of Soldiers disqualified for duty as a result of denial or revocation of security clear-
ance, professional certification, nuclear certification, or medical qualification, or relief from duty for cause.
(5)  Reassignment to or from patient or prisoner status.
(6)  Reassignment of Soldiers determined as excess due to unit inactivation or deactivation; base rea-
lignment and closure; organization or staffing changes; reclassification or other actions changing the oc-
cupational specialty or skill designator of the Soldier.
(7)  Reassignment of Soldiers via expedited transfer as authorized by DoDI 6495.02, Volume 1.
m.  Unit moves will not require Armywide equitable assignment policy.
n.  Concurrent assignment of Soldiers of the same immediate Family to the same military unit is not
prohibited. Good order and discipline will be considered in determining whether members of the same
Family are to be assigned to the same unit. Requests for reassignment to a different unit may be ap-
proved for all but one Soldier of the same Family and will be based on military requirements.
o.  Married Army couples and single Soldiers are eligible for duty worldwide and all assignments for
which they qualify, including assignments to imminent-danger or HFAs. Single Soldiers and military cou-
ples with dependents will have a current and viable Family care plan completed in accordance with AR
600–20. If the Soldier cannot or will not develop an adequate Family care plan, the commander will pro-
cess the Soldier for separation in accordance with AR 635–200.
p.  Refer to AR 614–30 for overseas assignment restrictions of birth parent Soldiers with newborns and
military couples or single parent Soldiers in the process of adopting a child.

3–3.  Assignment preferences
a.  Soldiers’ CONUS area of preference and overseas area of preference are considered in the assign-
ment process; however, assignments are made to fulfill Army requirements.
b.  When selecting a preference, Soldiers should choose installations or geographic areas where their
PMOS, SQIs, and additional skill identifiers (ASIs) are required.
3–4.  The Enlisted Assignment System of Record
a.  The Enlisted Assignment System of Record is the principal automated source for information con-
cerning enlisted assignment actions until subsumed by future personnel systems of record. It is an inter-
active online system for enlisted assignments and distribution functions.
b.  Detailed instructions and specifics on the Enlisted Assignment System of Record are available in the
appropriate user’s manual.
3–5.  Enlisted distribution target model
a.  The enlisted distribution target model (EDTM) is an automated system that creates enlisted distribu-
tion targets by MOS, grade, and unit identification code (UIC). The model fills each UIC reflected in the
personnel manning authorization document with projected available inventory from the MOS Level Sys-
tem according to the DCS, G–1 distribution policy.

b.  The EDTM constrains the assignment process to coincide with the projected operating strength tar-
gets. It represents assets the Army realistically expects to be available for distribution. The model targets
each UIC for fill according to the DCS, G–1 enlisted distribution policy. Therefore, the possibility exists
(depending on the fill priority and projected inventory) for a unit to be targeted at less than authorized
strength.
c.  Field personnel managers may view the targets—grouped by private through specialist (SPC), ser-
geant (SGT) through MSG, and SGM for the current month plus 4 through 12 months (CM+4 through
CM+12).
d.  The targets are produced monthly or more frequently if changes to projected authorizations or oper-
ating strength warrant an update. (Questions on the EDTM may be referred to the Chief, Modeling and
Requirement Branch, HRC (AHRC–FSR–M), 1600 Spearhead Division Avenue, Fort Knox, KY 40122-
5302.)
3–6.  Requisitions/job openings
a.  The allocation of requisitions for each of the requisitioning activities listed below will be limited to the
command's share of available inventory, determined by the EDTM. Requisitions are allocated to the field
via the Enlisted Assignment System of Record at the 4-character military occupational specialty code
(MOSC) level. Requisitioning activities then expand the allocations to the 9-character MOSC level, adding
security requirements, other details, and special instructions, if necessary, and transmit them to the HRC
by verifying requisitions in the Enlisted Assignment System of Record. The requisitioning activities are as
follows:
(1)  CONUS installations.
(2)  CONUS elements of functional commands.
(3)  U.S. Army Europe and Africa; Eighth U.S. Army; U.S. Army Pacific; and U.S. Army South
(USARSO).
(4)  MEDCOM.
(5)  U.S. Army Special Operations Command (USASOC).
b.  All other requisitioning activities will determine their 9-character MOSC level requirements and any
special instructions and transmit them by creating requisitions in the Enlisted Assignment System of Rec-
ord. If no requisitions are received from a requisitioning activity during the month, the assumption is that
no requirement exists.
c.  The Enlisted Assignment System of Record will generate the control and serial number when the
requisition is created. (See the Enlisted Assignment System of Record user’s manual for details.)
d.  The lead time required in submitting personnel requisitions provides time for selecting and preparing
Soldiers for movement. Actual lead times necessary for each ACOM, ASCC, or DRU are established dur-
ing the requisition allocation process or via direct communication between the HRC and the supported
command. (Questions on this process may be referred to the Chief, RD, FSD, HRC (AHRC–FSR), 1600
Spearhead Division Avenue, Fort Knox, KY 40122-5302, or usarmy.knox.hrc.mbx.epmd-readiness-
branch@army.mil).
3–7.  Stabilization of tours, assignment eligibility, and availability codes
a.  A request for individual stabilization, to include extensions, may be disapproved by any command-er
in the chain of command without referral to HRC.
b.  Assignment of personnel to positions requiring stabilization usually will be made by HQDA-directed
AI.
c.  Only Soldiers in authorized positions may be stabilized. Over-strength or excess personnel (or per-
sonnel not working in their PMOS) may not be stabilized except in the case of compassionate stabiliza-
tions.
d.  Personnel assigned to organizations or positions for which HQDA has approved stabilized tours nor-
mally will not be reassigned before completion of the stabilized tour. However, reassignment can occur if
they—
(1)  Have been granted HQDA approval of a request for compassionate reassignment.
(2)  Become disqualified to serve in the assignment; or are relieved for cause from the assignment.
(3)  Are selected by HQDA to fill high priority assignments or when HQDA has determined it is in the
best interest of the Service or the individual Soldier.
(4)  Become surplus to the needs of the organization.

Section II
Personnel Utilization
3–8.  Objectives
a.  To ensure efficient use of enlisted Soldiers in accomplishing the Army mission.
b.  To place Soldiers in positions that require skills, knowledge, and abilities as shown by their PMOS,
SMOS, or additional MOS (AMOS).
c.  To provide policies on personnel utilization that will strengthen and broaden MOS qualifications and
prepare Soldiers for career progression, greater responsibility, and diversity of assignment.
3–9.  Criteria
Soldiers will be assigned to an organizational element according to the authorized MTOE/TDA/Duty mili-
tary occupational specialty positions, consistent with their PMOS or CPMOS qualifications, and meet the
required criteria.
a.  The criteria for SIMOS are found in paragraph 3–12.
b.  The criteria for bonus recipients are found in AR 601–280.
c.  The criteria for first sergeant (1SG) positions are available in chapter 8, section IV.
d.  Soldiers with school-trained SQI or ASI will be assigned in the SQI or ASI for the minimum SRR for
the respective course. An SQI or ASI is not a substitute for an MOS and will not represent the sole skill
requirement for any position.

e.  Defense Language Institute graduates will be utilized in positions authorized foreign language abil-
ity. (Exceptions must be approved by the HRC.)
f.  Non-bonus first-term Soldiers will serve in their PMOS during their first-term of service. When exi-
gencies dictate, commanders will assure PMOS utilization through the first 12 months after completion of
advanced individual training (AIT). Commanders then may award SMOS or AMOS as appropriate. Only

g.  Intelligence career program Soldiers will be assigned in their PMOS only, unless approved by the
Chief, Military Intelligence Branch, HRC (AHRC–TAF–MI).
h.  Soldiers with PMOS 42R or 42S (band personnel) will be assigned in their PMOS only, unless ap-
proved by the Chief, Soldier Support Branch, HRC (AHRC–TAS–SSB).
i.  Soldiers in the grades of SGT through SGM or CSM will be assigned to fill MOS and grade authori-
zations in MTOE troop units before filling TDA staff positions. Commanders with classification authority
can authorize exceptions to meet essential local needs.
j.  Assign Soldiers within the same grade or up to two grades higher if no higher-ranking Soldiers are
available. Utilization within the same or higher skill level should be considered. The higher skill level will
include any other MOS to which the Soldier would advance in the normal line of progression in the MOS
career pattern set forth in DA Pam 611–21.

Section III

3–19.  General
In accordance with AR 190–45, provisions of 34 USC 20901 et seq., DoDI 1325.07, and AR 27–10 mili-
tary sex offenders are required to register with the installation provost marshal, with state or territorial sex
offender registration officials, and with local law enforcement officials of the jurisdiction in which the sex
offender will reside, work, or attend school upon leaving confinement, or upon conviction if not con-fined.
Military sex offenders include Soldiers who have been convicted of covered offenses under the UCMJ;
Soldiers who are required to register as sex offenders under state or territorial law; and Soldiers who
have been convicted by foreign governments of offenses equivalent to or closely analogous to the cov-
ered offenses under the UCMJ. The CG, HRC, will ensure the assignment management system supports
the Soldier in meeting this requirement. An assignment consideration (ASCO) code will be assigned to
Soldiers who have qualifying courts-martial or civilian court convictions that require them to register as a
military sex offender.

a.  Commanders will ensure that Soldiers convicted of a sexual offense are not assigned or deployed
on a TDY, temporary change of station (TCS), or PCS status to duty stations in a foreign country. The
only permitted OCONUS locations are Hawaii, Alaska, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, or territories or
possessions of the U.S.
b.  Soldiers currently serving in any unauthorized OCONUS locations who have been convicted of an

ized OCONUS location in any status (TDY or TCS) will immediately be returned to their parent organiza-

separation cannot be finalized during the period of confinement, reassignment takes precedence upon
completion of confinement, as opposed to the Soldier remaining in the non-permitted location for the pur-
pose of finalizing separation.
d.  Affected RC Soldiers, other than AGR Soldiers, whose domicile and unit of assignment are located

assignment, except where travel is necessary as part of the separation process.

CMFs, or SQIs listed in table 3–3. Military sex offenders currently utilized in those assignments or posi-

pational specialties, CMFs, or SQIs will be immediately reclassified.
f.  Soldiers currently serving in any prohibited occupational specialty CMF or assignment who have
been convicted of a qualified offense are ineligible for continued duty in that CMF or assignment. If appli-

dinate with HRC for reclassification and reassignment.
