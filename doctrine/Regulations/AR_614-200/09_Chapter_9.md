Chapter 9
Miscellaneous-Type Assignments
Section I
Home Base and Advance Assignment Program
9–1.  Scope
a. The practice of returning Soldiers to their previous permanent duty station or a different location af-
ter completing a dependent-restricted short tour is referred to as the Home Base and Advance Assign-
ment Program. The two types of assignments in the Home Base and Advance Assignment Program are—
(1) Home base assignment. Soldiers are projected to return to the installation where they were sta-
tioned prior to completing a dependent-restricted 12-month OCONUS short tour.
(2) Advance assignment. Soldiers are projected to return to a different installation than they were sta-
tioned prior to completing a dependent-restricted 12-month OCONUS short tour.
b. Participation in the Home Base and Advance Assignment Program is optional. Soldiers who de-cline
to participate will indicate so during their reassignment briefing, and the Soldier’s desire not to participate
in the Home Base and Advance Assignment Program will be indicated in their PCS orders. The servicing
MPD Chief must send an encrypted email message, with attachment showing the declination, to the
Chief, Strategic Readiness Branch (AHRC–FSR–S), FSD, in order to update the declination in the En-
listed Assignment System of Record. Upon completion of the dependent-restricted short tour, Soldiers
who declined participation in the Home Base and Advance Assignment Program will be reassigned ac-
cording to the needs of the Army.
9–2.  Criteria
a. RA Soldiers who are SPC on a second or subsequent enlistment, SPC (P), or CPL through MSG,
are eligible to participate in the Home Base and Advance Assignment Program when they are issued AI
to a dependent-restricted 12-month OCONUS short tour area. Soldiers are eligible to participate in the
program regardless of whether or not they have dependents.
b. Primary determining factors for the Home Base and Advance Assignment Program are—
(1) The needs of the Army.
(2) Assignment preference or volunteer location of the Soldier.
(3) Professional development considerations.
(4) Least cost factors.
c. Hawaii and Alaska residents may be provided a home base or advance assignment to their respec-
tive states. All others will not be given a home base or advance assignment in Hawaii or Alaska without
their consent.
d. Eligible Soldiers stationed in an OCONUS-accompanied long tour who volunteer for an inter-theater
transfer will be notified in advance of their projected home base or advance assignment.
e. The intent of the Home Base and Advance Assignment Program is to reduce PCS costs and to in-
crease stability for the Soldier and his or her Family.
(1) Soldiers who receive a home base assignment under the Home Base and Advance Assignment
Program must sign a statement indicating that they understand the intent of the program and that they are
expected not to use their dependent PCS entitlements unless the assignment is cancelled.
(2) Soldiers who receive a Home Base and Advance Assignment Program advance assignment to a
different installation will sign a statement indicating that they understand the intent of the program and are
expected not to use their dependent travel and transportation allowances except to the locale of the ad-
vance assignment.
(3) Single Soldiers with minor dependents may participate in the home-basing or follow-on assignment
programs with no HAAP restriction on dependent travel and transportation allowances, shipment of HHG,
non-temporary storage of HHG, or with-dependent dislocation allowance, if otherwise eligible, in support

Section II
Assignment of Choice for Department of the Army Noncommissioned Officer or Soldier of the
Year
9–3.  Objective
To provide an incentive for improved career management, a DA NCO or Soldier of the Year will be given
choice of available assignments.
9–4.  Criteria
a.  Soldiers selected as DA NCOs or Soldiers of the Year will be given their choice of assignment, pro-
vided valid requisitions exist, and they meet applicable statutory and regulatory provisions. When Soldiers
have received AI prior to being selected, and the AI are not to their choice of location, the AI will be de-
leted.
b.  Every reasonable effort will be made to assist Soldiers in obtaining their selected choice of assign-
ment.
Section III
Special Assignments
9–5.  Scope
Special assignments include special management command and joint, HQDA, and HRC assignments
that have special missions and require personnel with specific qualifications. Soldiers assigned to these
positions will be stabilized for a period of 36 months. However, the CG, HRC may reassign Soldiers with
less than 36 months in accordance with paragraph 3–7k(1).
a.  International and OCONUS Joint HQ, U.S. military missions, military assistance advisory groups,
Joint U.S. military advisory groups, and similar activities.
b.  The U.S. Central Command.
c.  HQDA and HRC.
d.  The U.S. Disciplinary Barracks.
e.  The U.S. Army Intelligence and Security Command.
f.  Office of the Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence and field activities.
g.  Defense Courier Service.
h.  The U.S. Criminal Investigation Division.
i.  The U.S. Transportation Command.

9–6.  General selection criteria
Soldiers may volunteer or be nominated provided the following criteria are met:
a.  Be a high school graduate or GED equivalent.
b.  Be a U.S. citizen (by birth or naturalization).
c.  Have or be able to obtain and maintain applicable security clearance that is required for the position.
If no clearance is required, a favorable NACLC or higher must be maintained.
d.  Not listed as a military sex offender (see table 3–3) and have a favorable National Sex Offender
Registry check.
e.  Have no record of conviction by court-martial or time lost to be made good under 10 USC 972 dur-
ing current enlistment or last 3 years, whichever is longer.
f.  Have no record of civil conviction, except for minor offenses that do not disqualify for top secret se-
curity clearance under AR 380–67.
g.  Have no personal habits or character traits that are questionable from a security standpoint, includ-
ing financial irresponsibility, foreign holdings or interest, alcohol, or drug misuse (does not include Sol-
diers who are declared rehabilitation successes under the ASAP), gambling, or emotional dysregulation.
h.  Have no diagnosed mental or psychiatric disorders which, in the opinion of competent medical au-
thority, may cause a defect in judgment or reliability with due regard to the transient or continuing effect of
the illness and the medical findings in such case.
i.  Have a minimum GT score of 100.
j.  Meet body composition requirements in AR 600–9.
k.  Have 36 months’ time remaining in service upon arrival at assignment unless designated OCONUS
tour is less.
l.  Soldiers must not be suspended from favorable personnel actions; however, a disqualifying flagging
action for a minor infraction is waivable. For the purposes of this regulation, a minor infraction is that
which would permit a flagging action to be transferred (see AR 600–8–2). The HRC assignment manager
is the waiver authority.
m.  Soldiers assigned to an airborne position must be airborne qualified or have volunteered for air-
borne training.
n.  Soldier must be fully MOS qualified.
9–7.  Waivers
Requests or recommendations for waivers of assignment criteria will only be considered for the following:
a.  Time lost to be made good under 10 USC 972.
b.  Education level.
c.  GT aptitude area score (not applicable to Allied Command Operations assignments).
d.  Not possessing the requisite grade (not applicable to Allied Command Operations assignments).
e.  Active Federal service time.
f.  The TOS and retainability.
9–8.  International and outside the continental United States Joint headquarters, U.S. military
missions, Military Assistance Advisory Group, Joint U.S. Military Advisory Group, and similar
activities

an OCONUS assignment. Soldiers who apply under a specific HRC announced volunteer program must
also meet the qualifications prescribed for that program. If selected, they will be considered as IA for as-

9–11.  Defense Courier Service
In addition to the criteria in paragraph 9–6, Soldiers—
a.  Must possess a top-secret security clearance under AR 380–67, with SSBI not older than 4 years
and 9 months prior to departing current duty stations.
b.  Must have a military physical profile serial code of 111221 or better, be able to lift 50 pounds, and
must have no physical condition that prevents frequent travel aboard aircraft. Waivers may be approved
by U.S. Transportation Command.
c.  Must have no record of punishment under UCMJ, Art.15, during current enlistment or last 5 years,
whichever is longer.
d.  Must have valid state driver’s license.
e.  Must be a U.S. citizen.
f.  Must be in the rank of CPL or above.
g.  Due to the nature of courier duties and the small unit environment of courier stations, dual military
married couples may not be assigned concurrently to the same Defense Courier Service unit.
h.  Defense couriers are subject to short notice TDY that may take them away from their duty station for
several days at a time. Single parents should consider this carefully before volunteering for courier duty.
Single parents will be required to have a viable family care plan in place to care for their depend-ents.
i.  An official passport is required for a position as a defense courier.

9–15.  Joint communications support element
As an exception to the 36-month stabilization period, Soldiers assigned to MOS 25 series coded positions
in the joint communications support element will incur a 4-year SRR and will be stabilized for a period not
to exceed 4 years TOS.
9–16.  Security forces assistance brigades
a.  The SFAB are specialized units with the core mission to assess, advise, liaise, and support opera-
tions with allied and partner nations. Their work strengthens our allies and partners while supporting U.S.
security objectives and the combatant commanders' warfighting needs. Soldiers assigned to SFABs are
highly trained and among the top tactical leaders in the Army. Stringent screening processes exist to en-
sure that only the highest quality Soldiers are accepted and assigned. After assessment and assignment,
Soldiers will be stabilized for 36 months.
b.  In addition to the criteria in paragraph 9–6, SFAB eligibility requirements are as follows:
(1)  Must maintain deployable status.
(2)  Must obtain & maintain a secret security clearance (interim acceptable).
(3)  Pass AMHRR check for derogatory information.
(4)  Pass the physical fitness test of record with a minimum of 70 points in each event.
(5)  Have a physical capacity, upper, lower, hearing, eyes, psychiatric of 111221 or better (no physical
fitness test of record limiting profile), and vision and hearing limitations must be correctable.
(6)  SSG and below must pass a rigorous 2-day assessment or a command interview and be selected
for service in an SFAB. SFC and above who have not completed a career developmental assignment in
their current grade must also meet this requirement.
(7)  Soldiers at the rank of SFC or above, who have completed a career developmental position in their
current grade, will conduct an interview with the SFAB leadership and will not be required to attend the
assessment.

Section IV
Nominative Assignments
9–17.  Scope
a. Stringent screening processes exist to ensure that only the most highly qualified Soldiers are se-
lected for nominative positions.
b. Requests for units/agencies to be designated as nominative must be coordinated with the Chief,
Nominative Branch, HRC, prior to submission to DCS, G–1. The Chief, Nominative Branch, HRC, will ad-
vise the organization on supportability and possible limitations, such as limited eligibility pool, reduced
manning due to eligibility requirements, and increased timeline for fill of vacancies. If the Chief, Nomina-
tive Branch, HRC, recommends concurrence, requests to DCS, G–1 will include full justification of the
need for nominative designation. Requests for nominative designation will only be submitted when all po-
sitions within a unit/agency will be designated as nominative. Individual positions or portions of a
unit/agency will not be considered for nominative designation. Only the most highly qualified Soldiers are
selected for nominative positions at the following agencies:
(1) Office, Secretary of Defense.
(2) Joint Staff.
(3) Defense Commissary Agency.
(4) Defense Finance and Accounting Service.
(5) Defense Information Systems Agency.
(6) Defense Threat Reduction Agency.
(7) Defense Logistics Agency.
(8) Defense Intelligence Agency.
(9) National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency.
(10) Uniformed Services University of Health Sciences.
(11) U.S. Delegation NATO Military Committee.
(12) Immediate Offices, Office, Secretary of the Army.
(13) Office, Army Chief of Staff.
(14) Military Personnel Exchange Program (see AR 614–10).
(15) Office of the Administrative Assistant to the Secretary of the Army.
(16) National Airborne Operations Center.
9–18.  Overview
a. All personnel actions pertaining to nominative assignments are processed by the Chief, Readiness
Division, HRC (AHRC–FSR).
b. The normal tour of duty for these assignments is 3 to 4 years, unless otherwise specified by the indi-
vidual agency.
c. Soldiers assigned to these agencies are contact replacements requested by the appropriate agency
approximately 9 to 12 months in advance of the incumbent's scheduled rotation date.
9–19.  Selection criteria
Soldiers may volunteer or be nominated provided the following criteria are met. Soldiers must—
a. Be high school graduates or have a GED equivalent.
b. Have physical profile of 111121 or better.
c. Be U.S. citizens (by birth or naturalization).
d. Have or be able to obtain and maintain applicable security clearances that are required for the posi-
tion. If no clearance is required, a favorable NACLC or higher must be maintained.
e. Not listed as a military sex offender (see table 3–3) and have a favorable National Sex Offender
Registry check.
f. Have no record of conviction by court-martial or time lost to be made good under 10 USC 972 during
current enlistment or last 3 years, whichever is longer.
g. Have no record of civil conviction, except for minor offenses that do not disqualify for top secret se-
curity clearance under AR 380–67.
