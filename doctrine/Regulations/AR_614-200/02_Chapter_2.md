Chapter 2
Responsibilities
2–1.  Assistant Secretary of the Army (Manpower and Reserve Affairs)
The ASA (M&RA) will—
a. Develop and oversee assignment policy.
b. Approve second PCS assignments within the same fiscal year.
c. Waive criteria on assignment limitations for first-term Soldiers.
2–2.  Deputy Chief of Staff, G–1
The DCS, G–1 will—
a. Provide advice and assistance to the ASA (M&RA) in developing assignment policy for enlisted per-
sonnel.
b. Designate all command sergeant major (CSM) positions.
c. Plan and supervise the execution of policy for the Space Imbalanced Military Occupational Specialty
(SIMOS) Program.
d. Grant exceptions on a case-by-case basis to non-statutory provisions of this regulation unless other-
wise restricted.
e. Plan and supervise the execution of the stabilization policy for enlisted personnel.
f. Plan and supervise the execution of the policy for the Enlisted Personnel Management System, U.S.
Army Individual Ready Reserve (EPMS–IRR).
g. Through the CG, HRC —
(1) Interpret and implement policies initiated by the DCS, G–1.
(2) Issue AI.
(3) Manage the distribution of Soldiers to support the Army's mission worldwide.
(4) Manage the CSM Program.
(5) Implement assignment and utilization policies and monitor sergeant major (SGM) assignments
throughout the Army.
(6) Provide all Soldiers with broad opportunities for career progression and development, including ap-
proving applications for training.
(7) Supervise all personnel management functions for SIMOS, including identifying military occupa-
tional specialties (MOSs) that are space imbalanced and publishing a SIMOS listing at least once a year.
(8) Make final decision on all Joint domicile (JD) assignments.
(9) Establish procedures for managing, developing, and assigning Soldiers in career development pro-
grams (Intelligence Career Program, Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) Career Program, Army Bands
Career Program (ABCP), technical escort training), and Cyber.
(10) Approve Army Special Operations Forces (ARSOF) reenlistment option applications for training
and assignment to ARSOF units, including—
(a) Approving voluntary reclassification applications for training and assignment to ARSOF units.
(b) Coordinating with Commander, U.S. Army John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center, and School
(AOJK), Fort Bragg, NC 28307-5000, (usasoc.arsof.proponency.shdmbx@socom.mil) on those applica-
tions that require a waiver.
(11) Delegate, when necessary, approval authority to Commander, U.S. Army John F. Kennedy Spe-
cial Warfare Center, and School to—
(a) Approve waivers for ARSOF reenlistment option applications.
(b) Approve all voluntary applications, including waivers (when needed) for ARSOF training and re-
classification into career management fields (CMF) 18, 37, and 38.
(c) Award appropriate CMF MOS to RA Soldiers who have completed the Civil Affairs (CA) Qualifica-
tion Course (CAQC), Psychological Operations (PSYOPS) Qualification Course (POQC), or Special
Forces (SF) Qualification Course (SFQC).
(d) Report Soldiers who fail to successfully complete the CAQC, POQC, or SFQC to the Branch Chief,
Army Special Operation Forces Branch (AHRC–TAA–SF), HRC.

(12)  Manage the Drill Sergeant (DS) Program. The CG, HRC, has centralized control over the selec-
tion, assignment, classification, and release of all RA Soldiers assigned to the DS Program. The DS As-
signment Team (AHRC–FSR–N), Nominative Branch, Readiness Division (RD), Force Shaping Direc-
torate (FSD), is the HRC point of contact for personnel matters concerning all RA Soldiers in the program.
This includes requests to enter, requests for deletion or deferment from the DS Program, re-quests for
third-year DS extensions, and Soldier appeals of DS Program removal actions for reasons other than suit-
ability screening.
(13)  Executes and administers procedures and programs to implement Department of the Army (DA)
policy pertaining to reclassification, including reclassifications—
(a)  For sergeant first class (SFC) and above, regardless of MOS.
(b)  Requiring recoupment of bonus.
(c)  Resulting from change to the MOS structure reflected in DA Pam 611–21.
(14)  Grant exceptions on a case-by-case basis to non-statutory provisions of this regulation if dele-
gated by the DCS, G–1, unless otherwise restricted. This authority may be delegated to an authorized of-
ficial in the grade of COL or equivalent civilian grade level who directs assignment policies or procedures
for the Army. This authority will not be delegated outside of the proponent agency or its direct reporting
unit (DRU) or field operating agency.
(15)  Executes procedures to implement the policies established herein.
(16)  Control operational requirements of individual Soldier stabilization.
(17)  Exercise assignment approval authority for Soldiers assigned to stabilized positions.
(18)  Ensure that the Reserve Component (RC) career counselor—as part of the transition life cycle
function—counsels, informs, and initially assigns those Soldiers with or without a military service obliga-
tion who are being released from AD into Army National Guard of the United States (ARNGUS) units,
USAR troop program unit (TPU), or the Individual Ready Reserve (IRR).
(19)  Budget for training, provide policy direction and monitor the personnel management of Individual
Mobilization Augmentee (IMA) and IRR.
(20)  Management of USAR AGR Soldiers in accordance with AR 135–18.
(21)  Manage the IRR (see AR 140–10) and approve all assignments to the IMA Program (see AR 140
–10). CG, HRC also coordinates with the CG, USAR Command, commanders of USAR geographic and
functional commands, and CG, U.S. Army Recruiting Command (USAREC), in filling unit shortages. The
CG, HRC will implement policies for IRR/IMA personnel management and training in coordination with
other members of the Army staff and designates personnel managers to manage an appropriate number
of IRR and IMA Soldiers.
(22)  Nominate Soldiers to fill Soldier Recovery Unit (SRU) cadre positions when installation re-sources
are not available. Identify IRR Soldiers, retiree recall volunteers, and IMA Soldiers available for assign-
ment to SRU cadre positions.
(23)  Provide AI for Soldiers being return to duty (RTD) from SRUs and send an email response to the
SRU commander/O–5 commander, U.S. Army Installation Management Command (IMCOM) command-
er, IMCOM MPD Chief, HQ U.S. Army Medical Command (MEDCOM) commander, and the Soldier's De-
fense Enterprise Email. This notification of assignment by email may be used as authority to issue Sol-
dier's individual PCS orders.
(a)  Report date for RTD Soldiers who are reassigned on the same installation (local move) will be
within 10 days from the HRC email assignment notification to SRU or MPD.
(b)  Report date for RTD Soldiers assigned to a unit requiring a PCS will be within 60 days from the
HRC AI or request for orders (RFO) notification.
2–3.  Chief, Army Reserve
The Chief, Army Reserve (CAR), who also serves as the CG, U.S. Army Reserve Command (USARC)—
a.  Assists in developing and overseeing policies for the management of all USAR Soldiers and pro-
vide management priorities to CG, HRC for AGR positions.
b.  Ensures USAR training divisions/separate brigade commanders conduct the DS Program within
their units as prescribed in paragraph 2–9d.
c.  Develops procedures and programs pertaining to the reclassification of USAR Soldiers.
d.  Delegates to USARC major subordinate command commanders the following responsibilities over
USAR TPU enlisted Soldiers (other than E-9) within their commands:
(1)  Planning and supervising the execution of assignment policy.

2–4.  Commanding General, U.S. Army Training and Doctrine Command
The CG, TRADOC will—
a.  Publicize career development programs, and—
(1)  Counsel prospective Soldiers on program opportunities and requirements.
(2)  Ensure applicants are eligible before applying for membership or training.
b.  Develop initial and transition training to support the SIMOS Program.
c.  Review modified table of organization and equipment (MTOE) or table of distribution and allowances
(TDA) for SIMOS positions to maximize CONUS documentation and ensure proper utilization and assign-
ment of Soldiers.
d.  Manage the DS Program by—
(1)  Operating the DS Academy.
(2)  Removing Soldiers from school (when reason warrants) and returning them to their home station.
(3)  Completing academic evaluation reports.
(4)  Providing strength management statistics.
(5)  Providing vital information on all Soldiers who report to, as well as who graduate from, DS Acad-
emy.
(6)  Providing historical data, including removal data, on Soldiers who have served DS duty.
e.  Approve reclassification of Soldiers in the grade of Staff Sergeant (SSG) and below in military occu-
pational specialties (MOSs) authorized by HRC.
f.  Manage Army Instructor Development and Recognition Program.
g.  The U.S. Army Training Center, Fort Jackson, is the Army proponent for and manages the DS Pro-
gram.
h.  Through the CG, USAREC—
(1)  Be responsible for the ARSOF Recruiting Program.
(2)  Award appropriate CMF 18, 37, and 38 MOS to RC Soldiers who elect to qualify by attending res-
ident instruction.
2–5.  Commanders of Army commands, Army service component commands, and direct reporting
units
Commanders of ACOMs, ASCCs, and DRUs will be responsible for the following personnel matters that
pertain to their command or to agencies under their jurisdiction. Commanders will—
a.  Submit enlisted personnel requisitions to the CG, HRC reflecting personnel requirements.
b.  Ensure that Soldiers within their commands are properly assigned and utilized.
c.  Approve reclassification of their Soldiers. For RA, commanders will approve reclassification of Sol-
diers, SSG, and below when their MOS is authorized by the HRC's reenlistment or reclassification
IN/OUT call message, or otherwise directed by the CG, HRC.
d.  Ensure Soldiers are afforded an opportunity for training and that—
(1)  Their applications are processed promptly.
(2)  When selected, they are fully qualified.
e.  Publicize the career development programs and—
(1)  Counsel prospective Soldiers on program opportunities and requirements.
(2)  Ensure applicants are eligible for membership or training.
f.  Approve or disapprove exchange assignments.
g.  Publicize the ARSOF Program and ensure Soldiers have access to ARSOF in-service recruiters and
recruiter briefings. Additionally, Commanders will work with ARSOF in-service recruiters to sched-ule as-
sessment and selection course dates to meet both unit and ARSOF operational requirements and the
Soldier's career goals.
h.  Delegate to subordinate senior commanders authority to assign Soldiers, including determining Sol-
diers' suitability for assignments from a security standpoint. Commanders should consult with appropriate
installation security officials to ensure Soldiers meet the security requirements of projected assignment.
i.  Recommend approval or disapproval of all positions slated for stabilization.
j.  Ensure Commanders of SRUs are responsible for reassignment of Soldiers being declared RTD as
prescribed in paragraph 2–8.

2–6.  Chief National Guard Bureau
The CNGB will ensure State Adjutants General comply with the personnel management programs pre-

2–7.  Directors of Army training centers and schools
a.  Directors of Army training centers and schools will—
(1)  As appropriate, grant waivers of qualifications specified in the Army Training Requirements and Re-
sources System (ATRRS) course catalog.
(2)  Evaluate ways to reduce or eliminate SIMOS (both current or projected) through changes to MTOE
or TDA, MOS mergers or conversions, or in the case of new fielding modification of the fielding schedule.
(3)  Identify non-SIMOS TDA positions within the personnel proponents' affected CMF to be recoded as
SIMOS. If no CMF position can be identified as SIMOS, the director will coordinate with the Chief, Military
Schools Branch (AHRC–FSF–EM), HRC, and provide justification to identify other TDA positions for the
affected SIMOS.
(4)  Recommend changes in service-remaining requirements for those MOSs that are deemed critically
short, thereby enhancing the return on the training investment and the maximum retainability of Soldiers
in shortage skills.
b.  The U.S. Army Training Center, Fort Jackson, is the Army proponent for and manages the DS Pro-
gram.
2–8.  Commanders of Soldier recovery units
Commanders having SRUs are responsible for reassignment of Soldiers being RTD as follows:
a.  Continental U.S. Soldier recovery unit. Immediately upon the Soldiers being declared RTD, the SRU
commander (or first O–5 commander in the chain of command, if the SRU commander is not an O–5 or
above) will request AI directly to the HRC via email to usarmy.knox.hrc.mbx.epmd-coad@army.mil.
b.  Outside the continental United States Soldier recovery unit. Immediately upon the Soldier being de-
clared RTD, the SRU commander (or first O–5 in the chain of command, if the SRU commander is not an
O–5 or above) will request AI via email from their respective OCONUS theater commander.
(1)  If the OCONUS theater commander determines that the Soldier will be reassigned within the
OCONUS area, OCONUS commander must provide SRU commanders/ O–5 the assignment decision
within 5 days of RTD notification. Further, the OCONUS theater commander will establish coordination
with the Chief of the servicing IMCOM military personnel division (MPD) to produce PCS orders within the
5-day required processing standard.
(2)  If the OCONUS theater commander determines that the Soldier cannot be reassigned within the
OCONUS command or has a near term or expired date eligible for return from overseas (DEROS),
OCONUS commander will forward request for AI as prescribed for CONUS SRUs in paragraph 2–8a.
