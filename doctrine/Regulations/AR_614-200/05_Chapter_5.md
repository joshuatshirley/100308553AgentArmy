Chapter 5
Optional Assignments and Soldier-Initiated Assignments or Actions
Section I
Optional Assignments
5–1.  Scope and objectives
a.  Soldiers must volunteer for optional assignments. They also must be highly motivated to complete
the required training and successfully perform the duties. Because of the unique mission or capabilities of
the units, a high state of readiness is required. Therefore, volunteers will be assigned on a priority basis.
Stringent screening processes exist to ensure that only the highest quality volunteers are accepted and
assigned. Soldiers who desire to apply for duty or assignment may do so without regard to current assign-
ment. Only fully qualified Soldiers will be accepted for duty in optional assignments.
b.  The objective of the Airborne Program is to ensure an adequate number of parachute-qualified Sol-
diers are available to maintain a high state of readiness.
c.  The objective of the Ranger Training Brigade Program is to maintain Army readiness through range
qualified Soldiers (SQI “G” and “V”).
d.  The U.S. Army Parachute Team (USAPT) (Golden Knights) objective is to increase public aware-
ness of and enhance the image of the Army both nationally and internationally. The Golden Knights are
highly qualified Soldiers who represent the Army through demonstration jumps, participation in national
and international-level jump competitions, tandem camps, and utilization of media relations or public con-
tact opportunities in support of Army recruiting and marketing efforts.
e.  The United States Army Marksmanship Unit (USAMU) objective is to select, equip, and train Sol-
diers to compete and win intra-service, national, international, and Olympic marksmanship competitions;
increase lethality through training, research, and development, doctrine development, and augmentation
to contingency operations; enhance accessions through demonstrations, clinics, and public engage-
ments; and promote the Army by fostering public confidence in Army marksmanship though active in-for-
mation and public relations programs focusing on the accomplishments and capabilities of USAMU Sol-
diers.

5–2.  Airborne duty (special qualification identifier “P” (parachutist))
a.  Soldiers must successfully complete airborne training (be awarded SQI “P”) to be assigned to an
airborne position (see chap 4 for training). Guidance for submitting an application for airborne training and
assignment is in DA Pam 600–8.
b.  Soldiers volunteering for airborne training must—
(1)  Have completed BT and AIT (except for MOS 92R) or the equivalent of such training. (That is, they
must possess civilian-acquired skills or qualify for on-the-job AIT on graduation from BT.)
(2)  Meet the medical qualifications for airborne training and duty (see AR 40–501).
(3)  Achieve the aptitude score for the MOS in which enlisting; this applies to all potential airborne vol-
unteer enlistees. Trainees with an enlistment option for MOS 92R will be scheduled by the Chief, Model-
ing and Requirement Branch, HRC (AHRC–FSR–M) to attend airborne training enroute to MOS 92R
training.
(4)  Not have more than 30 days lost during current enlistment due to one or more of the following rea-
sons:
(a)  Desertion.
(b)  Being absent from their organization, station, or duty without proper authority as determined by
competent authority.
(c)  Confinement by military or civilian authorities in connection with a trial, whether before, during, or
after the trial.
(d)  Inability as determined by competent authority, to perform the duties because of intemperate use of
drugs or alcoholic liquor or because of disease or injury resulting from their misconduct (see 10 USC
972).
(5)  Be less than 36 years old on the date of application. Soldiers in the grade of SGT or above may
obtain a waiver for age with favorable recommendation from a medical doctor.
(6)  Have enough service remaining on current enlistment to complete at least 12 months of service af-
ter airborne training. Applicants without enough service remaining to meet this requirement must take ac-
tion to meet length of service requirements (see AR 601–280). Action to meet time-in-service remaining
requirements must be completed before orders to attend airborne training are issued.
(7)  Not be alerted for or on AI to an overseas area (either as an individual or as a member of a unit)
unless the projected assignment is to an airborne unit.
(8)  Not be under court-martial charges or investigation that may result in trial by court-martial.
(9)  Not possess a DA-controlled or shortage MOS, unless that MOS is in an airborne table of organiza-
tion and equipment (TOE) identified by SQI “P” for airborne qualified Soldiers. Soldiers possessing a
PMOS that are not in an airborne TOE are ineligible to apply for airborne training while so assigned.
(10)  Not be in AIT. On declaration of general war or full mobilization, this criterion is suspended.
(11)  Not be currently enrolled in or committed for any course for award of an MOS not shown in an air-
borne unit TOE. (Waiver may be requested, if not already enrolled, provided application for airborne train-
ing contains a waiver of enlistment commitment.)
(12)  Have completed any service obligation incurred as a result of previous training received at an
Army course of instruction for an MOS not shown in an airborne TOE. (This does not apply if the MOS is
in an overage status Armywide within applicant's year group.)
(13)  Not have deliberately terminated previous airborne duty or training. (This does not apply to those
whose termination was based on extreme Family problems.) On declaration of general war or full mobili-
zation, this criterion is suspended.
(14)  Pass physical readiness test. The test will be—
(a)  Not more than 30 days before the date of application and again not more than 15 days before the
class start date for permanent party personnel. Those who fail to pass the second test will be reported to
CG, HRC, through the appropriate HRC branch manager, for reassignment. Orders assigning the Soldier
to airborne training will be revoked.
(b)  Within 2 weeks before departing from the BT activity for Soldiers completing BT and attending air-
borne training before reported for on-the-job AIT.
(c)  During AIT for new accessions to the Army, except as in paragraph 5–2b(14)(b). Soldiers who fail
the first test will be retested during AIT. Those who fail to pass the final test will be reported to Chief, IET
Management (AHRC–FSR–D), HRC, for reassignment.
(15)  Meet medical, physical, and mental prerequisites for attending airborne training. (See AR 40–501
and ATRRS Course Catalog.)

5–7.  Exemption from assignment to duty in a designated hostile fire area
Assignment to duty in a hostile fire or imminent-danger area (hereinafter referred to as “hostile fire area”
(HFA)) must be shared equitably by all similarly qualified Soldiers, except as outlined as follows:
a.  Combat Wounded Soldiers. Wounded Soldiers who have been hospitalized 30 or more days out-
side an HFA due to wounds received from combat action will not be returned to the hostile fire area un-
less they are medically qualified and they volunteer to return. Soldiers reassigned under this paragraph,
may be eligible for subsequent HFA tours. The provisions of this paragraph will not apply to Soldiers hos-
pitalized for injury, accident, or illness not attributable to HFA. This prevents considering cases such as
self-inflicted wounds and other noncombat causes.
b.  Surviving Family Member. If a military member of a Family, during military service in an HFA, is
killed, dies, is missing-in-action, is captured, or has been officially determined by the Department of Veter-
ans Affairs or a military Service to be 100 percent disabled, then Soldiers of the same Family will be ex-
empt, upon request, from serving in designated HFAs or, if serving in such an area, will be reassigned
from there. Family members include the following:
(1)  Parent. This includes stepfather, father by adoption, stepmother, mother by adoption, or those who
have stood in loco parentis as the Soldier’s parent for at least 5 years.
(2)  Brothers and sisters. This includes stepbrothers, brothers by adoption, half-brothers, stepsisters,
sisters by adoption, or half-sisters.
(3)  Spouse.
(4)  Children.
(a)  Legitimate child.
(b)  Legally adopted child.
(c)  Stepchild, if a member of the household at the time the stepchild entered military service.
(d)  An illegitimate child of a female Soldier.
(e)  An illegitimate child to whose support a male Soldier is ordered or decreed judicially to contribute,
who has been decreed judicially to have been fathered by a Soldier, or whose Soldier father acknowl-
edged in writing under oath that he is the father.
(f)  A person for whom the Soldier stood in loco parentis for at least 5 years.
c.  Sole surviving son or daughter. A sole surviving son or daughter may not be assigned to a PCS, a
TDY, or duties involving actual combat with the enemy if they, or one of their parents, or their spouse

5–8.  Requests for and waivers of hostile fire exemptions
a.  Requests for exemption from duty in an HFA may be submitted at any time. When a request is sub-
mitted to exempt a Soldier from their current assignment, the Soldier will be retained in place until action
on their application is finalized.
b.  Soldiers’ requests for exemption from duty in an HFA will be forwarded through command channels
to the CG, HRC, Special Actions Branch (AHRC–FSO–S), 1600 Spearhead Division Avenue, Fort Knox,
KY 40122-5303, usarmy.knox.mbx.epmd-compassionate-section@army.mil for approval. A Soldier's par-

the Soldier directly to the CG, HRC, Special Actions Branch. Requests for surviving family member ex-

c.  All requests will include substantiating evidence and identification of all personnel involved. Upon
approval, the Chief, Special Actions Branch, will process assignment deletions and any other actions re-

d.  When the parent(s) or spouse of a qualified sole surviving son or daughter requests protective as-

spousal request before a final determination is made by the approval authority. Soldiers may also waive
previously approved sole surviving son or daughter exemptions when initial approval was based on their
own application.
e.  A Soldier who enlists, reenlists, or voluntarily extends their period of AD after getting surviving family
member or sole surviving son or daughter status is considered as having waived their right to exemption
from assignment to a hostile fire area.
f.  Soldiers who withdraw their approved HFA exemption may request reinstatement of the exemption
at any time by resubmitting their request. The Soldier will be removed promptly to a "safe haven" within
the combat zone until the request for reinstatement is completed.
Section III
Compassionate Reassignments
5–9.  Overview
a.  Compassionate actions are requests from individual Soldiers when personal problems exist. The
two types of compassionate requests for personal problems are:
(1)  Temporary (resolvable within a year); or
(2)  Not expected to be resolved within a year.
b.  Soldiers may be reassigned, deleted, or deferred from AI, or attached as a result of an approved
compassionate request. Soldiers requesting reassignment may be assigned to an area other than their
requested geographical preference based on availability of medical services and the needs of the Army.
c.  Soldiers may request reassignment from their current duty location to any other duty location.
d.  Normally, the following conditions alone are not bases for a compassionate request:

k.  Soldier is to remain at current location while pending a compassionate decision. They are not to take

5–15.  Expedited transfers for victims of sexual assault or related offenses
In accordance with DoDI 6495.02, Volume 1, the intent behind the expedited transfer policy is to address
situations where a victim of sexual assault feels safe but uncomfortable and to assist in the victim’s recov-
ery by moving the victim to a new location where no one knows of the sexual assault. An example of
where a victim feels uncomfortable is where a victim may be experiencing ostracism and retaliation.
a.  The Army is committed to the health, safety, and well-being of its Soldiers. Victims of sexual as-sault
may not wish to remain in their current unit or organization after the sexual assault incident, as remaining
may negatively affect their emotional well-being, as well as the cohesion of the unit or organization. It is
Army policy that a presumption in favor of transferring or reassigning a sexual assault victim will be sup-
ported at their request, following that victim’s credible unrestricted report of sexual as-sault. Commanders
will consider requests for transfer or reassignment in an expedited manner.
b.  Soldier victims of sexual assault or related offenses listed under UCMJ, Articles 117a, 120, 120c,
and 130, or attempts to commit these offenses, are eligible to request an expedited transfer.
(1)  Soldiers may request an expedited transfer on behalf of their Family member aged 18 and older if
the Family member is a victim of sexual assault that was unrelated to domestic abuse, has filed an un-
restricted report of sexual assault, the sexual assault subject is a servicemember or the subject has a mili-
tary nexus, or the sexual assault occurred on a DoD installation or facility. Requests for expedited trans-
fers for adult Family members will be processed by the Soldier’s commander within 5 calendar days of the
submission of the application. If the application is disapproved by the commander, the Soldier will be
given the opportunity to request review by the first GO in his or her chain of command. The GO must
make a decision within 5 calendar days of submission of the request for review.
(2)  The following policy applies to the transfer of Family members:
(a)  The transfer will include the Soldier and the Soldier’s Family members, even if they are geo-graph-
ically separated.
(b)  The Family members may be transferred expeditiously, and the Soldier’s transfer may be delayed
based upon professional development or operational requirements.
(c)  The Soldier’s commander will ultimately determine whether Soldiers will be transferred at the same
time as their Family members.
(d)  The Soldier’s commander will assign personnel, within the command, if possible, to assist Soldiers
with approved dependent expedited transfers with the out-processing required for PCS moves, including
facilitating transportation, as necessary, and will ensure Soldiers receive any required evaluation report.
(e)  The losing and gaining SARCs will coordinate the transfer of the adult dependent’s case and facili-
tate the intake meeting with the gaining SARC at the new installation.
c.  Any threat to life or safety of a victim must be immediately reported to the command and CID.
d.  Soldiers and Family Members aged 18 and older who file an unrestricted report of sexual assault
must be informed by the SARC, sexual harassment/assault response and prevention victim advocate
(SHARP VA), commander, or civilian supervisor equivalent, at the time of making the report, or as soon
as practicable, of the option to request an expedited transfer from their assigned command or installation,
or to a different location within their assigned command or installation. A Soldier on AI may also request
an assignment deletion when the subject of the criminal investigation is assigned or inbound to the Sol-
dier’s gaining location.
e.  Brigade commanders will take all necessary steps to prevent a transfer from negatively impacting
the Soldier’s career to the extent possible. The commander will ensure the Soldier is fully informed re-

investigation and case disposition or the initiation of other adverse action against the subject, the effect

questing a transfer must be informed that they may have to return for the prosecution of the case, if the

5–16.  Applicability
Provisions of this section apply to AD Servicemembers in the following categories: RA, RC, USAR AGR,
and 10 USC ARNGUS AGR Soldiers married to members of the RA, RCs, other AD U.S. military Ser-
vices, USAR AGR, and 10 USC ARNGUS. Soldiers married to civilians or non-AD members of the Army
or other military Services are not included.
5–17.  Scope
a.  RA married Army couples desiring joint assignment to establish a common household or JD must
request such assignment by enrolling in the MACP. Application for enrollment can be initiated anytime,
including IET Soldiers, after date of marriage.
(1)  Only one Soldier needs to request enrollment in the MACP when both Soldiers are serviced by the
same MPD, BCT, or brigade S1; otherwise, both must apply. Company-level commanders are the signa-
ture authority for MACP requests on the PAR.
(2)  The servicing Chief of MPD or BCT/brigade S1 will submit the appropriate spouse information in
the Enlisted Assignment System of Record. Soldiers are not officially enrolled until their spouse infor-
mation is updated on the Enlisted Assignment System of Record.
(3)  After submission of the spouse data, the Chief, MPD, or BCT/ brigade S1 will verify enrollment
through the Personnel System of Record. Verification of enrollment must be made prior to notifying Sol-
diers of the status of their enrollment.
(4)  When one Soldier is considered for reassignment, the other Soldier is automatically considered for
assignment to the same location or area except when one Soldier is assigned to a dependent-restricted
location. AI for each member will indicate whether a joint assignment is approved. If one Soldier is consid-
ered for assignment, and the other Soldier does not have sufficient time remaining prior to ETS for an as-
signment and does not reenlist or extend, the provisions of the MACP do not apply.
(5)  Enrollment guarantees JD ASCO; however, it does not guarantee that the couple will be assigned
together at the same location or at the same time.
(6)  Consideration is continuous as long as the couple remains enrolled in the program.
(7)  Favorable consideration for JD assignment will depend on—
(a)  A valid requisition/requirement existing in the same area for both Soldiers’ military PMOS and
branch (officers) and grades.
(b)  Career progression of both Soldiers not being adversely affected and Soldiers being otherwise eli-
gible for the assignment.
(8)  Married Army couples who do not enroll in the MACP indicate that JD assignments are not de-
sired; therefore, this cannot be used as the basis to request deletion from an assignment.
b.  RA and USAR AGR Soldiers married to members of other branches of service who desire to be co-
located with their spouse may request joint stationing by submitting a PAR.
c.  USAR AGR Soldiers, upon notification of ASCO, must reiterate to talent managers their enrollment
in MACP.
d.  Action based on intended marriage will not be considered even as an exception to policy.

5–22.  Compassionate stabilizations due to death of a spouse
This policy was established to help a Soldier during a period of extreme hardship to ensure that they are
afforded the stability and allowed time to address personal affairs. Soldiers who experience the death of a

“S” with an expiration date.

5–23.  Stabilization of Soldiers and military Families with special needs (Exceptional Family

Army policy allows for military Families with special needs to be stabilized for up to four years so medical
or educational services, which cannot be immediately replicated or acquired elsewhere, are not disrupted.

TDY or TCS away from home station to participate in combat or operational deployments, training exer-
cises, or for personnel management or professional development reasons, after the first year of the ap-
proved stabilization timeframe. The stabilization period (if approved) starts on the date the EFM began
receiving the required services. The Family member that is the basis for the stabilization request must be
enrolled in the EFMP.
a. Criteria. The stabilization must support Army Readiness and professional development of the Sol-
dier, and one or more of the below situations must apply in order to establish eligibility for stabilization un-
der this program:
(1) A PCS move would disrupt access to necessary medical or educational services that would place
the EFM’s health, safety, or development in jeopardy.
(2) The EFM has a diagnosis that requires extensive support from TRICARE, State, and local re-
sources that would be difficult to replicate or reacquire in a timely manner.
(3) The Family has multiple EFM’s receiving a combination of TRICARE, state, and local services that
would be difficult to replicate or reacquire in a timely manner.

tional deployments, training exercises, or for personnel management or professional development after
the first year of the approved stabilization timeframe. I also understand that I may request termination of
my stabilization in writing (PAR) at any time during the stabilization period (a period not to exceed four

5–24.  Stabilization for Soldiers with high school seniors
a.  Policy. Soldiers with Family members in high school may request stabilization from PCS movement
during the child's senior year. The intent of the program is to provide stability to Soldiers with Family
members in their junior and senior years of high school. In order to qualify, the student must be enrolled
in Defense Enrollment Eligibility System (DEERS) and must be under the direct care or custody of the
Soldier submitting the request.
(1)  HRC Directors with oversight of military personnel assignments are the approval/disapproval au-
thority.
(2)  For Soldiers on AI, normal reassignment rules apply. That is, the Soldier may request deletion or
deferment in accordance with AR 600–8–11. These requests will be considered an exception to policy.
(3)  Soldiers’ EFMP is required to be current or closed in accordance with AR 608–75.
(4)  Soldiers enrolled in the MACP must both apply if both desire stabilization under these provisions.
(5)  Stabilization under these provisions may be changed or canceled due to the changing needs of the
Army.
(6)  Stabilization does not preclude Army assignment authorities from placing the Soldier (or one of the
two MACP-enrolled Soldiers serving at the same geographical location as the high school in which their
child is a student) on assignment to a dependent-restricted tour during the stabilization period.
(7)  Soldiers may be eligible to PCS before the start of the student's junior year of high school.
b.  Applicability and criteria.
(1)  The provisions of this section apply to RA and USAR AGR Soldiers only. ARNGUS Soldiers should

(2)  RA and USAR AGR Soldiers assigned to non-Army agencies or units must comply with all direc-
tives and regulations that apply to their command before submitting their request (for example, Soldiers
assigned to the Defense Courier Service must comply with DoDI 5200.33). Submission requirements are
prescribed below. Soldiers may only request stabilization when currently assigned to a duty station at the
same geographical location as the high school in which their child is a student (for example, a Soldier sta-
tioned OCONUS serving a dependent-restricted tour is ineligible to apply).
c.  Submission of requests.
(1)  Requests should be submitted no earlier than March of the student's "freshman" year, and not later
than the commencement of the student's sophomore school year (for example, Student's projected gradu-

this time frame will be considered as an exception to policy and must be justified as to the delay of sub-
mission.
