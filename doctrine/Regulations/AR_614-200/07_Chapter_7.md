Chapter 7
Sergeant Major of the Army, Command Sergeants Major, and Sergeants Major
Section I
Overview
7–1.  Scope
This chapter applies to RA and USAR Soldiers in the rank of U.S. Army Senior Enlisted Advisor to the
Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff (SEAC), Sergeant Major of the Army (SMA), CSM, and SGM.
7–2.  Summary
The Army expects its senior-most NCOs to serve selflessly no matter where assigned and utilized, bene-
fiting from its investment in a career’s worth of training and development. The Army will utilize individuals
based on an evaluation of their talent (knowledge, skills, and behaviors) by matching them to unit require-
ments. The management philosophies outlined within this chapter are designed to enhance Army readi-
ness by aligning individual capability with the Army’s needs while optimizing human performance and en-
gagement. Eligibility criteria in support of these efforts ensures consideration of highly qualified, broadly
skilled, and experienced NCO leaders who have been developed to assume higher levels of responsibil-
ity.
7–3.  Centralized management
The Vice Chief of Staff of the Army (VCSA) and the SMA have management oversight of all CSM/SGM
(including NCOs selected for USASMA training) across the Army.
a.  The SMA is the approval authority for all RA slates and has general oversight of all CSM and SGM
programs.
b.  The CAR, Deputy CAR (DCAR), and the USARC CSM have management oversight for USARC
CSM/SGM, and NCOs selected for USASMA training. The USARC CSM is the approval authority for all
USAR Slates and general oversight of all CSM and SGM programs.
c.  Management authorities are as follows:
(1)  All RA USASMA students (effective with the resident course start date to include students attending
a non-traditional training course, such as Joint Special Operations Senior Enlisted Academy or non-resi-
dent training) 1SG/MSG and SGM are the functional responsibility of the Branch Chief, Sergeants Major
Branch (AHRC–TAD–S), Sergeants Major Management Division, HRC.
(2)  All RA non-nominative CSMs are the functional responsibility of the Branch Chief, Command Man-
agement Branch (AHRC–TAD–C), Sergeants Major Management Division, HRC.
(3)  All RA nominative CSM/SGM are the functional responsibility of the Chief, Nominative Sergeants
Major Program Office (DACS–SMN), Sergeants Major Management Division, HRC.
(4)  All USAR CSM/SGM AGR, TPU (including nominative CSM/SGM), IMA, IRR, and USASMA stu-
dents are the functional responsibility of the Director, Senior Leader Development Office (SLDO), (DAAR–
SL), Office of the Chief, Army Reserve (OCAR).
d.  This chapter prescribes policies as follows:
(1)  Section I: RA and USAR.
(2)  Section II: RA only.
(3)  Sections III thru V: RA and USAR (AGR).
(4)  Section VI: USAR TPU only.
(5)  Section VII: USAR (TPU, IMA, and IRR).

7–4.  Lateral appointments (U.S. Army Senior Enlisted Advisor to the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs
of Staff, Sergeant Major of the Army, command sergeant major, or sergeant major)
Wearing of CSM rank requires formal appointment following a determination of qualification for such ap-
pointment by a HQDA CSM/SGM Evaluation Board, without exception. Soldiers will wear CSM or SGM
rank commensurate with the duties to which they are assigned and serving. Soldiers serving in an author-
ized CSM position will wear the CSM rank, and Soldiers serving in an authorized SGM position will wear

published by HRC (AHRC–TAD) for RA or OCAR (DAAR–SL) for USAR AGR. However, when AI are not
required, the lateral appointment will be announced by memorandum by the same agencies.
b. Effective date of lateral appointment.
(1) Lateral appointment to SEAC. The effective date is the date the Soldier assumes SEAC duties.
Rank entry date remains unchanged.
(2) Lateral appointment to SMA. The effective date is the date the Soldier assumes SMA duties. Rank
entry date remains unchanged.
(3) Lateral appointment to CSM. The effective date is the date the Soldier assumes CSM duties. Rank
entry date remains unchanged.
(4) Lateral appointment to SGM.
(a) All CSMs will be laterally appointed to SGM prior to a reduction in grade when otherwise required.
(b) In all instances, the effective date for lateral appointment to SGM is the day after the Soldier com-
pletes (or is removed from) the preceding CSM assignment (typically, the PCORD). The rank entry date
remains unchanged.
c. If this is the Soldier’s first appointment to CSM, the losing HQ S1 will prepare the DA Form 4873
(Certificate of Appointment to Command Sergeant Major) and present it at an appointment ceremony.
7–5.  Appointment of interim command sergeant major
When a command loses an authorized CSM and the position cannot be immediately filled, an interim
CSM may be identified. The interim CSM will remain until another CSM is assigned. The interim CSM—
a. Will be a SGM, 1SG, or MSG.
b. Will serve in the current grade held. If not already an SGM, they will not be promoted to SGM
against the CSM position.
c. Will remain assigned to their current duty position.
d. Will not be laterally appointed to CSM.
7–6.  Frocking of master sergeants to sergeant major
Refer to AR 600–8–19 for policy on frocking of MSG to SGM.
7–7.  Personnel suitability screening
Soldiers identified to attend the USASMA, initial appointment to CSM, selection/slating as a brigade-level
CSM, nominative/key billet CSM/SGM, or selection as the SMA are all subject to post-board suitability
screening as described in AR 600–78.
a. This process includes review of information contained within the restricted section of the Army Mili-
tary Human Resource Record (AMHRR), substantiated DA IG reports, and reports filed with the U. S.
Army Crimes Records Center. Suitability screening focuses on obtaining derogatory information that was
not available nor considered by the original board. Derogatory information is considered any credible, un-
favorable information that may reflect on a Soldier’s character, integrity, trustworthiness, or reliability. The
information may disqualify an individual from selection because of the potential adverse im-pact on the
efficiency of the Army.
b. The suitability process will be conducted in two phases. The first phase is the Enlisted Review
Board, followed by the second phase, potential referral to the U.S. Army Senior Enlisted Review Board.
(1) Enlisted Review Board.
(a) Conducts a post-board screening process by reviewing substantiated, relevant, and potentially seri-
ous in nature derogatory information pertaining to NCOs subject to screening.
(b) The Enlisted Review Board is a recommending body to the DMPM (DAPE–MP), who, on behalf of
the DCS, G–1, approves, or disapproves the board’s recommendations to either release the NCO with-
out further review or to refer the NCO to the Army Senior Enlisted Review Board.

Section II
Sergeant Major of the Army
7–8.  Sergeant Major of the Army overview
a.  Established in 1966, the SMA is the senior enlisted position of the Army. Appointed by the Chief of
Staff of the Army (CSA), the SMA serves at the discretion of the Secretary of the Army. The SMA is the
senior enlisted advisor and consultant to the CSA.
b.  The SMA provides information on problems and proposed solutions affecting enlisted personnel
concerning standards, profession development, growth, and advancement of NCOs, morale, training,
pay, promotions, and quality of life for Soldiers and their Family members. The SMA provides the enlist-
ed viewpoint to Members of Congress, DA selection boards, and committees, meets with military and ci-
vilian organizations to discuss enlisted affairs, and receives and represents Army enlisted personnel at
appropriate ceremonies.
7–9.  Sergeant Major of the Army selection
When required, an SMA selection board will be formally announced by the DCS, G–1, via an All-Army Ac-
tivity message.
a.  The board announcement message will contain all established eligibility criteria. Typically, eligibility
is limited to Soldiers with CSM experience at the nominative three or four-star GO level.
b.  Detailed instructions will be contained in the board announcement message.
7–10.  Sergeant Major of the Army responsibilities for senior noncommissioned officer
management
The SMA has a direct role in the nominative CSM or SGM process and serves, concurrent with the CSA,
as the approval authority for nominative slates. The SMA serves as the lead agent of the CSM or SGM
Nominative Program. The SMA serves as the exception authority for requests to serve longer than the
prescribed tour lengths for all nominative CSM or SGM.

7–13.  Nominative command sergeants major and nominative sergeants major (Regular Army)
selection process
Selection to serve as a nominative CSM or SGM is achieved utilizing a 2-phase process.
a.  HQDA CSM/SGM Evaluation Board (Phase 1).
(1)  A HQDA NCO evaluation board will convene annually to evaluate individual talent (skills,
knowledge, and behaviors) for the purpose of creating an order of merit list (OML). This OML will be used
to identify best-qualified NCOs for additional consideration (Phase II) to serve as a nominative CSM or
SGM at the one or two-star GO level, producing the Annual Nominative Eligibility List. Eligibility criteria
will be announced separately. Eligibility criteria will ensure consideration of highly qualified, broadly
skilled, and experienced NCOs who have been developed to assume higher levels of responsibility. This
effort enhances Army readiness by aligning individual capability with Army needs while optimizing human
performance.
(2)  NCOs permanently removed from the nominative program are not eligible for Phase II considera-
tion, regardless of their standing on the OML.
(3)  An NCO’s OML status does not, in, and of itself, constitute, or guarantee selection to a nominative
CSM/SGM position. All NCOs identified as fully qualified, subject to established requirements and their
individual OML status, will be referred to Phase II for further consideration and, at a later date, may be
nominated to a selecting official (GO/SES) to serve as a nominative CSM and or SGM.
(4)  Current/former nominative CSM/SGM are not eligible for evaluation by the CSM/SGM Evaluation
Board but are otherwise eligible for continued utilization in the Nominative Program.
b.  SMA Nominative Panel (Phase 2).
(1)  A SMA Nominative Panel will convene, at the discretion of the SMA, to consider Soldiers both se-
lected as best-qualified by the HQDA CSM/SGM Nominative Selection Board and current/former nomina-
tive CSM or SGM for individual nomination to a GO for selection to serve against a specific upcoming va-
cancy.
(2)  The SMA Nominative Panel will review and evaluate best-qualified CSM/SGM for potential nomina-
tion to a GO based on knowledge, skills, and behaviors of the NCO and the position requirements, for-
warding only those NCOs evaluated as best-qualified for the nominative position to the selecting GO for
consideration.
(3)  It is the responsibility of the individual CSM/SGM to ensure their AMHRR and Enlisted Record Brief
are accurate and up to date. The Chief, Nominative Sergeants Major Program Office will compile records
for review by the Panel. Other than as provided for in this regulation, documents that are not filed in the
AMHRR will not be authorized for consideration by the panel.
(4)  A letter of recommendation (LOR) is a required, specific memorandum that covers the nominee’s
strengths and weaknesses. The LOR will be written and sent by the CSM/SGM’s current command-
er/rater and will adhere to the format provided by the Nominative Sergeants Major Program Office. LORs
will not be accepted from the rated CSM/SGM.
(a)  All NCOs selected as best-qualified by the CSM/SGM Evaluation Board are required to have an
LOR submitted to the Chief, Nominative Sergeants Major Program Office within 30 days of the list re-
lease. The Chief, Nominative Sergeants Major Program Office will coordinate directly with the selectee’s
commander/rater for the letter.
(b)  All commanders/raters of newly assigned/first-time nominative CSM/SGM and currently serving
one and two-star level GO command level nominative CSM/SGM are required to submit an LOR annually.
(5)  Candidates selected by the panel will be approved by the VCSA and staffed to the interviewing GO
for interview and selection.

character or ethical deficiencies, or unprofessional actions, a CSM/SGM may be removed from the Nomi-

Should the SMA determine that the NCO’s conduct may warrant his or her removal from the eligible list or
the Nominative Program, the following will occur:
a. The Chief, Nominative Sergeants Major Program Office (RA) or Director, OCAR SLDO (USAR
(AGR)) will email a consideration of removal letter to the CSM/SGM. Individuals must acknowledge re-
ceipt of the official notification to the Chief, Nominative Sergeants Major Program Office, or Director,

b. Upon receipt of the letter, the individual will be afforded the opportunity to submit any rebuttal docu-
ments. The rebuttal must be submitted to the Chief, Nominative Sergeants Major Program Office, or Di-
rector, OCAR SLDO within 30 days.
c. The rebuttal will be reviewed and considered by the SMA (RA) or USARC CSM (USAR (AGR)) and
the final decision to remove the NCO from future eligibility will be made by the VCSA (RA) or the CAR
(USAR (AGR)). Upon final determination by the VCSA/CAR, the individual will be notified of the decision
in writing.
d. NCOs under investigation for any reason must immediately notify the Chief, Nominative Sergeants
Major Program Office, or Director, OCAR SLDO through their command, ACOM, or ASCC CSM.

7–16.  Command sergeants major/key billet sergeants major (Regular Army and U.S. Army
Reserve (Active Guard Reserve)) overview
a.  The CSM is the senior NCO in the HQ of units, commands, or organizations at battalion or higher
level or TDA units in which a commander had the authority to impose disciplinary action under the UCMJ.
A SGM Key Billet is a duty assignment at the SGM rank requiring specific, highly developed skills and ex-
periences that are deemed critical to a unit's mission. Key billet SGMs exercise sound judgment and rec-
ommend actions to the commander or key billet officer. They principally manage re-sources and oversee
processes that operate in a leadership environment. Enlisted Soldiers under the commander’s authority
include those authorized by unit TDA, those in TDA or MTOE of subordinate units, and assigned stu-
dents, trainees, and patients. The CSM is the senior enlisted trainer and spokesperson that enforces es-
tablished policies and standards for enlisted Soldiers pertaining to performance, care, conduct, appear-
ance, effective personnel utilization and management, and training; and ensures that subordinate NCOs
do the same.
b.  Requirement of physical readiness. In order to ensure leaders are fully prepared to perform the full
range of their responsibilities, all NCOs selected and slated to serve as CSM are required to meet the
physical demands and deployment requirements associated with the CSM position in which they serve.
Soldiers selected for assignment as a CSM, regardless of age, must have a Cardiovascular Screening
Program (CVSP) screening (the medical procedures for the CVSP are prescribed in AR 40–502), meet
body composition standards set forth in AR 600–9, and pass all events of the physical fitness test of rec-
ord, in compliance with any current profile, before assuming assignment to a CSM position. A CVSP
clearance granted during the Soldier’s last periodic health assessment is sufficient to meet the CVSP re-
quirement.
c.  The Army uses PDPCs to identify Soldiers who are or have served as brigade and battalion CSM or
Key Billet SGMs.
d.  The Army selects and appoints NCOs to serve as a CSM by utilizing the OML generated by an an-
nual CSM/SGM Evaluation Board process as provided for in this regulation and corresponding Memoran-
dum of Instruction, approved by the DCS, G–1, DMPM.
e.  In support of the U.S. Army Special Bands and The Judge Advocate General, Soldiers possessing
military occupational specialty 42S and 27D will be selected for appointment to specific 42S and 27D
CSM positions by means of CSM advisory appointment panels.
f.  Tour lengths are set in the board announcement message; however, tour lengths may be adjusted
based on unit operational readiness needs. Tour lengths are established by CG, HRC (in accordance with
SMA guidance for RA), or the Director, OCAR SLDO (in accordance with USARC CSM guidance) for
USAR (AGR). Incumbent CSM designees will move or relinquish their position, at a prescribed time,
which is designated as the PCORD. All CSMs will have a PCORD. The PCORD will be the determining
factor in designating future CSM requirements. All CSM selectees are expected to serve the full duration
of their prescribed tour.
(1)  The minimum CSM tour length to receive credit for a CSM tour is generally completion of one-half
of the normal tour.
(2)  Current serving CSM electing to retire will do so no later than the first day of the fourth month fol-
lowing their current PCORD unless selected for another assignment.
(3)  Soldiers who are deployed will retire no later than the first day of the fourth month following re-de-
ployment or first day of the fourth month following PCORD whichever is later, unless selected for an-other
assignment. Extensions are not authorized.

7–18.  Brigade command sergeants major and key billet Sergeant Major (Regular Army and U.S.

Selection for Brigade CSM and key billet SGM positions is one of the most important decisions the Army
makes. Leaders at this level are critical to the accomplishment of the Army’s mission for a variety of rea-
sons: they play a crucial role in the retention of our finest Soldiers and Civilians, ensure the mission suc-
cess of their units, and comprise the primary source of future Nominative SGMs and strategic leaders.
Therefore, the Army must select adaptive and innovative leaders who can both lead the Army enterprise
and generate results within Joint and Interagency environments.
a.  Selection to fill Brigade CSM and key billet SGM critical assignments occurs in three phases:
(1)  HQDA CSM/SGM Evaluation Board determines an input board score and generates an invitation
list for the SMAP, which assesses the enlisted population of the CAP. The invitation list is published by
the Chief, Command Management Branch, Sergeants Major Management Division, HRC.
(2)  The SMAP objectively assesses and identifies candidates who are “Certified” for Brigade CSM and
key billet SGM and “Not Yet Certified” for Brigade CSM and key billet SGM positions. The CAP then pro-

(3)  The SMA reviews the OML results, validates the OML, and selects the leaders who have merited
Brigade CSM and key billet SGM positions and who will be slated into those positions.
b.  The Secretary of the Army, in consultation with the CSA and SMA, through the CG, HRC, convenes
SMAP as an additional step to the brigade CSM and key billet SGM selection and slating process. The
HRC Assessments Director is the Executive Director for CAP. The purpose of SMAP is to expand the
Army’s understanding of each leader’s talents, assess whether they are certified for Brigade CSM and
key billet SGM leadership, and identify potential to lead at more senior levels. The SMAP encompasses
cognitive, non-cognitive, psychometric, future potential, strategic potential, physical fitness, written com-
munication, verbal communication, and peer and subordinate assessments, and culminates in the Army
Comprehensive Talent Interview (ACTI) with senior Army leaders. The Army uses the SMAP results to
inform principal Brigade CSM and key billet SGM selection and removal of candidates from Brigade CSM
and key billet SGM consideration.
c.  SMAP ACTI panels are composed of members that represent the varied career experience of the
Army population and are coordinated through the Colonels Officer Management Office, Sergeant Majors
Management Division, and other Army entities as required.
d.  SMAP candidates on permanent profile will take the ACFT at SMAP within the limitations of their
permanent profile. SMAP candidates on temporary profile will not take the ACFT while at SMAP.
e.  SMAP deferments. Candidates invited to attend SMAP must attend unless the candidate submits an
opt-out request or requests a deferment. The SMA is the approval authority for SMAP deferments. Candi-

7–25.  Command sergeants major/nominative sergeants major (U.S. Army Reserve (Troop
Program Unit)) objectives
The USAR TPU CSM Program is designed to improve the effectiveness of Soldiers occupying the most
responsible enlisted positions in the USAR. The objective of the program is to appoint the best-qualified
Soldiers possessing the highest qualities of leadership, integrity, dedication, and professionalism to fill
CSM positions. The objectives of the management program are to—
a.  Ensure the efficient use of CSMs to enhance USAR readiness and mission accomplishment.
b.  Provide USAR commands with CSMs whose experience and training have been expanded by pro-
gressive assignments.
c.  Improve the effectiveness of CSMs by providing a diversity of assignments.
d.  Provide CSMs assignments with greater responsibility at progressively higher levels.
e.  Avoid stagnation of CSMs in assignments that will inhibit or preclude the normal career progression
of other senior NCOs.

Section VII
The U.S. Army Reserve (Troop Program Unit, Individual Mobilization Augmentee, and Individual
Ready Reserve) Sergeants Major Program
7–34.  U.S. Army Reserve (Troop Program Unit, Individual Mobilization Augmentee, and Individual
Ready Reserve) sergeants major overview
USAR (TPU, IMA, and IRR) Soldiers who are selected for attendance to the USASMA for the purpose of
potential promotion must be managed efficiently and effectively. Individuals should rotate between opera-
tional and institutional assignments based on the availability of positions for which SGMs qualify. Doing
so will produce capable, well-rounded leaders that are able to leverage their knowledge, skills, and abili-
ties to accomplish assigned missions and better the USAR.
7–35.  U.S. Army Reserve (Troop Program Unit, Individual Mobilization Augmentee, and Individual
Ready Reserve) sergeants major assignment and utilization
a.  USAR (TPU) SGMs.
(1)  SGMs will be assigned to TOE, MTOE, or TDA positions based on unit authorizations.
(2)  Soldiers must be able to obtain and maintain the minimum-security clearance for the position
against which they are assigned and utilized.
(3)  SGMs will only be assigned to SGM positions. They may not be carried over-strength or assigned
to a special category code in the HR system of record (except when otherwise specified in the regulation).
Any such assignment may result in involuntary reassignment to another SGM position with-in commuting
distance or the IRR.
(4)  SGMs who fail to elect voluntary assignment upon completion of tenure or approved extension are
subject to involuntary reassignment to another SGM position within commuting distance or the IRR. They
may also elect to transfer to the Retired Reserve. Transfer to another SGM position in the same parent
UIC in which the SGM just completed tenure is not allowed.
(5)  SGMs will serve no more than 3 years in an authorized SGM position from the date of assignment
to position. Exceptions to policy for tenure may be submitted through the Director, OCAR SLDO to
USARC CSM no earlier than 1 year, but no later than 6 months before end of tenure.
(6)  SGMs who lose their position due to unit inactivation, reorganization, or relocation may be reas-
signed over-strength for no more than 1 year from the date of the unit inactivation, reorganization, or relo-
cation. Thereafter, they will be reassigned (involuntarily or voluntarily) to either a valid SGM position for
which they qualify or the IRR unless they voluntarily elect assignment to the Retired Reserve (if eligible).
(7)  If a tour of duty is available, a CSM may request a tour of ADT, ADOS, or ADOS–RC. The USARC
CSM is the approval authority for such requests. These tours are described in AR 135–200 and AR 135–
210.
b.  USAR (IMA) SGMs.
(1)  SGMs will be assigned to a Mobilization TDA position based on unit authorizations.
(2)  Soldiers must be able to obtain and maintain the minimum-security clearance for the position
against which they are assigned and utilized.
(3)  SGMs who fail to elect voluntary assignment upon completion of tenure or approved extension are
subject to involuntary reassignment to the IRR. They may also elect transfer to a USAR (TPU) position or
to the Retired Reserve. Transfer to another SGM position in the same UIC in which the SGM just com-
pleted tenure is not allowed.
(4)  SGMs will serve no more than 4 years in an authorized SGM position from the date of assignment
to position as prescribed in AR 140–145. Exceptions to policy for tenure may be submitted through the
Director, OCAR SLDO (DAAR–SL) no earlier than 1 year but no later than 6 months before end of tenure.
(5)  SGMs who lose their position due to unit inactivation, reorganization, or relocation may be reas-
signed over-strength for no more than 1 year from the unit inactivation, reorganization, or relocation date.
Thereafter, they will be reassigned (involuntarily or voluntarily) to either a valid SGM position for which
they qualify or the IRR unless they voluntarily elect assignment to the Retired Reserve (if eligible).
c.  USAR (IRR) SGMs.
(1)  SGMs assigned to the IRR may take part in Inactive Duty Training in a non-pay training status (for
retirements point credit only) per AR 140–185 when authorized by HRC upon receipt of an attachment
order to a military unit.
