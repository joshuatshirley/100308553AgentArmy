Chapter 10
Special Operations Forces Assignments
10–1.  Army Special Operations Forces Scope and objectives
a.  ARSOF assignments require Soldiers able to excel in the performance of their duties in highly com-
plex and dynamic environments throughout the full spectrum of modern warfare and peacetime missions.
The nature of SOF missions requires a high state of readiness; therefore, Soldiers will be assigned on a
priority basis. Stringent screening processes exist to ensure that only the highest quality Soldiers are ac-
cepted and assigned. Soldiers who desire to apply for duty or assignment may do so without regard to
current assignment.
b.  ARSOF in-service Recruiters from the Special Operations Recruiting Battalion will work with unit
commanders and Soldiers to schedule any assessment and selection course dates to meet both unit and
ARSOF operational requirements and the Soldier's career goals.
c.  The objective of SF is to conduct special operations for strategic and operational objectives, with an
emphasis on unconventional warfare capabilities, in all physical and permissive, uncertain, hostile, or po-
litically sensitive operational environments, with or through indigenous forces. Characterized by a highly
adaptive culture and a versatile organization, Special Forces conduct operations that have operational
and strategic implications. Special Forces Soldiers operate across the competition continuum and range
of military operations, integrating their capabilities with joint, interagency, intergovernmental, and multina-
tional partners, or with force-multiplying operations with indigenous groups and coalition forces as part of
a larger effort. At any given time, SF detachments are conducting these types of operations in dozens of
countries worldwide. Special Forces operations differ from conventional operations in the degree of politi-
cal sensitivity and risk and the focus on indigenous assets, operational techniques, and modes of employ-
ment.
d.  The objective of CA is to engage and leverage the civil component of the operational environment
while enhancing, enabling, or providing governance. CA forces accomplish this through the execution of
the CA core competencies throughout the range of military operations and across the competition contin-
uum. These CA missions are designed to provide and enable commanders with the capabilities to find,
disrupt, and defeat threats within the civil component. Threats in the civil component could be ineffective
government, infrastructure degradation, criminal threats, asymmetric threats, and other factors that lead
to unstable environments. Through CA operations, CA forces enable mission command, in-crease situa-
tional understanding, preserve combat power, and consolidate gains in support of the strategic objective
of establishing a secure and stable operational environment that is consistent with U.S. interests. CA
forces are specifically organized, trained, and resourced to address the civil environment and to integrate
civil knowledge, resources, and considerations into decision-making during activities that span the com-
petition continuum.
e.  The objective of PSYOPS is to affect the behavior and objective reasoning of foreign target audi-
ences in an effort to create conditions favorable to U.S. military and political interests. PSYOPS employ
teams and detachments that perform missions of operational and strategic significance across the opera-
tional continuum. PSYOPS support conventional and special operations forces in an effort to influence
neutral and hostile audiences favorable to friendly advantage. Training for PSYOPS is unique—ranging
from warrior-related shoot, move, communicate, and survive tasks to the intricacies of influence, decep-
tion, psychology, marketing, and multimedia development and distribution.
f.  The objective of USASOC Operational Support and Force Sustainment Soldiers is to provide excep-
tional support to all Special Operations units worldwide in all facets of their core competencies. These
core competencies include both conventional and ARSOF-unique capabilities.

10–2.  Special Forces Initial Accessions Program (18X)
Anyone desiring to volunteer for SF duty may contact the nearest recruiting office. The following are the
requirements for initial accession into SF—
a. Are 18 years of age by ship date to CMF 11 OSUT and have not reached their 32nd birthday prior to
ship date. Maximum age for ARNG SF individual accession volunteer is 36 years of age (waivable).
b. Are qualified for, and volunteer for, airborne training (must have airborne stamp on physical).
c. Citizenship (nonwaivable).
(1) Are a U.S. citizen, or
(2) Are a lawful permanent resident (LPR) enrolled in the Accelerated Naturalization program.
d. Have a minimum GT score of 110 and minimum combat operation (CO) score of 100 (waivable).
e. Are a High School Diploma Graduate; GEDs are waivable on case-by-case basis. Must have high
school diploma or GED prior to ship date.
f. Have no derogatory or adverse information that would preclude the granting of a secret or top-secret
security clearance (provided within the Standard Form 86).
g. Have an approved SF Physical prior to SFAS and the ability to complete a high-altitude low opening
physical during attendance in the special forces qualification course (SFQC). Female Soldiers must have
a negative pregnancy test within 30 days of SFAS report date.
h. Can meet the requirement to complete Hyperbaric Oxygen Chamber certification upon completion of
the high-altitude low opening physical.
i. Individuals will normally enlist in pay grade E–1 unless they meet prerequisites for a higher grade
identified in AR 601–210 and will enlist under Option 3 (Training). Upon in-processing at Fort Bragg the
Soldiers will attend the SF Preparatory and Conditioning course.
j. Those individuals already MOS qualified as 11B and airborne qualified will enlist under option 18
(U.S. Army First Assignment–No Training Enlistment Option) with assignment to Fort Bragg, NC, and
once in-processed will start training with the SF Preparatory and Conditioning course.
k. Complete the Defense Language Aptitude Battery (DLAB) test before the start of the SFQC or pro-
vide a Defense Language Proficiency Test (DLPT) or Oral Proficiency Interview (OPI) with a minimum of
1/1 reading and listening score, given within the last 12 months.
l. Must have a physical profile rating of no less than 111221 and meet additional requirements for eyes
and vision in accordance with AR 40–501 or be willing to undergo refractive eye surgery.
m. All Soldiers must be able to swim 50 meters wearing boots and an Army combat uniform prior to
graduating the SFQC.

e. Once Soldiers meet all criteria, they will be scheduled for SFAS (3 weeks, 3 days) as outlined in the
ATRRS course catalog.

10–6.  Special Forces Disqualifiers
The following disqualifiers apply to all applicants—
a.  Be barred to continued service or be under suspension of favorable personnel action.
b.  Have 30 days or more lost time under 10 USC 972, within the current or preceding enlistment. Appli-
cants with any loss totaling 29 days or less require a waiver from the SF commandant.
c.  A qualifying military or civilian conviction of a crime of domestic violence as defined by AR 600–20
(non-waivable).
d.  An open military or civilian investigation, charge, or indictment for a crime that could result in a quali-
fying conviction of a crime of domestic violence, as defined by AR 600–20 (non-waivable).
e.  Any use of a controlled substance without a valid prescription following the Soldier’s date of initial
entry into military service (waivable by the SF commandant).
f.  A military or civilian charge for driving under the influence or reckless driving within 2 years before
the start of an assessment and selection class (waivable by Deputy Commanding General (DCG), U.S.
Army Special Operations CoE).
g.  Any other open military or civilian administrative or criminal investigation, charge, or indictment on
the Soldier (waivable by the DCG, Special Operations CoE).
h.  Punishment under UCMJ within 2 years before the start of the SFAS (waivable by DCG, Special Op-
erations CoE).
i.  Any of the above circumstances occurring between the successful completion of SFAS and the be-
ginning of the SFQC are waivable with the exception of domestic violence and the illegal use of a con-
trolled substance. Any circumstance must be waived prior to starting SFAS or the SFQC. Candidates may
apply for the waiver before 2 years from the disciplinary action; however, they may not start SFAS or the
SFQC before the 2-year mark from the disciplinary action.
10–7.  Special Forces Additional requirements
a.  Soldiers must successfully complete the SFAS course before they can attend the SFQC. Soldiers
will attend the course in a TDY and return status and normally attend at any time; however, the following
exceptions apply:
(1)  Soldiers on OCONUS AI must have their career branch chief’s approval to attend but will not be
scheduled for SFQC until completion of their prescribed tour.
(2)  Soldiers on AI to dependent-restricted short tour may attend SFAS if a deferment is not required
but will not be scheduled for SFQC until completion of their prescribed tour.
(3)  Soldiers who volunteer for SFAS prior to receiving AI will be deferred to allow attendance at the
SFAS course. Graduates’ assignment to the SFQC will take precedence over any assignment conflict
provided the Soldier volunteered for SFAS prior to receiving AI.
(4)  Soldiers who volunteer for SFAS prior to receiving deployment orders will be deferred to allow at-
tendance at the SFAS course, provided their unit has not requested and received an exception to policy
for operational needs. Soldiers volunteering for SFAS after receiving deployment orders will deploy with
their unit.
b.  Upon successful completion of the SFAS course, Soldiers will be evaluated for placement in the
most applicable SFQC. Soldiers will be rescheduled for SFQC, as required, through the Special Opera-
tions CoE SFQC coordinator and the Chief, Army Special Operation Forces Branch (ARHC–TAA–SF).
Prerequisites for the SFQC are in the ATRRS course catalog. Soldiers will be scheduled for the next
available SFQC provided—
(1)  A second PCS in the same fiscal year is not required for OCONUS-based Soldiers.
(2)  Soldiers who are CONUS-based have completed at least 12 months TOS prior to PCS to school.
c.  Soldiers must obtain at least an interim secret or top-secret security clearance (based on initiation of
an entrance national agency check) prior to the MOS phase of the SFQC and be able to obtain a final se-
cret or top-secret security clearance prior to graduation.

(2)  Must complete 4-mile run in 36 minutes or less.
(3)  Must complete 4-and 8-mile foot marches at a 15-minute pace or less with a 35lb rucksack.
(4)  Must successfully complete all hands-on and written exams.
(5)  Must pass screening by the regimental Psychologist.
