5–25.  Stabilization policy for Soldiers/Spouses undergoing fertility treatment
a.  Soldiers who have received a justification memo for treatment or a referral to a fertility specialist by
a gynecologic surgeon/obstetrician will be stabilized from PCS for up to 365 days from the date of the first
appointment while undergoing fertility treatment. Both members of a dual Army couple will be stabilized
when currently assigned to the same location. A 365-day stabilization will also be provided for Soldiers
whose spouses are undergoing fertility treatment (including when the spouse is a civilian or a member of
another military Service).
(1)  Soldiers who received a RFO, AI, or PCS orders prior to receiving a fertility treatment justification
memo or referral are not eligible for this stabilization.
(2)  Soldiers may choose to waive their own stabilization or end it early.
b.  Soldiers/spouses who are granted a fertility profile for assisted reproductive technology procedures
are eligible for a stabilization extension of up to 365 additional days (4 assisted reproductive technology
cycles) from the date granted on the fertility profile. Soldiers/Spouses undergoing fertility treatments other
than assisted reproductive technology are not authorized this extension.
c.  Soldiers who receive RFO, AI, or PCS orders while stabilized for fertility treatment qualify for the sta-
bilization extension after receiving a fertility profile for assisted reproductive technology.
d.  Soldiers stationed OCONUS requesting fertility stabilization or extension must also submit a re-
quest for voluntary foreign service tour extension if the stabilization period exceeds their tour end date.
e.  Soldiers may choose to terminate their stabilization early.
f.  Submission of requests.
(1)  Soldiers will submit fertility treatment stabilizations, extensions, and early terminations via PAR or
memorandum, with supporting documents. The submission will be verified and endorsed by the bat-talion
commander and forwarded to the Actions, Policy, and Procedures Branch (usar-my.knox.hrc.mbx.epmd-
psa-branch@army.mil) for RA or Operations and Readiness Division, Reserve Personnel Management
Directorate, for USAR AGR.
(2)  The Chief, Actions, Policy, and Procedures Branch will stabilize Soldiers using AEA Code “G,” with
the appropriate year and month of stabilization termination.
Chapter 6
Career Development Programs and Instructor or Advisor Positions
Section I
Career Development Programs
6–1.  Overview and objectives
a.  A career development program is a system of intensive management of selected MOS or CMF.
These programs are established to ensure that there are enough highly trained and experienced Soldiers
to fill positions that require unique or highly technical skills. To develop Soldiers with the required profi-
ciency, career fields within each program often require—
(1)  Frequent movement from one job to another to gain required experience.
(2)  An above average frequency of advanced training.
(3)  Lengthy or frequent training periods.
b.  Each program provides the opportunity for career progression to the highest enlisted grade within
one or more MOS included in the program. Only the most highly qualified and career-motivated Soldiers
will be accepted into the following career development programs:
(1)  Intelligence.

6–3.  The Explosive Ordnance Disposal Career Program
a.  The EOD Career Program is a volunteer program designed to train Soldiers in locating, identifying,
evaluating, rendering safe, recovering, and disposing of foreign and domestic conventional, nuclear,
chemical, and biological ordnance; improvised explosive devices; weapons of mass destruction, and
large vehicle bombs; as well as conducting Post Blast investigations and intelligence gathering operations
on first seen explosive ordnance items, improvised explosive devices and their fusing or triggering sys-
tems, and other explosive ordnance related items or components. Soldiers serving in the EOD Career
Program also support very important person missions for the U.S. Secret Service, U.S. State Department,
and other Federal agencies. Upon completion of formal training, Soldiers are awarded MOS 89D (EOD
specialist).
b.  NPS Soldiers must meet the training selection standards, prerequisites, and MOS requirements pre-
scribed in DA Pam 611–21 and this regulation for accession into the EOD Program. SPCs may volunteer
for initial EOD training provided they meet the training selection standards prerequisites and MOS stand-
ards prescribed in AR 611–105, DA Pam 611–21, and this regulation. However, CPLs may not volunteer.
SPCs applying for initial EOD training must not be in a promotable status as of the date of the completion
of entry into EOD training.
c.  Soldiers, SFC, and below, who were previously qualified but have not worked in MOS 89D for 36
months or more may volunteer for reentry into the program (MOS 89D). However, Soldiers must meet the
training selection standards, prerequisites, and MOS requirements prescribed in DA Pam 611–21 and this
regulation and must complete all developmental training as directed by the personnel proponent. All Sol-
diers will complete NCOPDS technical track training for MOS 89D for their skill level when reentering the
program if such courses were not completed prior to their departure.
d.  Soldiers are not eligible to apply when they—
(1)  Are assigned (or alerted, or under orders for assignment) to units alerted for OCONUS movement.
(2)  Are under court-martial proceedings, investigation that may result in trial by court-martial, or sus-
pension of favorable personnel actions (see AR 600–8–2).
(3)  Have been relieved from EOD training or duty due to punitive action, denial, or revocation of secu-
rity clearance, or personal request for relief from EOD.
(4)  Have received EB or variable reenlistment bonus for current service obligation.
(5)  Have lost time under 10 USC 972 within 2 years of the date of application.
(6)  Are serving OCONUS. (Soldiers may apply from 10 through 5 months before DEROS.)
(7)  Have had their EOD badge revoked under provisions of AR 611–105.
e.  Soldiers desiring to participate in the EOD Career Program should contact the EOD unit closest to
their current duty station. The EOD unit commander or 1SG will provide further guidance on current ap-

(1)  To train eligible Soldiers in the following courses:

(b)  Sergeant major (resident and nonresident).
(c)  Battle staff.
(2)  To provide common core training for ALC, SLC, and MLC.
(3)  Proponency for Basic Leader Course (BLC) and enlisted professional development for the Army.
(4)  Proponency for MLC.
e.  BOLC exposes precommissioned candidates, newly commissioned officers, and warrant officers to
seasoned Soldiers with the broadest Army experience possible. NCO cadre influence and impact the
near, mid, and long-term professionalism and competence of the Army based on the solid foundation built
during BOLC. NCO cadre support all BOLC phases.
(1)  BOLC Phase I encompasses training those officers during their pre-commissioning experience.
This training is generally associated with the respective commissioning source: (ROTC, United States Mil-
itary Academy, Officer Candidate School, and Warrant Officer Candidate School).
(2)  BOLC Phase II is a 6-week course that trains, educates, and acculturates newly commissioned of-
ficers and warrant officers. BOLC Phase II develops competent, adaptable officers able to lead Sol-diers
in any environment.
(3)  BOLC objectives are to—
(a)  Develop competent, confident, and adaptive warrior leaders by training precommissioned, newly
commissioned officers, and warrant officers in warrior tasks, battle drills, and on how to perform in leader-
ship positions.
(b)  To develop by end state, an officer who demonstrates the characteristics of an Army leader, lives
the Army values, embodies the Army culture, and who is ready to lead small unit tactics upon arrival at
their first duty assignment.
f.  The objective of the USACC’s instructors and staff is to educate, train, and to commission leaders.
g.  Refer to AR 600–78 for suitability screening requirements, appointing authorities, removal authori-
ties, approval authorities, and appeals authorities for all training cadre positions.
6–8.  Selection criteria
The initial selection criteria for instructor duty are as follows:
a.  Be a high school graduate or possess the general educational development (GED) equivalent.
b.  Have no personal habits or character traits that are questionable from a security standpoint, such as
financial irresponsibility, unusual foreign holdings or interest, alcohol, or drug misuse (does not include
Soldiers who are declared rehabilitation successes under the ASAP), gambling, or emotional dysregula-
tion.
c.  Have no diagnosed mental or psychiatric disorders which, in the opinion of competent medical au-
thority, may cause a defect in judgment or reliability with due regard to the transient or continuing effect of
the illness and the medical findings in such case.
d.  Possess a favorable National Agency Check with Local Agency Check and Credit Check (NACLC),
or higher, investigation.
e.  No record of assault.
f.  Not listed as a military sex offender (see table 3–3) and have a favorable National Sex Offender
Registry check.
g.  Possess mature judgment and initiative.
