## Foreword

In 2022 FM 3-0 established multidomain operations as the Army’s operational concept. It expanded the idea
of combined arms to consider all domains, dimensions, types of forces, echelons, and roles on the battlefield.
Army leaders employ every available capability in complementary and reinforcing ways to achieve their
objectives. By doing so, they create dilemmas for our opponents that can be exploited, even if those
opponents enjoy some initial advantages. Along those lines, Army forces fight as formations, with every
echelon from squad through the theater army operating as an interdependent team. As we have observed from
recent conflicts around the world, fighting as a combined arms team is hard. It is even more difficult when
echelons of formations do not understand their roles and responsibilities. Our advantage is that Army doctrine
addresses how to think about challenges, and we trust our leaders to understand the big ideas and execute
operations.
Our approach demands a high degree of warfighting competency and subject matter expertise at every
echelon. It also requires the mental agility and flexibility to rapidly integrate new methods and capabilities
within our operational approach. The mastery required for success comes through commitment to learning
through education, training, and experience. Leaders must read and understand our doctrine, starting with
this manual. They must embrace a mission command approach and an offensive mindset that asks, “How do
I enable operations beyond my echelon or function? What do I need from other units or functions to perform
my mission?”
This manual will help guide continued modernization, professional military education, and training. I
encourage you to make FM 3-0 a core part of your self-study and leader-development plans. Make its content
part of your professional discourse. Read it generously and critically, and then discuss how to best employ
it. Support the Chief of Staff of the Army’s Harding Project by writing and encouraging others to write about
doctrine and how to improve it. In everything you do, make yourself, your unit, and our Army better.
This we’ll defend!
MILFORD H. BEAGLE, JR.
LIEUTENANT GENERAL, UNITED STATES ARMY
